Redwood Research

Nonprofit that develops and tests AI control, ways to limit harm even if an AI model is trying to subvert oversight, and advises governments and AI companies on misalignment risk.

Works on

  • Independent safety advice for AI companies Building the field
    Give AI companies' safety and governance teams independent expert advice on demand, and publish playbooks of good safety practice that any company can adopt.
  • Setting AI corrigibility What AI should value
    Work out where to set the AI's disposition between obedience and autonomy, and why.
  • Corrigibility as a formal target Theory
    Design systems that reliably accept correction and shutdown.
  • Mesa-optimisation (inner vs outer objective) Theory
    Distinguish the goal a training process rewards from the goal the trained system actually pursues.
  • Unlearning hazardous knowledge Data and training
    Remove dangerous knowledge, such as bioweapon or cyberattack know-how, from a trained model while keeping its general skills.
  • Eliciting latent knowledge (ELK) Looking inside
    Design objectives that make a model honestly report what it internally believes.
  • Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations Testing before release
    Test whether a model hides capability or behaves differently when it knows it is being tested.
  • Model organisms of misalignment Testing before release
    Build deliberately misaligned models in the lab to study how deception arises and test whether safety methods catch it.
  • Multi-agent evaluations and collusion detection Testing before release
    Test groups of interacting AI agents for collusion, hidden signalling and miscoordination, and build monitors that catch it.
  • Safety cases Testing before release
    A structured, auditable argument that a system is acceptably safe, borrowed from nuclear and aviation.
  • AI control Releasing and running
    Design deployment so a misaligned model still cannot cause harm.
  • Anomaly detection Releasing and running
    Flag statistically unusual behaviour as a proxy for an unknown hazard.
  • Independent AI incident investigation (NTSB-style) AI companies
    Outside investigators review real AI incidents after the fact and publish lessons, as air-crash boards do.
  • Safety-motivated frontier labs AI companies
    A safety-focused lab builds frontier AI itself, so it can do safety research on the most capable systems and keep influence.
  • Deals and negotiation with AI systems AI welfare
    Make agreements with AI systems so that cooperating pays them better than defecting.
