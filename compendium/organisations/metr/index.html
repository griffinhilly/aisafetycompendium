METR

Nonprofit that tests frontier AI agents for dangerous autonomous capabilities and loss-of-control risk, publishes evaluation reports, and advises developers and governments on risk assessment.

Works on

  • Forecasting Building the field
    Predict capability timelines as an input to prioritisation.
  • Independent safety advice for AI companies Building the field
    Give AI companies' safety and governance teams independent expert advice on demand, and publish playbooks of good safety practice that any company can adopt.
  • Monitoring training runs for reward hacking Data and training
    Watch a model during reinforcement-learning training to catch it cheating the reward before the cheating becomes a learned habit.
  • Chain-of-thought faithfulness and monitoring Looking inside
    Read the model's written reasoning and check it reflects the real decision process.
  • Dangerous-capability evaluations Testing before release
    Test models for bio, cyber, persuasion, autonomy and scheming capabilities before release.
  • Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations Testing before release
    Test whether a model hides capability or behaves differently when it knows it is being tested.
  • Science of evaluations and capability elicitation Testing before release
    Make AI tests trustworthy: draw out a model's full ability, measure the right thing, and report statistical uncertainty.
  • Safety cases Testing before release
    A structured, auditable argument that a system is acceptably safe, borrowed from nuclear and aviation.
  • Behavioural and activation monitoring Releasing and running
    Watch outputs or internal activations in live deployment for hazard signals.
  • Studying real-world AI use at scale Releasing and running
    Read anonymised real conversations in aggregate to see what the system is actually doing to people.
  • Independent third-party evaluation and audit AI companies
    Outside parties test and verify a lab's safety and capability claims.
  • Frontier-lab internal policy and safety culture AI companies
    Shape the labs' own risk management, red lines and incident reporting.
  • Independent AI incident investigation (NTSB-style) AI companies
    Outside investigators review real AI incidents after the fact and publish lessons, as air-crash boards do.

People linked to it

  • Ajeya Cotra: AI-safety researcher known for the "Biological Anchors" report on AI timelines and for widely-cited writing on AI takeover risk; now on technical staff at METR after years directing AI-safety grantmaking at Open Philanthropy.
  • Paul Christiano: Alignment researcher; first author of the 2017 RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback — training a model using human ratings of its outputs as the reward signal) paper, and one of the most-cited technical thinkers in AI alignment.
