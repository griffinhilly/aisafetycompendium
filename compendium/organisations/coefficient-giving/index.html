Coefficient Giving

Philanthropic funder (formerly Open Philanthropy) with a Navigating Transformative AI fund that issues open requests for proposals on technical AI safety, AI governance and capability evaluations.

Visit websiteOpen in the compendium

Works on

  • Taking on unowned problems and agendas Building the field
    Take on safety problems and research agendas that no team owns, for example by founding a new organisation or lab.
  • Recruiting senior talent from other fields Building the field
    Bring experienced people from law, security, hardware, policy and entrepreneurship into senior and founding roles in AI safety.
  • Forecasting Building the field
    Predict capability timelines as an input to prioritisation.
  • Grantmaking and regranting Building the field
    Move money to safety work quickly through grantmakers and regrantors who know the field well enough to spot good projects.
  • Independent safety advice for AI companies Building the field
    Give AI companies' safety and governance teams independent expert advice on demand, and publish playbooks of good safety practice that any company can adopt.
  • Public communication and content creation Building the field
    Explain AI risk to wide audiences through video, podcasts, books, social media and journalism.
  • Career advice and job matching Building the field
    Give people free career research, one-on-one advice and curated job listings that steer them into AI safety roles.
  • Research training and upskilling programmes Building the field
    Run courses, bootcamps and mentored fellowships that train newcomers for AI safety research, engineering and policy work.
  • Hubs and forums for safety researchers Building the field
    Run the offices, campuses, conferences and online forums where safety researchers work side by side, meet and publish.
  • Agent foundations Theory
    Formal theory of what an agent, a goal and rational choice even are.
  • Automated alignment research Data and training
    Use AI systems to do alignment research, so safety work can keep pace with growing capabilities.
  • Non-agentic and bounded AI designs Data and training
    Build a different kind of AI that is safe by design, such as a predictor with no goals of its own, instead of aligning goal-pursuing agents.
  • Mechanistic interpretability Looking inside
    Reverse-engineer the network's internals into human-readable circuits and algorithms.
  • Chain-of-thought faithfulness and monitoring Looking inside
    Read the model's written reasoning and check it reflects the real decision process.
  • Dangerous-capability evaluations Testing before release
    Test models for bio, cyber, persuasion, autonomy and scheming capabilities before release.
  • Model-character evaluation against a written spec Testing before release
    Measure how closely a model's behaviour matches its developer's own specification.
  • Adversarial robustness and red-teaming Testing before release
    Attack the system deliberately to find and close jailbreaks.
  • Model organisms of misalignment Testing before release
    Build deliberately misaligned models in the lab to study how deception arises and test whether safety methods catch it.
  • Low-probability estimation Testing before release
    Estimate how likely a model is to produce a rare catastrophic output, without having to find an input that triggers it.
  • Real-world trials of AI agents Testing before release
    Run AI agents with real money, tools and people in open-ended settings, and record how they behave and fail.
  • AI control Releasing and running
    Design deployment so a misaligned model still cannot cause harm.
  • Behavioural and activation monitoring Releasing and running
    Watch outputs or internal activations in live deployment for hazard signals.
  • Agreement verification technology Global coordination
    Build the technology and protocols that let governments, or AI developers, verify that an AI agreement is being kept.
  • Differential technology development Society and power
    Delay risky innovation and accelerate safer substitutes, adjusting relative timing.
  • Concentration-of-power and lock-in research Society and power
    Study how AI lets a small group entrench control that cannot be contested or reversed.
  • Improved epistemics and decision quality Society and power
    Strengthen forecasting and institutional decision-making as a general safety lever.

People linked to it

  • Ajeya Cotra: AI-safety researcher known for the "Biological Anchors" report on AI timelines and for widely-cited writing on AI takeover risk; now on technical staff at METR after years directing AI-safety grantmaking at Open Philanthropy.
  • Carl Shulman: Independent AI-forecasting researcher, an influential-but-low-profile figure in the AI safety and effective altruism (EA: the movement that uses evidence and reasoning to find the most good per dollar or career hour) worlds, who since 2024 has also directed research at an AI-themed hedge fund.
  • Holden Karnofsky: Co-founder of GiveWell and Open Philanthropy; now a safety-policy staffer at Anthropic.
  • Joe Carlsmith: Philosopher who writes on AI existential risk; left Open Philanthropy in 2025 to join Anthropic, where he now helps write the document that defines Claude's values and character.
  • Nick Beckstead: A philosopher-turned-grantmaker who wrote an early, widely cited statement of the longtermist argument (the view that positively shaping the far future is a key moral priority), then ran the FTX-funded Future Fund, which collapsed with FTX in 2022.
