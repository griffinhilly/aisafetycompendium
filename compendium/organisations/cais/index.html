Center for AI Safety

San Francisco nonprofit that does technical and conceptual AI safety research, builds the research field, and advocates for safety standards.

Visit websiteOpen in the compendium

Get involved or get in touch

Works on

  • Philosophical analysis of safety concepts Building the field
    Use academic philosophy's literature and methods to test and sharpen the concepts AI safety relies on, such as corrigibility, impact, honesty and rational agency.
  • Public communication and content creation Building the field
    Explain AI risk to wide audiences through video, podcasts, books, social media and journalism.
  • Research training and upskilling programmes Building the field
    Run courses, bootcamps and mentored fellowships that train newcomers for AI safety research, engineering and policy work.
  • Empirical values research as input to normative debate What AI should value
    Study what values models actually express and what people actually want, to inform the "align to what" argument.
  • Unlearning hazardous knowledge Data and training
    Remove dangerous knowledge, such as bioweapon or cyberattack know-how, from a trained model while keeping its general skills.
  • Circuit breakers and representation rerouting Data and training
    Train the model so the internal patterns that lead to harmful output get redirected, which cuts the output off. The authors say this works even against attacks it was not trained on.
  • Tamper-resistant safeguards for open-weight models Data and training
    Build safeguards into released model weights so they survive attempts to strip them out by further fine-tuning.
  • Lie detection and honesty training Looking inside
    Build detectors that catch a model stating what it believes false, and train models to be honest rather than merely plausible.
  • Dangerous-capability evaluations Testing before release
    Test models for bio, cyber, persuasion, autonomy and scheming capabilities before release.
  • Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking Testing before release
    Score models on many value dimensions at once and compare their profiles.
  • Adversarial robustness and red-teaming Testing before release
    Attack the system deliberately to find and close jailbreaks.
  • Science of evaluations and capability elicitation Testing before release
    Make AI tests trustworthy: draw out a model's full ability, measure the right thing, and report statistical uncertainty.
  • Anomaly detection Releasing and running
    Flag statistically unusual behaviour as a proxy for an unknown hazard.
  • Mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM) Global coordination
    Deter any state from seeking AI dominance through the threat that rivals would sabotage its AI project.
  • Differential technology development Society and power
    Delay risky innovation and accelerate safer substitutes, adjusting relative timing.
