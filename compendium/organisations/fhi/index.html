Future of Humanity Institute

Oxford research group founded in 2005 by Nick Bostrom that studied big-picture questions about civilisation and helped develop AI alignment and AI governance as fields; closed in 2024.

Visit websiteOpen in the compendium

Works on

  • Forecasting Building the field
    Predict capability timelines as an input to prioritisation.
  • Moral uncertainty procedures What AI should value
    Combine several ethical theories' verdicts into one decision rather than picking a theory.
  • Non-agentic and bounded AI designs Data and training
    Build a different kind of AI that is safe by design, such as a predictor with no goals of its own, instead of aligning goal-pursuing agents.
  • Differential technology development Society and power
    Delay risky innovation and accelerate safer substitutes, adjusting relative timing.
  • Governing creation and copying of digital minds AI welfare
    Make rules on how many digital minds are created and copied, so numbers cannot swamp society or produce bad lives.

People linked to it

  • Carl Shulman: Independent AI-forecasting researcher, an influential-but-low-profile figure in the AI safety and effective altruism (EA: the movement that uses evidence and reasoning to find the most good per dollar or career hour) worlds, who since 2024 has also directed research at an AI-themed hedge fund.
  • Nick Beckstead: A philosopher-turned-grantmaker who wrote an early, widely cited statement of the longtermist argument (the view that positively shaping the far future is a key moral priority), then ran the FTX-funded Future Fund, which collapsed with FTX in 2022.
  • Nick Bostrom: Philosopher who framed the modern case for AI existential risk (the risk that advanced AI permanently destroys or drastically curtails humanity's future).
  • Robin Hanson: Economist known for prediction markets (markets where people trade on the outcome of future events, turning bets into forecasts) and contrarian futurism.
  • Toby Ord: An Oxford moral philosopher who studies existential risk (the chance that humanity's entire future is destroyed) and, more recently, the economics of frontier AI development.
