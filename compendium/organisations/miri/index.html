Machine Intelligence Research Institute

Nonprofit that argues for an internationally coordinated pause on superintelligent AI development, with a technical governance team on verification and halting; earlier work was on formal alignment theory.

Works on

  • Public communication and content creation Building the field
    Explain AI risk to wide audiences through video, podcasts, books, social media and journalism.
  • Setting AI corrigibility What AI should value
    Work out where to set the AI's disposition between obedience and autonomy, and why.
  • Mild optimisation (satisficing) Theory
    Train systems to do "good enough" instead of maximising, cutting the pull toward extreme strategies.
  • Agent foundations Theory
    Formal theory of what an agent, a goal and rational choice even are.
  • Power-seeking formalism Theory
    Prove that optimal policies tend to acquire power, as a structural risk result.
  • Corrigibility as a formal target Theory
    Design systems that reliably accept correction and shutdown.
  • National AI project AI companies
    Run frontier AI development as a government-led effort, for security against espionage and safety management.
  • Halting frontier AI development Global coordination
    Prepare the legal, technical and institutional means for an internationally coordinated halt to the most dangerous AI development.
  • Agreement verification technology Global coordination
    Build the technology and protocols that let governments, or AI developers, verify that an AI agreement is being kept.
  • Mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM) Global coordination
    Deter any state from seeking AI dominance through the threat that rivals would sabotage its AI project.
  • International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style) Global coordination
    An international body that sets AI standards and checks that states and developers follow them.
  • Using the first aligned AI to stop unsafe AI Society and power
    A limited AI that its builders can control is used for one decisive act, such as stopping hostile superintelligent AI from emerging.

People linked to it

  • Carl Shulman: Independent AI-forecasting researcher, an influential-but-low-profile figure in the AI safety and effective altruism (EA: the movement that uses evidence and reasoning to find the most good per dollar or career hour) worlds, who since 2024 has also directed research at an AI-themed hedge fund.
  • Eliezer Yudkowsky: A self-taught AI researcher and writer who founded LessWrong, the main online hub of the rationalist community, and co-founded the AI-safety research group MIRI (Machine Intelligence Research Institute).
  • Katja Grace: AI forecasting researcher; co-founder and lead researcher of AI Impacts.
  • Nate Soares: President of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), a nonprofit that researches how to make smarter-than-human AI safe.
  • Rob Bensinger: Communications staff member at the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), and editor of the book edition of Eliezer Yudkowsky's "Rationality: From AI to Zombies."
  • Scott Garrabrant: Mathematician and AI-alignment researcher known for logical induction and embedded agency; a former research fellow at the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), now working independently.
