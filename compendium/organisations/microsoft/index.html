Microsoft (AI Red Team and Frontier Governance Framework)

Technology company with an AI Red Team that probes generative AI products for safety and security failures, an open-source red-teaming toolkit (PyRIT), and a published Frontier Governance Framework.

Visit websiteOpen in the compendium

Works on

  • Empowerment (information-theoretic) Theory
    Measure how much future control an agent has, and reward an assistant for increasing the human's control.
  • Unlearning hazardous knowledge Data and training
    Remove dangerous knowledge, such as bioweapon or cyberattack know-how, from a trained model while keeping its general skills.
  • Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking Testing before release
    Score models on many value dimensions at once and compare their profiles.
  • Adversarial robustness and red-teaming Testing before release
    Attack the system deliberately to find and close jailbreaks.
  • Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems) Releasing and running
    Give AI systems unique IDs so their actions can be traced to the system and the party responsible.
  • Frontier-lab internal policy and safety culture AI companies
    Shape the labs' own risk management, red lines and incident reporting.
  • Know-your-customer checks by compute and cloud providers AI companies
    Require cloud and compute providers to verify their customers and report large training runs, making providers checkpoints on frontier development.
  • Autonomy-first product and prototype building People's own judgement
    Fund small builds that strengthen judgement and self-formation rather than convenience.
  • Proof of personhood (personhood credentials) People's own judgement
    Lets a person prove an online account belongs to a real human without revealing who they are.
  • Worker bargaining power over AI at work Society and power
    Give workers and unions a negotiated say over how employers use AI, through contracts and labour agreements with tech firms.
