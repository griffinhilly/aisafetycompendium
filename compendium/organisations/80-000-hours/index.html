80,000 Hours

Nonprofit giving free career research and one-on-one advising, now focused mostly on helping people work on making advanced AI go well; also publishes problem profiles including AI power concentration.

Works on

  • Public communication and content creation Building the field
    Explain AI risk to wide audiences through video, podcasts, books, social media and journalism.
  • Career advice and job matching Building the field
    Give people free career research, one-on-one advice and curated job listings that steer them into AI safety roles.
  • Reducing geographic constraints on AI-safety talent Building the field
    Widen the talent pool by expanding where AI-safety work gets done. People who cannot move to the Bay Area, London or Washington DC can then contribute through remote and part-time programmes, paid entry roles and local hubs.
  • Gradual disempowerment People's own judgement
    Study how humans could lose meaningful control step by step, with no single catastrophic event. One method simulates many small choices to rely on AI.
  • Space resource governance Global coordination
    Agree rules now against exclusive grabs of offworld resources, before advanced AI makes them reachable.
  • Concentration-of-power and lock-in research Society and power
    Study how AI lets a small group entrench control that cannot be contested or reversed.
  • Improved epistemics and decision quality Society and power
    Strengthen forecasting and institutional decision-making as a general safety lever.

People linked to it

  • Toby Ord: An Oxford moral philosopher who studies existential risk (the chance that humanity's entire future is destroyed) and, more recently, the economics of frontier AI development.
  • William MacAskill: An Oxford-trained philosopher and co-founder of the effective altruism (EA) movement — the project of using evidence and reasoning to do the most good possible.
