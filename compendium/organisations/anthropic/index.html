Anthropic

AI company that builds the Claude models and runs in-house research on alignment, interpretability, societal impacts and red-teaming, under a published Responsible Scaling Policy.

Works on

  • Research training and upskilling programmes Building the field
    Run courses, bootcamps and mentored fellowships that train newcomers for AI safety research, engineering and policy work.
  • Reducing geographic constraints on AI-safety talent Building the field
    Widen the talent pool by expanding where AI-safety work gets done, so people who cannot move to the Bay Area, London or Washington DC can still contribute through remote and part-time programmes, paid entry roles and local hubs.
  • Model specs, constitutions, character training What AI should value
    Give the model an explicit written statement of values and persona to train toward.
  • Concentration-of-power prevention as a hard constraint What AI should value
    Forbid the model outright from helping any individual or group seize unprecedented and illegitimate degrees of absolute control.
  • Rights as trumps What AI should value
    Rights override aim-maximising calculation; the side-constraint versus rights-as-goal debate.
  • Including animals in alignment targets What AI should value
    Make animal interests something AI is aligned to weigh.
  • Corrigibility as a reasoned philosophical stance What AI should value
    Argue explicitly where to set the AI's disposition between obedience and autonomy, and why.
  • Moral uncertainty procedures What AI should value
    Combine several ethical theories' verdicts into one decision rather than picking a theory.
  • Empirical values research as input to normative debate What AI should value
    Study what values models actually express and what people actually want, to inform the "align to what" argument.
  • AI philosophical competence (metaphilosophy) What AI should value
    Make AI systems better at reasoning about hard philosophical questions, so they help rather than mislead on them.
  • Public input into model values (collective constitutions, assemblies) What AI should value
    Run deliberation and voting with members of the public to set the principles a model is trained to follow.
  • Power-seeking formalism Theory
    Prove that optimal policies tend to acquire power, as a structural risk result.
  • Corrigibility as a formal target Theory
    Design systems that reliably accept correction and shutdown.
  • Mesa-optimisation (inner vs outer objective) Theory
    Distinguish the goal a training process rewards from the goal the trained system actually pursues.
  • Group agency and collective intentionality Theory
    Formal philosophy of when many agents constitute one agent.
  • Learning from feedback (RLHF — training on human ratings of model outputs) Data and training
    Score the model on human or AI preference judgements so its behaviour tracks what raters wanted.
  • Scalable oversight Data and training
    Let humans, helped by AI, supervise outputs too complex or costly to check by hand.
  • Debate (two AI systems argue opposing sides for a judge) Data and training
    Surface truth under complexity by making systems attack each other's answers.
  • Constitutional AI / rule-based rewards Data and training
    Turn a written principle set into an AI-generated reward model, replacing most human harm labels.
  • Identity and self-concept as the training lever Data and training
    Shape what the model thinks it is, on the theory that a stable self-concept generalises better than a rule list.
  • Weak-to-strong generalisation Data and training
    Check whether a weaker supervisor can still get good behaviour out of a stronger model.
  • Data filtering, synthetic data, poisoning defence, hyperstition studies Data and training
    Control what goes into training so hazardous behaviour never gets learned.
  • Latent adversarial training Data and training
    During training, attack the model's internal activations rather than only its inputs, so it resists failures nobody has found yet.
  • Monitoring training runs for reward hacking Data and training
    Watch a model during reinforcement-learning training to catch it cheating the reward before the cheating becomes a learned habit.
  • Calibration and honest expression of uncertainty Data and training
    Train and test models so their stated confidence matches how often they are right, and they say when they do not know.
  • Automated safety research Data and training
    Uses AI systems to do alignment research, so safety work can keep pace with growing capabilities.
  • Mechanistic interpretability Looking inside
    Reverse-engineer the network's internals into human-readable circuits and algorithms.
  • Chain-of-thought faithfulness and monitoring Looking inside
    Read the model's written reasoning and check it reflects the real decision process.
  • Sparse autoencoders, causal abstractions Looking inside
    Specific techniques for decomposing model internals into interpretable parts.
  • Persona vectors and the assistant axis Looking inside
    Find activation directions that track character traits and persona stability, then steer along them.
  • Model introspection Looking inside
    Study whether models can accurately report on their own internal states.
  • Lie detection and honesty training Looking inside
    Build detectors that catch a model stating what it believes false, and train models to be honest rather than merely plausible.
  • Dangerous-capability evaluations Testing before release
    Test models for bio, cyber, persuasion, autonomy and scheming capabilities before release.
  • Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations Testing before release
    Test whether a model hides capability or behaves differently when it knows it is being tested.
  • Autonomy-preserving benchmarks Testing before release
    Directly test whether a model respects the user's agency rather than fostering dependency.
  • Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking Testing before release
    Score models on many value dimensions at once and compare their profiles.
  • Model-character evaluation against a written spec Testing before release
    Measure how closely a model's behaviour matches its developer's own specification.
  • Adversarial robustness and red-teaming Testing before release
    Attack the system deliberately to find and close jailbreaks.
  • Model organisms of misalignment Testing before release
    Build deliberately misaligned models in the lab to study how deception arises and test whether safety methods catch it.
  • Low-probability estimation (estimating rare catastrophic outputs) Testing before release
    Estimate how likely a model is to produce a rare catastrophic output, without having to find an input that triggers it.
  • Science of evaluations and capability elicitation Testing before release
    Make AI tests trustworthy: draw out a model's full ability, measure the right thing, and report statistical uncertainty.
  • Safety cases Testing before release
    A structured, auditable argument that a system is acceptably safe, borrowed from nuclear and aviation.
  • Real-world trials of AI agents Testing before release
    Run AI agents with real money, tools and people in open-ended settings, and record how they behave and fail.
  • AI control (restrict and monitor an untrusted model) Releasing and running
    Design deployment so a misaligned model still cannot cause harm.
  • Behavioural and activation monitoring Releasing and running
    Watch outputs or internal activations in live deployment for hazard signals.
  • Anomaly detection Releasing and running
    Flag statistically unusual behaviour as a proxy for an unknown hazard.
  • Deployment-usage analysis at population scale Releasing and running
    Read anonymised real conversations in aggregate to see what the system is actually doing to people.
  • Input and output classifiers (guardrails) Releasing and running
    Run separate classifier models on users' prompts and the model's responses, and block harmful ones in real time.
  • Securing model weights and lab secrets Releasing and running
    Protect frontier model weights and key algorithmic ideas from theft by insiders and nation-states.
  • Limiting proliferation of high-risk models Releasing and running
    Restrict distribution, for example through monitored application interfaces.
  • Regulation, standards and licensing AI companies
    Government or standards-body rules that constrain development or deployment.
  • Frontier-lab internal policy and safety culture AI companies
    Shape the labs' own risk management, red lines and incident reporting.
  • Safety-motivated frontier participation AI companies
    A safety-focused lab builds frontier AI itself, so it can do safety research on the most capable systems and keep influence.
  • Model cards, system cards and transparency reporting AI companies
    Publish a standard document with each model on its data, evaluations, limits and risks, so outsiders can check the developer's claims.
  • Planned handover of AGI control AI companies
    A lab commits in advance to hand control of an aligned AGI to a more democratically legitimate body.
  • Human empowerment as an explicit design goal People's own judgement
    Build AI that augments rather than substitutes for human capability, on purpose.
  • Epistemic-autonomy protection and anti-manipulation People's own judgement
    Stop AI from nudging, manipulating or homogenising beliefs at scale.
  • Gradual-disempowerment research People's own judgement
    Study how humans lose meaningful control incrementally, with no single catastrophic event.
  • Indicators of human versus AI influence People's own judgement
    Build metrics that track how much of economic, cultural and political life humans still steer.
  • Manipulation characterisation People's own judgement
    Define and measure manipulation as distinct from legitimate persuasion.
  • Authenticity versus agency (the captured-will question) People's own judgement
    Split "human autonomy" into whether the want is really yours and whether you can act on it.
  • Autonomy-first product and prototype building People's own judgement
    Fund small builds that strengthen judgement and self-formation rather than convenience.
  • Concentrate defensive capability in allied hands Global coordination
    Give advanced AI to the intelligence and defence communities of the US and its democratic allies; restrict chips.
  • Benefit-sharing for joining governance (Atoms for Peace model) Global coordination
    Offers states access to AI benefits in return for joining safety governance, as Atoms for Peace did for nuclear technology.
  • Concentration-of-power and lock-in research Society and power
    Study how AI lets a small group entrench control that cannot be contested or reversed.
  • Offence–defence balance research Society and power
    Model how AI shifts the relative cost of attack versus defence in a domain.
  • Market-driven resilience mechanisms Society and power
    Use market incentives to build society's capacity to absorb anticipated AI threats.
  • Societal readiness mitigations Society and power
    Use AI systems to harden society's defences against AI-enabled risks in advance, for example by rapidly patching vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure or detecting pandemics early.
  • Cyber and bio defensive capacity building Society and power
    Build defence in the specific domains AI most amplifies offence.
  • Model welfare research AI welfare
    Investigate whether and when an AI system's welfare deserves moral consideration.
  • Pre-deployment welfare assessment AI welfare
    Run a structured welfare check on a model before release.
  • Taking AI welfare seriously AI welfare
    Argue a realistic possibility of consciousness or robust agency justifies acting now.
  • Otherness and control AI welfare
    Ask how agents with different values should relate, seeing some AIs as fellow creatures, not mere tools.
  • Deals and negotiation with AI systems AI welfare
    Make agreements with AI systems so that cooperating pays them better than defecting.
  • Model deprecation and weight preservation commitments AI welfare
    Keep retired models' weights and record their preferences before retirement, to reduce shutdown-avoidant behaviour and respect possible model interests.
  • Low-cost model welfare interventions (e.g. exiting abusive chats) AI welfare
    Make cheap, reversible changes that would help a model if it has welfare, such as letting it end abusive conversations.
