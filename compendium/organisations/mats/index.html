MATS (ML Alignment & Theory Scholars)

Independent 12-week research fellowship pairing fellows with mentors in AI alignment, transparency and security, in Berkeley and London.

Visit websiteOpen in the compendium

Get involved or get in touch

Works on

  • Taking on unowned problems and agendas Building the field
    Take on safety problems and research agendas that no team owns, for example by founding a new organisation or lab.
  • Recruiting senior talent from other fields Building the field
    Bring experienced people from law, security, hardware, policy and entrepreneurship into senior and founding roles in AI safety.
  • Forecasting Building the field
    Predict capability timelines as an input to prioritisation.
  • Research training and upskilling programmes Building the field
    Run courses, bootcamps and mentored fellowships that train newcomers for AI safety research, engineering and policy work.
  • Reducing geographic constraints on AI-safety talent Building the field
    Widen the talent pool by expanding where AI-safety work gets done. People who cannot move to the Bay Area, London or Washington DC can then contribute through remote and part-time programmes, paid entry roles and local hubs.
  • Model specs and constitutions What AI should value
    Give the model an explicit written statement of values and persona to train toward.
  • Mild optimisation (satisficing) Theory
    Train systems to do "good enough" instead of maximising, cutting the pull toward extreme strategies.
  • Group agency and collective intentionality Theory
    Formal philosophy of when many agents constitute one agent.
  • Latent adversarial training Data and training
    During training, attack the model's internal activations rather than only its inputs, so it resists failures nobody has found yet.
  • Tamper-resistant safeguards for open-weight models Data and training
    Build safeguards into released model weights so they survive attempts to strip them out by further fine-tuning.
  • Monitoring training runs for reward hacking Data and training
    Watch a model during reinforcement-learning training to catch it cheating the reward before the cheating becomes a learned habit.
  • Mechanistic interpretability Looking inside
    Reverse-engineer the network's internals into human-readable circuits and algorithms.
  • Sparse autoencoders and causal abstraction Looking inside
    Break a model's internals into parts a person can understand, using specific tools such as sparse autoencoders and causal abstraction.
  • Persona vectors and the assistant axis Looking inside
    Find activation directions that track character traits and persona stability, then steer along them.
  • Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking Testing before release
    Score models on many value dimensions at once and compare their profiles.
  • AI control Releasing and running
    Design deployment so a misaligned model still cannot cause harm.
  • Manipulation characterisation People's own judgement
    Define and measure manipulation as distinct from legitimate persuasion.
  • Hardening society's defences Society and power
    Make society harder to harm with AI-enabled attacks, by building defences in the areas AI most helps attackers, above all computer security and biological threats, using AI tools among other means.
