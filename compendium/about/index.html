About the AI Safety Compendium

What it is

The AI Safety Compendium maps the ideas proposed for making advanced AI safe. It holds 154 approaches in 12 groups, under 6 bands. Each approach has a full entry: what it is, how it works, where research stands, open problems, criticisms, who works on it and where to start reading.

It also lines up 24 published overviews of the field side by side, in 7 groups by who wrote them. It lists 237 organisations and 45 people who shaped the field. The open problems list gathers 622 open problems from 154 approaches in one place.

How it was made

It began with a scan of published overviews of AI safety by expert panels, governments, labs, funders and researchers. 24 of them make up the chart of sources. A further 199 published sources were scanned for approaches the page was missing. Lab research programmes, funder calls and lists of mentored research projects added more. In all, 227 sources were covered.

The sources were read from October 2026 onward. Each quote is copied word for word from its source. Each full entry was checked claim by claim against the sources it cites. Entries under People were checked against current web sources on 25 October 2026.

Research and checking used AI assistance.

The full method is under Sources and method.

Corrections and additions

Every approach and organisation has a “Suggest a correction” link. It opens an issue on the compendium’s public GitHub page; a free account is needed.

A missing approach, organisation or source can be suggested the same way.
