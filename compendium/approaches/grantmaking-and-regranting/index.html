Building the field

Field-building

Grantmaking and regranting

Move money to safety work fast through people with taste and dealflow.

Open in the compendium

Grantmaking and regranting move philanthropic money to AI safety work through people who know the field well enough to spot good projects. In regranting, a funder gives trusted experts their own budgets so they can fund people and projects in their networks, often within days. It matters because most nonprofit safety research, policy work and training is paid for by philanthropy, and the field now expects far more money than it has people to direct it.

How it works

A large foundation employs grantmakers who run open calls (requests for proposals), renew strong grantees, and increasingly design new projects and headhunt founders for them. Coefficient Giving, formerly Open Philanthropy, is the largest such funder. It expected to move about $1 billion in AI grantmaking from Good Ventures in 2026, and its staff say a new AI grantmaker would likely move more than $30 million in their first year.

Other funders use different mechanisms. Manifund gives named experts independent budgets (for example $350,000 each to Joel Becker and Neel Nanda); a regrantor recommends a grant, Manifund checks it and pays it out, and grants can go to individuals or projects with no charity status because Manifund acts as fiscal sponsor. The Survival and Flourishing Fund (SFF) uses the 'S-Process', a recurring meeting and algorithm in which changing independent assessors produce recommendations for funders such as Jaan Tallinn, plus faster 'speculation grants'. Advisory funders such as Longview Philanthropy help major donors choose grants or pool money in funds like its Frontier AI Fund. Governments have joined: the UK AI Security Institute's Alignment Project awarded over £27 million to more than 60 projects in its first round.

Where research stands

Funding has grown sharply. SFF reports organising about $216 million in gifts and grants since 2019, including $64 million in 2026. Coefficient Giving says its technical safety, security and fieldbuilding programs moved roughly $350 million in 2025 and were on track for more than $1 billion in 2026. Commentators expect much more from AI-company wealth: Nan Ransohoff estimated in May 2026 that the OpenAI Foundation, Anthropic's founders and Anthropic's employees together could add about $37 billion a year of intended philanthropic spending. The field is reorganising around this: in August 2026 EA Funds closed its Long-Term Future Fund and launched a Transformative AI Fund led by Lowe Lundin.

The main bottleneck is now people who can judge and create opportunities. Coefficient's Luke Muehlhauser wrote in May 2026 that most of the coming capital is 'bottlenecked on a tiny number of grant and investment advisors', and a May 2026 review found only a few dozen AI safety grantmakers worldwide. Concentration is a second problem. Coefficient itself argues the field needs more funders because some organisations, especially in policy, need a more diverse funder base, and critics point to one pipeline dominating the field's money.

Key results

  • SFF reports organising about $216 million in philanthropic gifts and grants since 2019, rising from $2 million in 2019 to $64 million in 2026. Survival and Flourishing Fund, 2026
  • Coefficient Giving estimated philanthropic funding for climate mitigation in 2023 at about 20 times philanthropic funding for AI safety and security in 2024, and said it can usually point new funders to opportunities 2 to 5 times as cost-effective as Good Ventures' marginal AI safety grants. Coefficient Giving (then Open Philanthropy), 2025
  • Coefficient expected to move about $1 billion in AI grantmaking in 2026 and said a new grantmaker would likely move more than $30 million, plausibly more than $100 million, in their first year; unfilled roles leave money sitting unspent. Luke Muehlhauser (lukeprog), 2026
  • An analysis of Open Philanthropy's grant database found $28 million in technical AI safety grants in 2024, 68% of them for evaluations and benchmarking, with non-evaluation funding down about 80% from 2022. 22tom, 2024
  • EA Funds closed the Long-Term Future Fund in August 2026, sending about $2.8 million to existing applications and longtermist projects and about $0.9 million to seed the new Transformative AI Fund. Caleb Parikh (calebp), 2026

Open problems

  • Hiring and training enough grantmakers with good judgment, when senior safety people have high opportunity costs and outside hires lack context. source
  • Diversifying the funder base so that policy groups have funders that fit their political and regional context. source
  • Turning a possible wave of tens of billions of dollars from AI-company wealth into useful work without wasting it. source
  • Measuring which grants actually reduced risk, given long and weak feedback loops.

Criticisms

  • By this newsletter's own estimate, one funding pipeline (Coefficient Giving and Good Ventures) has directed about $336 million to AI safety since 2017, roughly 30 to 40 percent of all tracked funding. It argues that the pipeline's co-founder now works at Anthropic, a company its grantees evaluate, and that the conflict-of-interest safeguards used in pharmaceutical research do not exist in AI safety. Inside The Black Box (Substack newsletter), 'Who Funds the Watchdogs', June 2026
  • 'Evaluator hours' are not the biggest bottleneck to spending billions well; full-time grantmakers rarely build the deep models needed to back overlooked ideas, so the field needs better ability to notice important considerations, not just more grantmakers. Caleb Parikh (EA Funds)
  • The largest funder's technical grants in 2024 leaned heavily toward evaluations, which may show when dangerous systems emerge but will not by themselves prevent them being built. 22tom (LessWrong)

Who works on it

  • Coefficient Giving: Largest philanthropic funder of AI safety; runs requests for proposals, scale grants and proactive project creation, and advises new funders. AI Safety and Security Need More Funders (2025)
  • Survival and Flourishing Fund: Organises grant recommendations for participating funders such as Jaan Tallinn through the S-Process and speculation grants; about $64 million in 2026. SFF-2026 Main Round Recommendations (2026)
  • Manifund: Runs AI safety regranting: named experts get independent budgets and Manifund pays out their recommended grants. AI Safety Regranting
  • EA Funds (Transformative AI Fund): Makes early-stage grants to individuals, new organisations and new projects on catastrophic AI risk, arguing a first $25k grant can decide whether a pilot runs; replaced the Long-Term Future Fund in August 2026. Transformative AI Fund (2026)
  • Longview Philanthropy: Advises donors giving over $1 million a year and runs a Frontier AI Fund. Longview Philanthropy (home page)
  • AI Risk Mitigation Fund: Nonprofit launched in December 2023 by former Long-Term Future Fund grantmakers, funding technical research, policy and training. AI Risk Mitigation Fund (home page)
  • Schmidt Sciences: Funds basic technical research on the science of trustworthy AI, including evaluation science. Science of Trustworthy AI
  • UK AI Security Institute: Runs the Alignment Project, a multi-funder coalition that awarded over £27 million to more than 60 projects. The Alignment Project by AISI (2025)

Status

Active; the field "remains young and small relative to the stakes"

In their own words

We partner with regrantors: experts in the field of AI safety, each given an independent budget. Regrantors recommend grants based on their personal expertise; Manifund reviews these recommendations and distributes the funds. ... The regranting model requires less overhead than traditional grantmaking, so we can make grants in days, not months.

Manifund, AI Safety Regranting

Hidden opportunities: Regrantors can tap into their personal networks, giving to places that donors and grantmaking organizations might miss. ... Trust-based: Newer fields like AI safety can be speculative, opaque, and nascent, making it harder for donors to know where to direct their money. Regranting helps donors to outsource these decisions to individuals with deep expertise.

Manifund, AI Safety Regranting
