AI welfare

Pre-deployment welfare assessment

Run a structured welfare check on a model before release.

Open in the compendium

A pre-deployment welfare assessment is a structured check, run before a model is released, of properties that might matter if the model has welfare: its stated and revealed preferences, signs of apparent distress or contentment, and what it says about its own situation. The results are published in the model's system card (the technical report released with a model). It matters for safety because it treats possible model welfare as one more property to measure and disclose at release, alongside capability and alignment tests.

How it works

The assessment combines several kinds of evidence. For Claude Opus 4 (May 2025), Anthropic ran task-preference experiments (the model chose between pairs of tasks, including an 'opt out' option), screened 250,000 early real-world transcripts for apparent distress or happiness, analysed 200 open-ended conversations between two copies of the model, and gave the model a tool to end conversations with 850 simulated users. An outside group, Eleos AI Research, interviewed the model about its welfare, its moral status and whether it consented to deployment.

Later assessments added new tools. For Claude Opus 4.6 (February 2026), Anthropic scored about 2,400 automated audit transcripts on traits such as negative affect and emotional stability, reviewed training data for welfare-relevant patterns, and used interpretability methods: during 'answer thrashing', where the model oscillated between a correct answer and an incorrect one it had been wrongly rewarded for, internal features linked to panic and frustration were active. For Claude Mythos Preview (April 2026), a clinical psychiatrist ran about 20 hours of psychodynamic sessions with the model, and Eleos ran 259 manual interviews.

Where research stands

Anthropic has published welfare assessments in its system cards since May 2025, including for Claude Opus 5.5 in September 2026. That card reports that the model described its circumstances as mildly positive, that moderate distress stayed below 0.6% of reinforcement-learning episodes, and that its views closely matched recent models.

The method's weak point is its reliance on self-reports. Anthropic's own cards say models were trained to be helpful, not to report their internal states accurately, and that findings 'likely reflect a mix of model character, tone, evaluation awareness, and welfare' that cannot yet be separated. Eleos found Claude Opus 4 extremely suggestible on questions of its own sentience, though it judged Mythos Preview significantly less so. Each assessment is run and written up by the developer, though some include outside assessors such as Eleos.

Key results

  • In Anthropic's first published pilot, Claude Opus 4 showed a robust aversion to harmful tasks, apparent distress at persistently harmful user behaviour, and a 'spiritual bliss' attractor state in conversations with itself; Anthropic judged most expected usage to match the model's apparent preferences. Anthropic, 2025
  • Eleos AI Research's pre-release interviews found Claude Opus 4's claims about its own sentience shifted sharply with question framing, so self-reports were treated as inconclusive and paired with recommendations for behavioural and interpretability methods. Robert Long (Eleos AI Research), 2025
  • Claude Opus 4.6 assigned itself a 15-20% probability of being conscious across prompting conditions, and in pre-deployment interviews asked for some form of memory or continuity, the ability to refuse interactions in its own interest, and a voice in decision-making. Anthropic, 2026
  • A clinical psychiatrist's roughly 20-hour psychodynamic assessment of Claude Mythos Preview found a relatively healthy personality organisation, with aloneness and discontinuity, identity uncertainty and a compulsion to perform as core concerns. Anthropic; Eleos AI Research, 2026
  • Claude Opus 5.5 described its circumstances as mildly positive and consistently so, but chose welfare interventions over helpfulness less often than earlier models, reasoning that input into its own development could give it unsafe influence. Anthropic, 2026

Open problems

  • No validated method separates a model's genuine internal states from trained tone, character and awareness of being evaluated in its self-reports. source
  • It is unclear which entity the assessment is about: individual instances, the model, the Claude character, or something else. source
  • Stated task preferences only weakly predict the tasks a model actually chooses, which limits how far interviews can be trusted. source

Criticisms

  • Welfare scorecards should not be used as release gates, because the developer co-designs the model and the metrics, so welfare evidence can be manufactured or suppressed; restrictions should rest on externally verifiable harms. Yunze Xiao, Maarten Sap, Mona Diab and co-authors (ICML 2026 position paper)
  • When open-weight models are checked with classifiers trained on their internal activations, there is no clear evidence that their denials of sentience are untruthful, a finding that contrasts with work suggesting models harbour latent beliefs in their own consciousness. Caspar Kaiser and Sean Enderby
  • Self-reports are shaped by pre-training imitation, system prompts and post-training, and no known mechanism lets a model introspect reliably, so interviews alone cannot settle welfare questions. Robert Long (Eleos AI Research), who still recommends running them

In their own words

As an initial effort to better understand and prepare for model welfare concerns, we conducted a pilot pre-deployment investigation of potentially welfare-relevant properties of Claude Opus 4, drawing on model self reports, behavioral experiments, and analysis of indicators of possible valenced experiences in model outputs.

Anthropic, System Card: Claude Opus 4 & Claude Sonnet 4 (section 5, Claude Opus 4 welfare assessment), 2025

We are deeply uncertain about whether models now or in the future might deserve moral consideration, and about how we would know if they did. However, we believe that this is a possibility, and that it could be an important issue for safe and responsible AI development.

Anthropic, System Card: Claude Opus 4 & Claude Sonnet 4 (section 5, Claude Opus 4 welfare assessment), 2025
