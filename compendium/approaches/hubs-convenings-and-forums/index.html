Building the field

Field-building

Shared offices, hubs, convenings and forums

Run the offices, campuses, conferences and online forums where safety researchers work side by side, meet and publish.

Open in the compendium

Shared offices, hubs, convenings and forums are the places where AI safety people work side by side, meet and publish. They include research centres and co-working spaces, event campuses, recurring conferences and the online forums where much alignment research first appears. They matter because a young field depends on finding collaborators and quick feedback, and these spaces also house many small organisations and training programmes.

How it works

Shared offices give small groups a home. The London Initiative for Safe AI (LISA) runs a hub in central London that hosts organisations such as Apollo Research, BlueDot Impact and the Institute for AI Policy and Strategy, plus training programmes such as ARENA, LASR Labs and Pivotal. It offers workspace, advice and events. Constellation is an independent research centre in Berkeley, California, with visiting fellowships, the Astra fellowship and a residency for founders. FAR.AI runs FAR.Labs, a co-working space in downtown Berkeley with seminars, catered meals and a 50-person event room. The Cambridge AI Safety Hub runs courses, events and part-time research programmes in Cambridge, UK.

Venues and conferences bring wider groups together. Lightcone Infrastructure bought a $16.5 million former hotel in Berkeley, renovated it for about $6 million, and runs it as Lighthaven, a campus for events, fellowships and visiting researchers. FAR.AI runs the Alignment Workshop series, held in cities including San Diego, Singapore, Vienna, London and Seoul (July 2026), and conferences that connect policymakers with technical experts. Iliad runs an annual gathering of over 100 people on mathematical and theoretical approaches to alignment. The Centre for Effective Altruism runs EA Global, selective conferences where attendees apply in advance.

Online forums are the field's informal journals. The LessWrong team launched the AI Alignment Forum in 2018. About 50 to 100 members can post directly; others post on LessWrong, where members can promote their work to the Forum. The Forum offers immediate publication and quality ratings by readers in place of formal peer review. In 2026 Iliad and its parent organisation, Principles of Intelligence, launched the Alignment Journal, a peer-reviewed venue that is experimenting with paid, attributed peer review, to add the quality certification that forums lack.

Where research stands

This infrastructure is established, but its value is measured mostly by users' ratings and the operators' own estimates. By August 2021 several hundred researchers had written about 2,000 posts and 9,000 comments on the Alignment Forum. In November 2024 Lightcone estimated that LessWrong gets about 20 million views a year from about 3 million unique users. Lightcone says that in 2017 learning alignment from public writing was basically not possible, and that by 2020 many people had a workable route in through 20 to 30 hours with the best LessWrong material. Ratings are high: ARENA reports that its 29 participants in early 2026 rated being at LISA 9.9 out of 10, and Lightcone reports a median venue rating of 10 out of 10 at Lighthaven conferences that asked about the venue.

Harder evidence is scarce. Open Philanthropy's 2020 survey of people it selected for their high expected impact on longtermist work found that meetup groups and discussion platforms got 10-20% of the impact respondents credited to named influences, and that items tied to rationalist ideas drew negative responses more often. Venues are costly and need steady donations: in November 2024 Lightcone sought about $3 million for the next 12 months, partly to meet interest payments on its Lighthaven mortgage. The hubs also cluster in a few cities. A July 2026 essay named San Francisco, Berkeley, London, Oxford and Washington, DC as the places where most AI safety talent lives.

Key results

  • The AI Alignment Forum, launched in 2018, had several hundred contributing researchers, about 2,000 posts and 9,000 comments by August 2021; about 50 to 100 members can post without review. Ruby and habryka (AI Alignment Forum team), 2021
  • Lightcone estimates that LessWrong gets about 20 million views a year from about 3 million unique users; it bought the Lighthaven property for $16.5 million and spent about $6 million renovating it. Oliver Habryka (Lightcone Infrastructure), 2024
  • ARENA reports that its 29 participants in January-February 2026 rated the value of being in the LISA environment 9.9 out of 10, its highest recorded score. ARENA (James Hindmarch, Joly Scriven), 2026
  • In Open Philanthropy's 2020 survey, meetup groups and discussion platforms got 10-20% of the impact respondents credited to named influences, and items tied to rationalist ideas drew negative responses more often than others. Eli Rose (Open Philanthropy), 2021
  • A 2023 community survey of 558 LessWrong users found respondents were 75% cisgender male and 79% non-Hispanic White, with almost half in the United States. Wikipedia contributors, 2026

Open problems

  • Giving forum research the quality certification and legitimacy of peer review without losing speed; the Alignment Journal is one attempt. source
  • Measuring whether hubs and events change what gets researched, beyond attendee ratings; Lightcone calls judging Lighthaven by its effects on LessWrong writing 'a quite bad approximation' of its total impact. source
  • Paying for costly venues: in late 2024 Lightcone needed about $3 million in donations for the next year, including interest payments on its Lighthaven mortgage. source
  • Spreading hubs beyond a few cities so that people who cannot relocate can still take part. source

Criticisms

  • Preprint servers and online forums move fast, but generally at the expense of quality certification and institutional legitimacy, and their review coverage can suffer when trends and hype misdirect attention. Alignment Journal founders (Dan MacKinlay and co-authors, March 2026)
  • Hubs concentrate the field in a few cities, so mid-career people who cannot move are pushed away, shrinking the talent pool. Seth Lifland (LessWrong, July 2026)

Status

Established: the AI Alignment Forum has run since 2018; Lightcone estimated LessWrong's traffic at about 20M views a year (Nov 2024); hubs cluster in a few cities such as Berkeley and London; value is measured mostly by attendee ratings.
