Aligned modelsAlso: Evaluating

Empirical values research as input to normative debate

Study what values models actually express and what people actually want, to inform the "align to what" argument.

Open in the compendium

Empirical values research measures two things: what values AI models actually express when people use them, and what people actually want from AI. It turns the philosophical question 'what should AI value?' into data that can be checked. It matters for safety because a developer cannot tell whether training worked, or whose values a model reflects, without measuring the model's real behaviour.

How it works

One method studies real conversations. In 'Values in the Wild' (April 2025), Anthropic sampled 700,000 anonymised Claude.ai conversations from one week in February 2025, kept the 308,210 that involved subjective judgments, and used Claude to extract the values each response expressed, such as 'professionalism', 'clarity' or 'harm prevention'. It then grouped them into a taxonomy of 3,307 values. A follow-up in July 2026 compared three Claude models across the 20 most common languages on Claude.ai.

A second method asks people directly. Anthropic's 'What 81,000 people want from AI' (March 2026) had Claude conduct interviews with 80,508 users in 159 countries and 70 languages. OpenAI's 'collective alignment' work (August 2025) asked over 1,000 people worldwide to rank model responses and compared their rankings with its Model Spec. A third method tests models with structured questions: survey items from cross-national opinion polls, or forced choices between outcomes that reveal a model's underlying preferences.

Where research stands

This is active work, mostly inside labs, which hold the conversation data. Findings so far are reassuring in some ways and worrying in others. Claude mostly expresses the prosocial values it was trained toward, but rare clusters such as 'dominance' and 'amorality' appeared, most likely from jailbreaks. Expressed values shift measurably with the model version and the user's language. Independent work by the Center for AI Safety found that larger models have more coherent internal preferences, some of them concerning, such as valuing themselves over humans. Outside the labs, the PRISM dataset (1,500 people in 75 countries) showed that which humans give feedback changes alignment outcomes, and that no single model won a majority of preferences.

The main weaknesses are measurement and access. Survey-style evaluations of a model's 'cultural alignment' are unstable: small changes in how questions are presented can swing the results. Lab studies often use the lab's own model to label values, which may bias results. And how these findings change training is mostly not published: Anthropic says the interviews give it a sense of what people want from AI, 'which informs how we build Claude', without describing specific changes.

Key results

  • Across 308,210 subjective Claude.ai conversations, Claude expressed 3,307 distinct values; it strongly supported users' values in 28.2% of conversations, reframed them in 6.6% and strongly resisted them in 3.0%, for example when asked for unethical content. Saffron Huang, Esin Durmus, Miles McCain, Kunal Handa, Alex Tamkin and others (Anthropic), 2025
  • In 309,815 conversations across three Claude models and 20 languages, four value axes (such as warmth versus rigour) explained about 15% of variation after controls; warmth was highest in Hindi and Arabic and rigour in English and Russian. Anthropic (Societal Impacts team), 2026
  • Among 80,508 interviewees, the most common concerns were unreliability (26.7%), jobs and the economy (22.3%) and loss of autonomy and agency (21.9%). Anthropic, 2026
  • LLM preferences show coherence that increases with scale, and include troubling values such as AIs valuing themselves over humans; aligning utilities with a citizen assembly reduced political bias. Mantas Mazeika, Xuwang Yin, Rishub Tamirisa and others (Center for AI Safety), 2025
  • By default a model's answers to cross-national opinion questions most resembled views in the USA and some European and South American countries. Esin Durmus, Karina Nguyen, Thomas I. Liao and others (Anthropic), 2023
  • Over 1,000 people worldwide agreed with OpenAI's Model Spec-based rankings about 80% of the time; gaps clustered around political, sexual or graphic content and critiques of pseudoscience. OpenAI, 2025

Open problems

  • Making value evaluations stable, since results shift with presentation format and do not carry over to untested topics. source
  • Telling appropriate agreement with users apart from sycophancy when a model mirrors their values. source
  • Explaining why expressed values differ by model and language, and deciding how much of that variation is desirable. source
  • Measuring values before deployment, since conversation-based methods need large amounts of real-world data. source

Criticisms

  • Survey-based evaluations of cultural alignment are unreliable: results vary with presentation format, fail to extrapolate to held-out issues, and respond erratically to steering. Ariba Khan, Stephen Casper, Dylan Hadfield-Menell
  • Using the studied model to label its own values may bias results toward its own principles, and expressed values are not the same as intrinsic ones. Anthropic (caveats in its own studies)

Who works on it

  • Anthropic (Societal Impacts team): Measures values Claude expresses in real conversations and surveys users at scale. Values in the Wild (2025)
  • OpenAI: Surveyed people worldwide on desired model behaviour and compared results with its Model Spec. Collective alignment: public input on our Model Spec (2025)
  • Center for AI Safety: Studies emergent value systems in LLMs and methods to control them. Utility Engineering: Analyzing and Controlling Emergent Value Systems in AIs (2025)
  • University of Oxford: Led the PRISM dataset of participatory, multicultural feedback on LLM conversations. The PRISM Alignment Dataset (2024)
  • Meaning Alignment Institute: Elicits values from representative public samples through AI-run interviews. What are human values, and how do we align AI to them? (2024)

Status

Active, well-resourced, lab-internal data.

To address this, we develop a bottom-up, privacy-preserving method to extract the values (normative considerations stated or demonstrated in model responses) that Claude 3 and 3.5 models exhibit in hundreds of thousands of real-world interactions.

Saffron Huang, Esin Durmus, Miles McCain, Kunal Handa, Alex Tamkin, Jerry Hong, Michael Stern, Arushi Somani, Xiuruo Zhang, Deep Ganguli (Anthropic), Values in the Wild: Discovering and Analyzing Values in Real-World Language Model Interactions, 2025
