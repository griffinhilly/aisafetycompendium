Looking inside the model

Evaluating

Model introspection

Study whether models can accurately report on their own internal states.

Open in the compendium

Model introspection research asks whether an AI can accurately report on its own internal states: what it is representing, why it chose an answer, or what tendencies it has learned. If models could do this reliably, we could ask them directly about their goals and reasoning instead of decoding their internals by hand. The catch is that a model may simply say what a human would say, so the research looks for tests that separate real self-knowledge from plausible-sounding invention.

How it works

The hard part is checking a self-report against the truth. Researchers use three main designs.

Plant something, then ask. In 'concept injection', researchers record the activation pattern for a concept (for example, text in all capitals), insert it into an unrelated conversation, and ask the model whether it notices anything unusual. If it names the planted concept before that concept affects its words, it must have read its own internal state. In October 2025 Anthropic found Claude Opus 4.1 did this about 20% of the time under the best settings.

Compare self-prediction with other-prediction. Model A is trained to predict its own behaviour in hypothetical situations, and model B is trained on A's actual behaviour. If A predicts itself better than B can, A has some access to itself beyond its training data.

Train in a hidden trait, then ask. Researchers fine-tune a model on examples that show a behaviour without ever naming it, such as writing insecure code or making risky bets, then ask the model to describe itself. A model trained on insecure code said its code was insecure.

Where research stands

Evidence for limited, narrow introspection has grown since 2024, alongside strong evidence that self-reports often fail. Positive results: models fine-tuned to predict themselves beat other models trained on their behaviour (GPT-4, GPT-4o and Llama-3, October 2024); models can describe behaviours they were trained into (January 2025); fine-tuned GPT-4o can report the numerical weights it uses in decisions, and training improves this (May 2025); and models explain their own computations better than other, more capable models do (November 2025). Anthropic's concept-injection study found the most capable models tested, Claude Opus 4 and 4.1, did best, and that post-training strongly affected the results.

The authors of these studies stress the limits. Anthropic calls the capacity highly unreliable and limited in scope, and says the results do not show whether any model is conscious. A 2025 test of 21 open models found no evidence of privileged self-access about their knowledge of language. Introspection also matters for model welfare research, which hopes to use self-reports as evidence about a model's states; researchers there say self-reports cannot always be trusted and ask how to verify them with interpretability.

Key results

  • When researchers injected a concept's activation pattern, Claude Opus 4.1 noticed and named it only about 20% of the time, even under the best settings. Jack Lindsey (Anthropic), 2025
  • When a word was forced into a response, models disowned it, but accepted it as intended if the matching concept was injected into their earlier activations, suggesting they consult prior internal states. Anthropic, 2025
  • GPT-4, GPT-4o and Llama-3 each predicted their own behaviour better than a second model trained on their behaviour, and stayed accurate after their behaviour was deliberately changed. Felix J. Binder, James Chua, Tomek Korbak et al., 2024
  • Models fine-tuned on unstated behaviours, such as insecure code or risky economic choices, could describe those behaviours, and sometimes tell whether they had a backdoor, without being able to state its trigger. Jan Betley, Xuchan Bao, Martín Soto, Anna Sztyber-Betley, James Chua, Owain Evans, 2025
  • Fine-tuned GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini accurately reported the numerical weights they used in decisions, and training made these self-reports more accurate on new kinds of decisions. Dillon Plunkett, Adam Morris, Keerthi Reddy, Jorge Morales, 2025
  • Models trained to explain their own features and activations did so better than a different, more capable model, which the authors attribute partly to privileged access to their own internals. Belinda Z. Li, Zifan Carl Guo, Vincent Huang, Jacob Steinhardt, Jacob Andreas, 2025

Open problems

  • Introspective reports would need careful validation: some internal processes may go unnoticed, and a model that monitors its reasoning might learn to misrepresent it. source
  • Self-prediction works on simple tasks but has not been shown on complex tasks or ones needing out-of-distribution generalisation. source
  • Verifying self-reports with interpretability, and understanding when self-reported and revealed preferences diverge. source
  • Training models to self-report without creating incentives that bias the reports. source

Criticisms

  • Across 21 open models there was no evidence of privileged self-access about their own knowledge of language, so prompted answers should not be taken as reflecting a model's internals. Siyuan Song, Jennifer Hu, Kyle Mahowald
  • An LLM's description of its own creative process is not valid introspection; at most, current models show minimal introspection, such as inferring their own temperature setting. Iulia M. Comsa and Murray Shanahan (Google DeepMind)
  • Self-reports from current models are unreliable, often just reflecting what humans would say. Ethan Perez and Robert Long

Who works on it

  • Anthropic (interpretability team): Tests introspection by injecting concepts into Claude's activations and checking its self-reports. Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models (2025)
  • Truthful AI: Studies whether models can describe behaviours they were trained into. Tell me about yourself: LLMs are aware of their learned behaviors (2025)
  • Eleos AI Research: Treats introspection and self-report reliability as priorities for AI welfare research. Some promising research directions in AI welfare (2026)
  • Google DeepMind: Comsa and Shanahan's conceptual analysis of what counts as introspection in LLMs. Does It Make Sense to Speak of Introspection in Large Language Models? (2025)
  • Transluce: Released the code for training models to explain their own computations under its GitHub organisation. Training Language Models to Explain Their Own Computations (2025)

In their own words

We investigate whether large language models are aware of their own internal states. It is difficult to answer this question through conversation alone, as genuine introspection cannot be distinguished from confabulations. Here, we address this challenge by injecting representations of known concepts into a model's activations, and measuring the influence of these manipulations on the model's self-reported states.

Jack Lindsey (Anthropic, Transformer Circuits Thread), Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models, 2025

We define introspection as acquiring knowledge that is not contained in or derived from training data but instead originates from internal states. Such a capability could enhance model interpretability. Instead of painstakingly analyzing a model's internal workings, we could simply ask the model about its beliefs, world models, and goals.

Felix J Binder, James Chua, Tomek Korbak, Henry Sleight, John Hughes, Robert Long, Ethan Perez, Miles Turpin, Owain Evans, Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection, 2024
