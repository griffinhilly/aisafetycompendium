Alignment as jurisprudence

Treat alignment like judging: principled decisions under constraints, applied to cases nobody foresaw.

Open in the compendium

Alignment as jurisprudence treats the job of guiding an AI like the job of guiding a judge. Jurisprudence is the study of how judges should decide cases, especially new cases that existing rules did not foresee. The idea is that the law's centuries of experience with rules, principles, precedent and interpretation can help AI systems apply written values sensibly to situations their designers never imagined.

How it works

A judge facing a new case starts from statutes or constitutional rules, looks at earlier cases (precedents), and reasons from principles and analogies when the rules run out. An AI model governed by a written constitution faces the same problem: the document is general, and each user request is a new case. Nicholas Caputo's 2025 article in the Yale Journal of Law & Technology maps two legal theories onto two alignment methods. Ronald Dworkin's 'interpretivism', which resolves hard cases by appeal to the principles that best justify the law as a whole, resembles Constitutional AI, which trains a model from broad principles. Cass Sunstein's account of law as analogical reasoning, where people agree on particular outcomes without agreeing on deep theory, resembles case-based alignment, which trains or prompts a model with judged examples.

Researchers have started to build on this. 'Case repositories' collect questions a model might be asked, have experts and members of the public judge them, and use the judgments as precedents. 'Case law grounding' retrieves relevant past decisions and uses them to guide a new one. A Princeton team borrowed tools from statutory interpretation: refine ambiguous rules, as a legislature or agency would, and constrain how rules are applied, as legal canons constrain judges.

Where research stands

This is mainly a conceptual programme with a few early empirical tests. Caputo's article is the main statement. Caputo is also one of 17 co-authors of a January 2026 'legal alignment' agenda, which lists legal interpretation as one of three research directions. The empirical results so far are encouraging but small. Precedent-based decisions were more accurate in four of five communities tested. Rule refinement and interpretive constraints made judgments about a 5,000-scenario sample of real chatbot conversations more consistent across interpreters.

What is missing is evidence at the scale of frontier models, and an answer to the problem that law itself is often indeterminate. Anthropic's 2026 constitution moved toward explained principles and judgment over rigid rules, which fits the Dworkinian side of the analogy.

Key results

  • Jurisprudence and alignment share a structure: both try to predict and shape decisions by powerful actors in unforeseen future cases, so legal theories of rules, principles and analogy can improve how alignment combines rules and cases. Nicholas A. Caputo, 2025
  • Case repositories, built from seed cases, expert workshops, LLM-generated variations and public judgments, are proposed as a complement to constitutional alignment. K. J. Kevin Feng, Quan Ze Chen, Inyoung Cheong, King Xia, Amy X. Zhang, 2023
  • Grounding decisions in retrieved precedents made decisions significantly more accurate in 4 of 5 communities, with gains of 20.8 to 32.9 percentage points when an LLM was prompted with precedents. Quan Ze Chen, Amy X. Zhang, 2025
  • Refining ambiguous rules and adding interpretive constraints, modelled on rulemaking and legal canons, significantly improved consistency of rule application on a 5,000-scenario subset of WildChat. Luxi He, Nimra Nadeem, Michel Liao, Howard Chen, Danqi Chen, Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar, Peter Henderson, 2025
  • Legal interpretation is one of three directions of a proposed 'legal alignment' field, alongside legal compliance and legal concepts as a structural blueprint. Noam Kolt, Nicholas Caputo and 15 others, 2026

Open problems

  • Building the equivalent of appellate review for AI: current alignment pipelines lack safeguards against inconsistent readings of the same rule. source
  • Choosing the right analogy in hard cases, where precedents are far from the new situation. source
  • Deciding who judges the cases in a repository, given that values are diverse and conflicting. source

Criticisms

  • In the hard cases that matter most, analogical reasoning without a governing principle gives no real guidance, so the decision-maker can pick whatever analogy it likes; Caputo raises this objection and answers it with Sunstein's replies. Nicholas A. Caputo (stating the critic's objection)
  • Law is often complicated, indeterminate and contested, so importing legal methods also imports legal indeterminacy. Noam Kolt and co-authors

Who works on it

  • Oxford Martin AI Governance Initiative: Home of Caputo's legal-alignment research and the 2026 legal alignment agenda paper. Legal Alignment for Safe and Ethical AI (2026)
  • Princeton University, Center for Information Technology Policy (POLARIS Lab): Built a statutory-interpretation framework to reduce ambiguity in AI constitutions. Statutory Construction & Interpretation for AI (2025)
  • University of Washington: Developed case repositories and case law grounding for precedent-based alignment. Case Repositories: Towards Case-Based Reasoning for AI Alignment (2023)

In their own words

Jurisprudence, the study of how judges should properly decide cases, and alignment, the science of getting AI models to conform to human values, share a fundamental structure. These seemingly distant fields both seek to predict and shape how decisions by two sets of powerful actors, judges and increasingly powerful artificial intelligences (AIs), will be made in the unknown future.

Nicholas A. Caputo, Alignment as Jurisprudence, 2025

Drawing on leading accounts of jurisprudence, particularly Dworkin's principle-oriented interpretivism and Sunstein's positivist account of law as analogical reasoning, and on cutting-edge alignment approaches, namely Constitutional AI and case-based reasoning, it illustrates the value of a more sophisticated legally-inspired approach to the interplay of rules and cases in finetuning alignment ...

Nicholas A. Caputo, Alignment as Jurisprudence, 2025
