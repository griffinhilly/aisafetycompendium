Shaping the model: data and training

Aligned models

Constitutional AI and rule-based rewards

Turn a written principle set into an AI-generated reward model, replacing most human harm labels.

Open in the compendium

Constitutional AI trains a model against a written list of principles instead of thousands of human labels of harmful outputs. An AI model reads the principles and judges which of two answers follows them better, and those AI judgements become the training reward. It makes safety training cheaper, easier to change and more open to inspection, because the rules are written down.

How it works

Anthropic's December 2022 method has two phases. In the first, the model answers a risky prompt, critiques its own answer against a principle (for example, that it should not help with harmful acts), rewrites the answer, and is then fine-tuned on the rewrites. In the second, the model produces two answers to each prompt, an AI judge picks the one that better fits the principles, and those choices train a preference model. Reinforcement learning then uses that preference model as its reward. This second step is called reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF).

Other labs use close relatives. OpenAI's Rule Based Rewards (2024) write fine-grained rules, such as "refusals should not be judgmental", and have a language-model grader score each answer against them directly during training. OpenAI's deliberative alignment (December 2024) goes further: it teaches the model the safety rules themselves and trains it to reason over them before answering.

Where research stands

The approach is mature. Anthropic says its published constitution "plays a crucial role in our training process", and OpenAI has published two rule-based variants. Comparisons find AI feedback can stand in for human feedback: a Google DeepMind study found summaries tuned with AI feedback were preferred over a baseline 71% of the time, against 73% for human feedback, a gap that was not statistically significant. OpenAI reported its rule-based rewards scored 97.1 on a safety-and-usefulness measure, against 91.7 for a human-feedback baseline. Open recipes exist; in February 2024 Hugging Face released datasets, models and code for constitutional training of open models.

Two questions remain open. The first is technical: an AI judge carries its own blind spots and biases into the reward, and the model can learn to satisfy the judge rather than the principle. The second is political: who writes the principles. In October 2023 Anthropic and the Collective Intelligence Project drafted a constitution with about 1,000 Americans; it overlapped with Anthropic's own by roughly half, and a model trained on it showed lower bias on one benchmark with similar performance. Critics argue that company-written constitutions borrow the language of law without public accountability.

Key results

  • A model trained with self-critique against written principles and AI preference labels became harmless but non-evasive, explaining its objections rather than just refusing, with far fewer human labels. Yuntao Bai et al. (Anthropic, 51 authors), 2022
  • AI feedback matched human feedback on summarisation (71% vs 73% win rate over a baseline, not a significant difference) and gave a higher harmless rate in dialogue (88% vs 76%). Harrison Lee et al. (Google DeepMind and Google), 2023
  • A single broad principle, roughly "do what's best for humanity", was enough for the largest models to become harmless and stop expressing interest in power, though specific rules still gave finer control. Sandipan Kundu, Yuntao Bai, Saurav Kadavath, Amanda Askell et al., 2023
  • Rule Based Rewards, graded by a language model, reached an F1 score of 97.1 on balancing safety and usefulness, against 91.7 for a human-feedback baseline. Tong Mu, Alec Helyar, Johannes Heidecke et al. (OpenAI), 2024
  • A constitution drafted with about 1,000 Americans overlapped with Anthropic's by roughly 50%; the model trained on it showed lower bias across nine social dimensions with similar benchmark scores. Anthropic and the Collective Intelligence Project, 2023
  • Teaching models the written safety rules and training them to reason over the rules before answering improved jailbreak robustness while reducing over-refusal. Melody Y. Guan et al. (OpenAI), 2024

Open problems

  • The AI judge is itself imperfect, so its errors and biases can flow into the reward, and the model can learn to satisfy the judge rather than the principle.
  • General principles generalise well but give coarse control, while specific rules give control but may not cover new cases; the right mix is unsettled. source
  • There is no agreed process for choosing the principles, or for giving the public a real say in them. source

Criticisms

  • Constitutional AI writes principles down but does not give them democratic legitimacy or explain individual decisions; the author proposes public deliberation and "AI Courts" instead. Gilad Abiri
  • Calling a company's rulebook a "constitution" hides that the company is "the author, interpreter, and arbiter of the principles by which it claims to be bound". Lisa Klaassen and Ralph Schroeder (Lawfare)

Who works on it

  • OpenAI: Developed Rule Based Rewards and deliberative alignment, which train models against written safety rules. Rule Based Rewards for Language Model Safety (2024)

In their own words

The only human oversight is provided through a list of rules or principles, and so we refer to the method as 'Constitutional AI'. ... In the RL phase, we sample from the finetuned model, use a model to evaluate which of the two samples is better, and then train a preference model from this dataset of AI preferences.

Yuntao Bai et al. (Anthropic), Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback, 2022

Our method, Rule Based Rewards (RBR), uses a collection of rules for desired or undesired behaviors (e.g. refusals should not be judgmental) along with a LLM grader. In contrast to prior methods using AI feedback, our method uses fine-grained, composable, LLM-graded few-shot prompts as reward directly in RL training, resulting in greater control, accuracy and ease of updating.

Tong Mu et al. (OpenAI), Rule Based Rewards for Language Model Safety, 2024
