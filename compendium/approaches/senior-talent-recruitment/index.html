Building the field

Field-building

Recruiting senior talent from other fields

Bring experienced people from law, security, hardware, policy and entrepreneurship into senior and founding roles in AI safety.

Open in the compendium

This approach recruits experienced people from outside AI safety, such as security engineers, hardware engineers, lawyers, Washington policy staff, intelligence officers and entrepreneurs, into founder and senior roles in safety work. Its premise is that the field now has many early-career researchers but too few senior people who can lead teams, mentor others and bring specialist skills. It matters because many planned safety institutions, from model-weight security to treaty verification, need expertise the field has not grown internally.

How it works

A dedicated recruiter works like an executive-search firm with a mission. It maps what the field needs, finds people who are not looking for a new job, pitches them on the problem, and vets them carefully. Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind lists this as 'Senior talent headhunting' (September 2026). Its first-year test is to help four or five people move from government or industry jobs, and its two-year test is to place roughly 10 to 15 specialists into founder or leadership roles each year.

Recruiting is paired with 'context building', because a senior hire from outside may not yet understand AI safety as a field. Tailwind's companion idea, an 'AI context accelerator', proposes paid, executive-education-style programs for professionals with 15 or more years of experience; Coefficient's policy team was already running a 30-person pilot. Existing services fill parts of the chain: Impact Ops runs active headhunting and hiring rounds for high-impact nonprofits, Successif gives free career advising to experienced professionals moving into AI risk work, Heron helps cybersecurity professionals move into frontier AI security, and the Horizon Institute for Public Service trains and places technology specialists in US federal agencies, congressional offices and think tanks.

Where research stands

Evidence that senior talent is the bottleneck comes mostly from interview studies, not hard data. In 2024 MATS interviewed 31 senior figures and found that organisations of 10 to 30 staff most needed 'Amplifiers', people who shine as managers and communicators. A second MATS study, 23 interviews in late 2025, found organisations are 'capacity constrained by a lack of senior researchers who can mentor and supervise junior talent', which pushes them to hire only people who need little supervision. A May 2026 review of three years of hiring posts concluded the field has strong pipelines for young researchers (2,000 to 2,500 fellows expected to graduate from fellowships in 2026) but scarce senior operators, research managers and political operators, and no systematic data on founder and leader shortages.

The dedicated headhunting firm Tailwind describes was still an open call as of October 2026. Recruiting and advising services exist. The main unknown is whether outside experts, once recruited, make better decisions than people who grew up inside the field.

Key results

  • From 31 interviews in 2024, MATS found that growing safety organisations (10 to 30 staff) prioritise 'Amplifiers' (strong managers and communicators) over hands-on researchers, while lab safety teams prioritise fast experimentalists. yams, Carson Jones, McKenna Fitzgerald, Ryan Kidd, 2024
  • From 23 interviews in late 2025, MATS found that a shortage of senior people who can supervise juniors drives hyper-selective hiring, and that AI automation is raising, not lowering, the bar for human contributors. jteichma (MATS Research), 2026
  • A review of three years of published hiring material found strong pipelines for junior researchers but scarce senior operators, research managers and experienced political operators, with no systematic data on leader and founder shortages. Weronika Żurek, 2026
  • Coefficient Giving set success tests for a headhunting firm: help four or five people transition in year one, and place roughly 10 to 15 specialists into founder or leadership roles a year by year two. Coefficient Giving, 2026

Criticisms

  • When hiring grantmakers, choosing an experienced person from outside the AI safety community 'does not always turn out to be a good choice', because traits such as value alignment and agency are not tied to experience; it is usually a better bet to hire a young person and give them time to learn. An AI governance grantmaker, as reported by Weronika Żurek
  • The evidence that senior talent is the binding constraint is interview impressions from hiring managers and funders, not a quantitative study, so the size of the gap is uncertain. MATS Research (stated limitation of its own study)

  • Impact Ops: Recruitment service for high-impact organisations, using active headhunting and managed hiring rounds. Recruitment
  • Successif: Free career advising, training and introductions for experienced professionals moving into AI risk reduction. Successif (home page)
  • Heron AI Security: Tel Aviv nonprofit that helps experienced cybersecurity professionals move into frontier AI security, including founders of new organisations. Heron AI Security (home page)
  • Horizon Institute for Public Service: Trains and places fully funded technology fellows in US federal agencies, congressional offices and think tanks. Horizon Institute for Public Service (home page)
  • MATS Research: Publishes interview studies of AI safety hiring needs, including demand for senior and 'Amplifier' roles. AI Safety Talent Needs in 2026: Insights for Field-Building Organizations (2026)

In their own words

Many of the initiatives in this list benefit from particular kinds of expertise, such as information security, hardware engineering, law, D.C. policy or intelligence work, or entrepreneurship. ... We think there's a big opportunity to recruit people with these kinds of backgrounds to found new projects or take on senior roles in existing high-impact organizations.

Coefficient Giving, Project Tailwind: Initiatives (Senior talent headhunting), 2026

We'd like an impact-motivated headhunting firm dedicated to engaging and recruiting senior or specialized talent. We expect this to require a deep understanding of the ecosystem's needs and impact, highly specialized searches, a willingness to approach candidates who may not actively be looking to do something new, and significant effort on both pitching and vetting top talent.

Coefficient Giving, Project Tailwind: Initiatives (Senior talent headhunting), 2026
