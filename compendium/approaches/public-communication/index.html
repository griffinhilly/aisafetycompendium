Building the field

Field-building

Public communication and content creation

Explain AI risk to wide audiences through video, podcasts, books, social media and journalism.

Open in the compendium

Public communication explains AI risk to people outside the field, through YouTube explainers, podcasts, popular books, social media and news reporting. It is often a person's first introduction to the topic, so it brings new people into the field. It also shapes what the public, journalists and officials understand about AI risk, and so what policy is possible.

How it works

Creators do most of the work, with funders behind them. Robert Miles has made YouTube explainers on alignment problems for about ten years. In an informal 2025 survey of who people think of as AI-safety communicators, almost everyone named him first, and a few said his videos helped lead them into safety careers. Rational Animations, a US nonprofit with a remote team of about 40, makes animated videos on AI risk; Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving) recommended grants of about $2.8 million to it from 2021 to 2024. In July 2025 80,000 Hours launched AI in Context, a host-led channel presented by Aric Floyd. Its first long video retold AI 2027, a forecast written as a story by the AI Futures Project. Funders also pay existing creators to cover the topic. The Future of Life Institute's Digital Media Accelerator funds videos, newsletters and podcasts on AI risk and requires creators to disclose its support.

Journalism runs through newsrooms. The Tarbell Center for AI Journalism trains journalists for three months and then pays their salaries while they report on AI for nine months at outlets such as TIME, MIT Technology Review and CBS News. It also gives reporting grants of $1,000 to $20,000 and grants of $50,000 or more to outlets, and it publishes Transformer, an editorially independent outlet. Tarbell argues that expert reporters keep decision-makers informed, hold companies to their safety promises, and spot early incidents that point to larger risks.

A third route is books and organisational campaigns. In January 2024 the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) said policy and communications would now take priority over research. In September 2025 its co-founder Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares published a book, If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies, which reached the New York Times best-seller lists.

In nuclear power, Isabelle Boemeke, a Brazilian model, runs 'Isodope', a social-media persona devoted only to promoting nuclear power. Her 2022 TED talk had passed 1.8 million views by August 2025. AI safety's nearest equivalents are host-led YouTube channels. In October 2026 Robert Miles's channel had about 170,000 subscribers, AI in Context about 450,000, Species (Drew Spartz) about 470,000, Siliconversations about 180,000 and Doom Debates (Liron Shapira) about 150,000. 80,000 Hours calls its own approach 'personality-driven'. But a 2025 directory of AI-safety video lists groups still looking for on-camera hosts, and it gives no audience figures for the short-form creators on TikTok. The evidence does not show a single-issue, mass-audience influencer of Boemeke's kind in AI safety, and whether that gap is worth filling has not been tested.

Where research stands

By January 2026 at least 19 AI-safety YouTube channels had more than 10,000 subscribers, and nine had more than 100,000. AI in Context's first long video had 8.9 million views and about 1.4 million watch hours by December 2025, against an expected 15,000 to 50,000 views, at about $0.11 per hour watched including staff time. A September 2025 comparison of channels on cost found that good AI-safety channels produce about 150 to 300 quality-adjusted viewer-minutes per dollar, with the best results from channels that earn their own revenue and from videos of 5 to 30 minutes. Journalism support is expanding too: in 2025 Tarbell planned to grow from 16 to 20 fellows a year, and its 2027-28 fellowship applications close on 22 November 2026.

The effects on audiences are poorly measured. AI in Context tracks views and watch hours but has no good data on who watches or whether behaviour changes, and it is unsure what to ask viewers to do. Survey experiments give early guidance on messages. In three experiments with 10,800 people in the US and UK, existential-risk headlines raised concern about catastrophe without lowering concern about present harms. In another, fear raised perceived risk, but support for regulation depended more on showing that regulation would work. Stories with an identifiable victim raised concern more than statistics did. Funded journalism faces a separate question of trust, after Fox News reported in August 2026 that some outlets did not tell readers that Tarbell paid their fellows.

Key results

  • AI in Context's first two long videos drew 8.9 million and 2.7 million views, far above the 15,000 to 50,000 views expected, at $0.11 and $0.39 per hour watched including staff time. Chana Messinger (80,000 Hours), 2025
  • An early cost-effectiveness comparison of AI-safety YouTube channels estimated about 150 to 300 quality-adjusted viewer-minutes per dollar for good channels, with creators who earn money from their videos and 5-30 minute videos doing best. Marcus Abramovitch, 2025
  • In three survey experiments with 10,800 people in the US and UK, existential-risk headlines raised concern about catastrophe without reducing concern about AI's present harms. Emma Hoes, Fabrizio Gilardi, 2025
  • In an experiment with 1,200 Americans, fear and message credibility raised perceived AI risk, but support for regulation depended more on conveying that regulation would be effective. Johanna Roniger, 2024
  • Among 1,794 participants in the US, UK and Australia, stories about AI harms with an identifiable victim raised concern more than stories built on statistics, because readers found them more vivid. Tabi Ward, Michael Noetel, 2024
  • A book arguing that superhuman AI would kill everyone reached the New York Times best-seller lists for hardcover and for combined print and e-book nonfiction on its release in September 2025. Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nate Soares, 2025

Open problems

  • Measuring impact beyond views: who watches, and whether watching changes beliefs, careers or votes. source
  • Knowing what to ask audiences to do once they are persuaded; AI in Context says it is unsure, and a January 2026 review calls for more content on practical actions. source
  • Finding enough scriptwriters and on-camera hosts; scriptwriting is AI in Context's biggest bottleneck, and some groups are seeking hosts. source
  • Avoiding messages that backfire, such as convincing people that AI will be powerful without convincing them that building it carelessly is dangerous. source
  • Keeping philanthropically funded journalism independent and clearly disclosed to readers. source

Criticisms

  • Talk of AI destroying humanity plays into tech companies' agenda, distracts regulators from harms AI causes now, and fuels competition between nations (2025 survey experiments found no such distraction effect among the public). Nature editorial (27 June 2023)
  • Blasting out hyper-simplified messages could do more harm than good, partly by associating important ideas with being annoying. Holden Karnofsky (Cold Takes, January 2023)
  • Several major outlets host reporters whose salaries a donor-funded group pays, and some do not tell readers, which raises questions about disclosure standards. Leo Briceno (Fox News, 4 August 2026)
  • Some viewers find the videos fear-mongering, alarmist or too speculative, and one critic said the AI 2027 video told a story without making an argument. YouTube commenters and Matt Reardon, as summarised by AI in Context

Who works on it

  • 80,000 Hours: Runs the AI in Context YouTube channel and a video podcast, and publishes career advice that treats communication as fieldbuilding. 80,000 Hours is producing AI in Context — a new YouTube channel. Our first video, about the AI 2027 scenario, is up! (2025)
  • Tarbell Center for AI Journalism: Trains journalists and pays them to report on AI in major newsrooms; gives reporting and outlet grants; publishes Transformer. Tarbell Fellowship (2026)
  • Rational Animations: US nonprofit making animated YouTube videos on AI risk; about 500,000 subscribers in October 2026. Rational Animations (YouTube channel) (2026)
  • AISafety.info: Explainer website and chatbot on AI risk, founded by Robert Miles and maintained by specialists and volunteers. AISafety.info (2026)
  • Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI): Said in January 2024 that policy and communications would take priority over research; its co-founder Eliezer Yudkowsky co-wrote a best-selling book on AI extinction risk. MIRI 2024 Mission and Strategy Update (2024)
  • Palisade Research: Runs a video program on AI risk (frominside.ai) alongside research for policymakers and communicators. Palisade Research home page (videos) (2026)
  • Seismic Foundation: Nonprofit providing marketing and communications for the responsible-AI movement, including content for the general public. Seismic Foundation (2026)
  • AI Futures Project: Wrote AI 2027, a forecast told as a story; 80,000 Hours lists it among projects that are often someone's first introduction to AI safety. AI 2027 (2025)
  • Future of Life Institute: Funds creators through its Digital Media Accelerator and has given Tarbell $100,000 to $1 million. Digital Media Accelerator (2026)
  • Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy): In Tarbell's top donor tier ($1M+, 2023-2026); as Open Philanthropy it recommended about $2.8 million in grants to Rational Animations from 2021 to 2024. Tarbell: Our Supporters (2026)
  • Survival and Flourishing Fund: Gave Tarbell $1M+ in lifetime support and offered a 1:1 match of up to $200,000 on its 2025 fundraiser. Tarbell Center for AI Journalism (Manifund) (2025)
  • Longview Philanthropy: In Tarbell's top tier of lifetime donors ($1M+), listed for 2026. Tarbell: Our Supporters (2026)
  • Long-Term Future Fund: Has funded Robert Miles's AI-safety videos since 2019, most recently $150,000 in 2025 for a year of monthly videos. EA Funds grants database (2025)

Status

Active and growing: at least 19 AI-safety YouTube channels had over 10,000 subscribers by Jan 2026; AI in Context's first video passed 8.9M views (Dec 2025); effects on what viewers do are largely unmeasured.
