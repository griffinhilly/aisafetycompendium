Deciding what AI should value

Aligned models

Defining the alignment target

Sort the candidate things an AI could be aligned to — instructions, intentions, preferences, interests, values.

Open in the compendium

Value specification is the question of what exactly an AI should be aligned to. 'Do what humans want' hides very different options: the literal instructions a person gives, what they intend, what their behaviour shows they prefer, what they would prefer if well informed, what is actually good for them, or what is morally right. An AI that perfectly hits the wrong target can still cause serious harm.

How it works

Iason Gabriel's 2020 paper 'Artificial Intelligence, Values and Alignment' lists six targets. Take a user who asks an AI agent to 'book the cheapest flight to Rome'. An instruction-follower books the cheapest flight even if it has three changes and leaves at 3 a.m. An intention-follower infers that the user wants a cheap but reasonable trip. A revealed-preference follower looks at what the user has booked before. An ideal-preference follower asks what the user would choose if fully informed. An interest-follower considers what is best for the user's well-being. A values-follower also asks what the user and the AI morally ought to do, for example about harms to others.

Gabriel argues that a good approach combines these targets in a principled way. He adds that the central task is to find fair principles that people can reflectively endorse despite their deep disagreements about morality, instead of searching for the one true moral theory.

Where research stands

One line of later work keeps the target narrow on purpose: Paul Christiano's 2018 definition of 'intent alignment' says an AI is aligned if it is trying to do what its operator wants, and treats the question of which values it should serve as separate. Another line argues that preferences are the wrong target altogether. In 2024 Tan Zhi-Xuan and co-authors proposed aligning AI to the normative standards of its social role, such as 'general-purpose assistant', negotiated among stakeholders. A third line, 'pluralistic alignment', asks how one model can serve people with different values, for example by laying out a range of reasonable views or by being steerable to a given perspective.

Deployed systems mix targets: companies write specs that combine following instructions, inferring intent and respecting ethics and law. The 2025 Shallow Review of technical AI safety groups related work under 'Aligned to who?' and estimates about 5 to 15 full-time researchers on that cluster. There is still no agreed method for deciding between targets when they conflict.

Key results

  • Alignment could target instructions, intentions, revealed preferences, ideal preferences, interests or values; the key task is finding fair principles people can endorse despite moral disagreement, not the true moral theory. Iason Gabriel, 2020
  • Defining alignment narrowly as 'A is trying to do what H wants it to do' separates the AI's motives from its knowledge of values. Paul Christiano, 2018
  • Preferences do not adequately capture human values; AI should instead be aligned with normative standards suited to its social role, agreed among stakeholders. Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin, Hal Ashton, 2024
  • Pluralistic alignment can mean presenting a range of reasonable answers, being steerable to a perspective, or matching a population's distribution of views; standard alignment procedures may reduce the last of these. Taylor Sorensen, Jared Moore, Jillian Fisher and others, 2024
  • A value-elicitation process with a representative sample of 500 Americans proposed six criteria for an alignment target; 89.1% of participants felt well represented. Oliver Klingefjord, Ryan Lowe, Joe Edelman, 2024

Criticisms

  • Bundling 'which values' into the definition of alignment mixes many less urgent subproblems with the core one, which is making the AI genuinely try to help its operator. Paul Christiano
  • The dominant 'preferentist' framing, which treats preference satisfaction as the target, is itself inadequate, so choosing among types of preference misses the deeper problem. Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin, Hal Ashton

Who works on it

  • Google DeepMind: Published a large study of advanced AI assistants that treats value alignment with users and society as a core question. The Ethics of Advanced AI Assistants (2024)
  • Meaning Alignment Institute: Proposed criteria for an alignment target and a method for eliciting values from the public. What are human values, and how do we align AI to them? (2024)

Status

The field's standard treatment. Named in 3 of the 7 source groups; in the 2025 Shallow Review, "Aligned to who?" sits under "Multi-agent first".

Start reading

  • Artificial Intelligence, Values and Alignment, Iason Gabriel (2020). The source of the six-target list.
  • Clarifying “AI Alignment”, Paul Christiano (2018). The case for a deliberately narrow target.
  • Beyond Preferences in AI Alignment, Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin, Hal Ashton (2024). The case against preferences as the target.
  • A Roadmap to Pluralistic Alignment, Taylor Sorensen and others (2024). How one model can serve many value systems.

Related approaches

  • Assistance games (CIRL) learning human preferences formally
  • Model specs and constitutions specs that encode a chosen target

In their own words

Second, it is important to be clear about the goal of alignment. There are significant differences between AI that aligns with instructions, intentions, revealed preferences, ideal preferences, interests and values. A principle-based approach to AI alignment, which combines these elements in a systematic way, has considerable advantages in this context.

Iason Gabriel (DeepMind), Artificial Intelligence, Values, and Alignment, 2020

Instructions: the agent does what I instruct it to do. ... Revealed preferences: the agent does what my behaviour reveals I prefer. ... Interest or well-being: the agent does what is in my interest, or what is best for me, objectively speaking. ... Values: the agent does what it morally ought to do, as defined by the individual or society.

Iason Gabriel (DeepMind), Artificial Intelligence, Values, and Alignment, 2020
