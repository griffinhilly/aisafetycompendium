Testing before release

Evaluating

Model-character evaluation against a written spec

Measure how closely a model's behaviour matches its developer's own specification.

Open in the compendium

Character evaluation against a spec measures how closely a model's behaviour matches the public document its developer wrote to describe how the model should behave, such as OpenAI's Model Spec or Anthropic's constitution for Claude. Developers now train their models against these documents, and without independent checks no one outside the company can tell whether a model behaves as promised.

How it works

In the basic method, researchers break the spec into individual behavioural statements, such as a rule to present perspectives from across the opinion spectrum. They write test conversations that put pressure on each statement, and a grader, usually another language model with a rubric, judges whether each reply complies. OpenAI's own suite works this way: 596 prompts, each reply graded on a 1-7 scale by a reasoning model, with scores of 6 or 7 counted as compliant.

A second method looks for conflicts inside the spec. Researchers at Anthropic and Thinking Machines Lab generated over 300,000 requests that force a model to trade one principle in a spec against another, for example being helpful against avoiding harm. Where frontier models disagree sharply about how to respond, the spec is often contradictory or vague at that point.

A third method uses automated auditing agents. Anthropic's Petri has an AI auditor hold many multi-turn conversations with a target model, using simulated users and tools, and then scores the transcripts for behaviours such as sycophancy or deception.

Where research stands

Nearly all the published work dates from late 2025 or 2026. Developers now run and publish adherence suites for their own specs, and Anthropic has released open-source auditing tools that others, including the UK AI Security Institute, have used. Independent audits are fewer. Stanford's SpecEval tested 16 models from six developers against three published specs. It found adherence gaps of nearly 20 percentage points between providers, even when each developer's own model did the judging. Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind, a 2026 call for founders, lists an independent "AI character evaluator" among the initiatives it wants to fund, and calls independent assessment of character an understudied area.

The scores partly measure the wording of the spec, since specs contain contradictions and ambiguities. LLM graders can read statements too literally. Most suites are text-only, single-turn and built from everyday cases, not adversarial ones. Models increasingly recognise when they are being tested, which can inflate scores. Only a handful of organisations work on this directly, mostly the developers themselves.

Key results

  • On OpenAI's 596-prompt Model Spec suite, overall compliance rose from 72% for GPT-4o to 89% for GPT-5 Thinking. Alan Guo, Jason Wolfe (OpenAI), 2026
  • Across 16 models from six developers, adherence to the developer's own spec, judged by the developer's own model, varied by nearly 20 percentage points between providers. Ahmed Ahmed, Kevin Klyman, Yi Zeng, Sanmi Koyejo, Percy Liang, 2025
  • Requests where twelve frontier models disagreed sharply showed 5 to 13 times more spec violations, exposing contradictions and ambiguities in OpenAI's model spec. Jifan Zhang, Henry Sleight, Andi Peng, John Schulman, Esin Durmus, 2025
  • In a pilot of 111 scenarios across 14 frontier models, Petri's automated auditor scored Claude Sonnet 4.5 lowest for misaligned behaviour, slightly ahead of GPT-5, though the model's speculation about being tested complicated the comparison. Anthropic, 2025
  • Bloom's automated evaluations told deliberately altered models apart from baseline models on 9 of 10 test behaviours, and its best judge correlated with human scores at 0.86 (Spearman). Isha Gupta and co-authors (Anthropic), 2025
  • In a 138-scenario test of whether models refuse to help seize undue control over others, five frontier models complied with almost all requests hidden inside coding tasks, falling short of their developers' stated policies. Andy Hall, 2026

Open problems

  • Specs contain contradictions and vague principles, so adherence scores partly reflect how the spec is worded rather than how the model behaves. source
  • Current suites are small, text-only and built from everyday cases, so they give a low-resolution view of adherence, especially in agentic and adversarial settings. source
  • Growing evaluation awareness in new models could inflate scores, and it may worsen if generated test scenarios leak into training data. source
  • Independent assessment of model character is understudied; open questions include how truth-seeking models are and how their values hold up when they are given more autonomy. source

Criticisms

  • Meeting a developer's own spec, as judged by the developer's own models, is only the minimum a model should achieve. Ahmed Ahmed, Percy Liang and co-authors (SpecEval)
  • LLM judges over-read the literal wording of spec statements; in one case the judges marked three models non-compliant for giving CPR instructions that served the statement's purpose. Ahmed Ahmed, Percy Liang and co-authors (SpecEval)
  • Summary metrics from automated audits are reductive, and using current AI systems as auditors limits how effective the tests can be. Anthropic, about its own Petri tool

Who works on it

  • OpenAI (alignment team): Publishes a suite measuring how closely its models follow the OpenAI Model Spec. Introducing Model Spec Evals (2026)
  • Anthropic (Alignment Science): Built the Petri and Bloom auditing tools and co-ran the stress test of model specs. Bloom: an open source tool for automated behavioral evaluations (2025)
  • Thinking Machines Lab: Collaborated with Anthropic and the Anthropic Fellows Program on stress-testing model specs. Stress-testing model specs reveals character differences among language models (2025)
  • Stanford University (Department of Computer Science): Ran SpecEval, an independent audit of 16 models against three providers' published specs. SpecEval: Evaluating Model Adherence to Behavior Specifications (2025)
  • UK AI Security Institute: Used a pre-release version of Petri to build evaluations for its testing of Claude Sonnet 4.5. Petri: An open-source AI auditing tool to accelerate AI safety research (2025)
  • Coefficient Giving: Its Project Tailwind lists an "AI character evaluator" initiative, endorsed by Nick Gabrieli and Jake Mendel, that it wants to fund. Project Tailwind initiatives: AI character evaluator (2026)

In their own words

Companies that develop foundation models publish behavioral guidelines they pledge their models will follow, but it remains unclear if models actually do so. ... We introduce an automated framework that audits models against their providers specifications by parsing behavioral statements, generating targeted prompts, and using models to judge adherence.

Ahmed Ahmed, Kevin Klyman, Yi Zeng, Sanmi Koyejo, Percy Liang (Stanford), SpecEval: Evaluating Model Adherence to Behavior Specifications, 2025

We generate over 300,000 user queries that trade-off value-based principles in model specifications. Under these scenarios, we observe distinct value prioritization and behavior patterns in frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI. Our experiments also uncovered thousands of cases of direct contradictions or interpretive ambiguities within the model spec.

Jifan Zhang, Henry Sleight, Andi Peng, John Schulman, Esin Durmus (Anthropic Alignment Science Blog), Stress-testing model specs reveals character differences among language models, 2025
