Safer alternative AI designs (non-agentic and bounded systems)

Build a different kind of AI that is safe by design, such as a predictor with no goals of its own, instead of aligning goal-pursuing agents.

Open in the compendium

This approach builds AI on a different design that is meant to be safe by construction, instead of training a goal-pursuing agent and then trying to align it. One example is Scientist AI, a system meant to predict and explain the world without goals of its own. Supporters argue such systems could deliver much of the benefit of advanced AI with less risk of deception or loss of control. Critics argue they are easily turned into agents and will lose out to agents on usefulness.

How it works

In February 2025 Yoshua Bengio and 12 co-authors proposed Scientist AI. It has two parts: a world model that generates theories to explain data, and a question-answering 'inference machine'. Both state their uncertainty explicitly. The system is meant to explain the world from observations, not to act in it. Used as a guardrail, LawZero writes, it could answer questions such as 'Will executing this source code pose a security risk?'

LawZero, the nonprofit that now builds it, says agency rests on three things, each a matter of degree: affordances (what a system can do in the world), goal-directedness and intelligence. The design limits the first two and pushes the third. Two choices do the work. First, training text is 'contextualized': the claim "Newton has an apple" is kept apart from "Newton said 'I have an apple' in 1666", so goals written in the data are treated as evidence to explain, not as drives to adopt. Second, training never rewards the system for the effects its predictions have once used.

Other designs share the aim. Conjecture's Cognitive Emulation (proposed in February 2023) aimed to build systems that reason in human-like ways from understandable parts, keeping opaque learned components to a minimum, so that capabilities stayed within a human-like range. Anthony Aguirre's essay Keep the Future Human (March 2025) argues for closing the gates to smarter-than-human, autonomous, general-purpose AI and building 'Tool AI' instead: powerful, trustworthy AI tools. Eric Drexler's earlier Comprehensive AI Services model (2019) treats advanced AI as a system of task-focused services rather than one unified agent.

Where research stands

The approach is early. LawZero, launched in Montréal on 3 June 2025 with what it described as over 15 researchers, is the best-funded of the efforts described here. Its launch announcement lists early donors including the Future of Life Institute, Jaan Tallinn, Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving) and Schmidt Sciences. In July 2026 LawZero announced a donation of computing resources from NVIDIA. On 16 September 2026 Canada and Germany announced plans to invest CAD 150 million and EUR 100 million in LawZero (up to CAD $300 million in LawZero's figure; Germany's share is subject to European Commission notification), to grow its team, open an office in Germany and build dedicated computing in Canada. Its published work so far consists of papers, including the 2025 proposal, a non-technical overview (February 2026) and a formal safety argument (July 2026). That argument proves that, under stated assumptions about training and about how rare dangerous predictors are, a trained predictor is unlikely to carry harm above a set threshold when deployed behind its guardrail. LawZero's research page lists papers, not a released system.

Conjecture launched Cognitive Emulation in February 2023 but said it would share few technical details while others raced to build general AI. In a March 2026 retrospective, Connor Leahy wrote that Conjecture focused on the agenda in 2024, pivoted from research to product work in 2025, and was drawing to a close; Cognitive Emulation is no longer an active safety research agenda. The idea of 'tool' or non-agentic AI is older: it was debated in 2012, and in 2016 Gwern argued that market pressure would push tools to become agents. Two questions remain open: whether a system can stay non-agentic once wrapped in software that acts on its answers, and whether it can be useful enough that companies choose it over agents.

Key results

  • Proposes Scientist AI, a non-agentic system made of a theory-generating world model and a question-answering inference machine, as a safer path than generalist agents and as a guardrail against agents. Yoshua Bengio, Michael Cohen, Damiano Fornasiere, Joumana Ghosn and 9 others, 2025
  • Argues agency rests on affordances, goal-directedness and intelligence, and names two design ingredients: contextualizing training data so facts are separated from statements about them, and 'consequence invariance', which blocks feedback from downstream outcomes. Damiano Fornasiere, Oliver Richardson, Gaël Gendron, Iulian Serban, Yoshua Bengio (LawZero), 2026
  • Gives a formal safety argument that, under assumptions on training dynamics and on how rare dangerous predictors are, a predictor trained toward a Bayesian posterior is unlikely to be dangerous when deployed behind a guardrail, because a dangerous one would have to underestimate harm in a coordinated way across many queries. Yoshua Bengio, Oliver Richardson, Tomáš Gavenčiak, Michael Cohen and 12 others, 2026
  • Sets out Cognitive Emulation: build 'predictably boundable systems, not directly aligned AGIs', by emulating human-like reasoning and keeping uninterpretable components to a minimum. Connor Leahy, Gabriel Alfour (Conjecture), 2023
  • Argues for closing the gates to smarter-than-human, autonomous, general-purpose AI and focusing instead on powerful, trustworthy AI tools. Anthony Aguirre, 2025
  • Looking back in July 2023, Drexler judged that his 2019 model of AI as composed services fits the large-language-model era 'quite well', though the technology itself diverged from what he expected. Eric Drexler, 2023

Open problems

  • How to keep a non-agentic predictor safe once software uses its answers to take actions; LawZero's guarantees concern agency arising inside the predictor itself, and its paper notes the predictor can still be used as part of an agentic system. source
  • Whether a system that only observes, and never experiments, can learn correct causal models of the world. source
  • Whether non-agentic or bounded systems can be useful enough that developers choose them over more capable agents. source
  • How to train on human text without the model taking on the goals of the people who wrote it; LawZero's answer is to contextualize the data. source

Criticisms

  • Tool AIs will be less intelligent and less economically valuable than agents that act and learn on their own, so users of agents will outcompete them and tools will tend to become agents. Gwern Branwen (2016, revised 2018)
  • A planning oracle sounds much less safe once its goal is written as maths rather than plain English, and building one needs much of the same work as building an aligned agent. Eliezer Yudkowsky (June 2012, replying to Holden Karnofsky)
  • A Scientist AI asked how to solve a hard problem may answer with a plan that involves building an agent; and a system kept from exploring will fall behind labs whose AI can. Matthew Khoriaty (LessWrong, May 2026)

Status

Early research, newly well funded. LawZero launched in June 2025 with what it said were over 15 researchers, and on 16 September 2026 Canada and Germany announced plans to invest CAD 150 million and EUR 100 million in it (up to CAD $300 million by LawZero's count). Its output so far is papers, including a formal safety argument (July 2026), not a released system.

Start reading

  • The Scientist AI: Safe by Design, by Not Desiring, Damiano Fornasiere, Oliver Richardson, Gaël Gendron, Iulian Serban, Yoshua Bengio (2026). LawZero's own non-technical overview of the design.
  • Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks: Can Scientist AI Offer a Safer Path?, Yoshua Bengio et al. (2025). The full case against agentic AI and for a non-agentic alternative.
  • Cognitive Emulation: A Naive AI Safety Proposal, Connor Leahy, Gabriel Alfour (2023). Short statement of a different safe-by-design route, bounded human-like systems; Conjecture's agenda until its 2025 pivot to products.
  • Why Tool AIs Want to Be Agent AIs, Gwern Branwen (2016). Argues that competition pushes non-agentic designs to become agents.
  • "Reframing Superintelligence" + LLMs + 4 years, Eric Drexler (2023). A readable summary of the services model, updated for language models.
  • Keep the Future Human, Anthony Aguirre (2025). Pairs 'Tool AI' with policy limits on autonomous general AI.

Related approaches

  • Guaranteed-safe AI and formal verification another safe-by-design route, by proof
  • Mild optimisation / satisficing limits optimisation pressure instead
  • Mesa-optimisation (inner vs outer objective) goals arising from training
  • AI control (restrict and monitor an untrusted model) assumes agents; Scientist AI as monitor
  • Input and output classifiers (guardrails) guardrails on agents
  • Calibration and honest expression of uncertainty calibrated, honest predictions
  • Halting frontier AI development Tool AI paired with limits on AGI

In their own words

Accordingly, we propose as a core building block for further advances the development of a non-agentic AI system that is trustworthy and safe by design, which we call Scientist AI. This system is designed to explain the world from observations, as opposed to taking actions in it to imitate or please humans.

Yoshua Bengio, Michael Cohen, Damiano Fornasiere, Joumana Ghosn, Pietro Greiner, Matt MacDermott, Sören Mindermann, Adam Oberman, Jesse Richardson, Oliver Richardson, Marc-Antoine Rondeau, Pierre-Luc St-Charles, David Williams-King, Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks: Can Scientist AI Offer a Safer Path?, 2025

The goal of the CoEm agenda is to build predictably boundable systems, not directly aligned AGIs. ... We consider the current paradigm of developing AIs that are as general and as powerful as possible, as quickly as possible, to be intrinsically dangerous, and we focus on designing bounded AIs as a safer alternative to it.

Connor Leahy, Gabriel Alfour (Conjecture), Cognitive Emulation: A Naive AI Safety Proposal, 2023
