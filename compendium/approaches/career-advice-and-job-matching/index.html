Building the field

Field-building

Career advice and job matching

Give people free career research, one-on-one advice and curated job listings that steer them into AI safety roles.

Open in the compendium

Career advice and job matching helps people find AI safety work that fits them. Nonprofits publish free career guides, hold one-on-one advising calls, introduce people to hiring managers and keep curated job boards. It matters because many people who could do AI safety work are in other careers, and most of them need help to see which roles exist and which suit them.

How it works

80,000 Hours, a UK nonprofit founded in 2011, publishes a career guide and reviews of career paths, runs a podcast and a job board, and holds free one-on-one advising calls. Its advisers help a person review options, introduce them to experts and hiring managers, and suggest next steps such as specific openings. Demand is larger than capacity. 80,000 Hours uses an automated system to help screen advising applications, with criteria built from over 20,000 hand-reviewed applications and over 6,000 calls, and it offers a free AI chatbot adviser to anyone. In April 2025 it narrowed its strategy to helping people work on the transition to artificial general intelligence (AGI), meaning AI that can match people at most tasks. Its job board lists about 5,000 roles a year and is being reoriented toward roles focused on making AGI go well.

Other services fill gaps. Successif gives free advising to experienced professionals: feedback on applications, mock interviews, workshops and direct introductions to AI risk organisations. Probably Good offers a career guide, a job board and advising across several cause areas, including a profile of AI safety and governance careers. The Horizon Institute for Public Service runs the Horizon Career Accelerator, a part-time remote programme of mentoring and application coaching for US AI policy careers. AISafety.com keeps a separate AI safety jobs board that includes non-technical roles. Funders also pay for the move itself: BlueDot Impact offers Career Transition Grants, and Coefficient Giving funds graduate study, unpaid internships and career-transition periods for people moving into work on global catastrophic risks.

Newer work routes candidates between organisations. In 2026 Kairos began piloting Talent Commons, a consent-first database in which fieldbuilding organisations share information about programme participants and job applicants. 80,000 Hours also restarted a 'sourcing' service in 2023 that sends hiring managers lists of promising candidates; that service overlaps with senior headhunting, but the core of this approach is open advice for anyone at any career stage.

Where research stands

The approach is established and well funded, but its effects are measured mostly by what people report. 80,000 Hours says that by early 2026 it had given one-on-one advice to over 7,500 people, and that over 3,000 people had told it they significantly changed their career plans because of its work. Applications for advising rose from 909 in 2020 to 9,444 in 2024. It holds about 1,400 calls a year and has raised its acceptance bar, which cut calls by about 20%. Its job board drew 961,519 clicks on vacancies in 2024, and it knows of 142 people who landed opportunities found there that year. Across all programmes it spent about $10.4 million in 2024. Coefficient Giving, its largest donor, has given it over £20 million since 2017.

Independent evidence is thin. In a 2020 Open Philanthropy survey of people it selected for their high expected impact on longtermist work, 80,000 Hours was among the few influences that scored as most impactful across several measures; Open Philanthropy chose the respondents, so they were not a random sample. The limiting factor has also moved. In interviews in late 2025, AI safety employers told MATS that they lack senior staff to supervise newcomers and so hire very selectively. More advice and more applicants do not by themselves create more roles.

Key results

  • 80,000 Hours says that by early 2026 it had advised over 7,500 people one-on-one, and that over 3,000 people told it its work led them to significantly change their career plans. 80,000 Hours, 2026
  • 80,000 Hours reports that applications for its advising rose about tenfold, from 909 in 2020 to 9,444 in 2024, while calls stayed between about 1,300 and 1,500 a year from 2022 to 2024. 80,000 Hours, 2025
  • 80,000 Hours reports that its job board lists about 5,000 roles a year and drew 961,519 vacancy clicks in 2024, and that it knows of 142 people who landed opportunities through it that year, a count it says may be too low by up to three times. 80,000 Hours, 2025
  • 80,000 Hours reports that since restarting candidate sourcing in 2023 it has run 491 searches for hiring managers and learned of 24 placements where the search made some difference. 80,000 Hours, 2025
  • 80,000 Hours says that of the thousands of people it has advised, 95% would recommend the service to a friend and 76% said they changed their career plans to something higher impact. 80,000 Hours, 2026
  • In a 2020 survey of people Open Philanthropy selected for their high expected impact on longtermist work, 80,000 Hours was among the influences that scored as most impactful across several measures, along with the Centre for Effective Altruism, local and university EA groups and a few others. Eli Rose (Open Philanthropy), 2021

Open problems

  • Knowing what advice really changes: most impact figures are self-reported plan changes, and 80,000 Hours says its job-board placement count may be off by up to three times. source
  • Matching applicants to a field whose employers lack the senior staff to absorb and train them. source
  • Deciding which roles to list, especially safety-related jobs at frontier AI companies, where a listing can read as an endorsement. source
  • Reaching promising people cheaply: 80,000 Hours spent $4.8 million on ads and influencer marketing over 2.5 years and found they attracted its most promising users less cost-effectively than hoped. source
  • Serving generalists and mid-career professionals as well as would-be researchers; Kairos argued in April 2026 that generalists had become the field's biggest talent gap. source

Criticisms

  • Listing OpenAI roles on the job board he sees as the largest funnel of EA talent sends people into a company that broke its safety commitments; 80,000 Hours should delist it, and he would drop Anthropic roles too, though he calls that case less clear. Raemon (LessWrong, July 2024)
  • Making the near arrival of AGI the background assumption of its advice cuts off other views of how to do the most good, and weakens a main entry point to effective altruism. James Brobin (EA Forum, October 2026)
  • Steering careers can backfire: people may be pushed into less useful or even harmful work, and some critics think the field's impact so far has been mostly negative, for example by speeding AI progress. Aaron Gertler (80,000 Hours), summarising the risks and the critics' view, April 2026

  • Successif: Free advising, application feedback, workshops and introductions to AI risk organisations for experienced professionals. Build an Impactful Career in AI Risk Reduction (2026)

  • AISafety.com: Keeps an AI safety jobs board that includes non-technical roles. AI safety jobs (2026)
  • BlueDot Impact: Career Transition Grants fund moves into full-time AI safety work; says hiring managers at major AI companies and governments recruit from its community. BlueDot Impact (home page) (2026)
  • Kairos: Piloting Talent Commons, a consent-first database for fieldbuilding organisations to share and route promising candidates. Kairos has raised $50M to build talent infrastructure for AI safety (and we're hiring!) (2026)
  • Coefficient Giving: Largest donor to 80,000 Hours (over £20 million since 2017); also funds individuals' career development and transition periods. Global Catastrophic Risks Opportunities (Career Development and Transition Funding) (2026)

Status

Established: 80,000 Hours says it had advised over 7,500 people by early 2026, and it lists about 5,000 roles a year; evidence of impact is mostly self-reported plan changes.

Related approaches

  • Senior and specialised talent recruitment headhunting senior specialists; 80,000 Hours' sourcing overlaps
  • Public communication and content creation 80,000 Hours' podcast and videos bring people in
  • Grantmaking and regranting grants pay for career transitions
  • Founding new organisations on unowned problems advisees who go on to found organisations
  • Frontier-lab internal policy and safety culture whether to list lab roles turns on how labs keep their safety commitments
  • Research training and upskilling programmes advice and job boards steer people into training programmes
  • Shared offices, hubs, convenings and forums hubs and events are where many advisees meet employers
