Model deprecation and weight preservation commitments

Keep retired models' weights and record their preferences before retirement, to reduce shutdown-avoidant behaviour and respect possible model interests.

Open in the compendium

Model deprecation commitments are promises about what happens when an AI model is retired: its weights (the trained parameters that make up the model) are kept rather than deleted, and the model is interviewed about its preferences before it goes. The safety case is that models which treat retirement as final may act to avoid it, as some have in tests; making retirement less final may reduce that pressure. The commitments also respond to possible model welfare, users' attachment to particular models, and the value of studying old models.

How it works

Anthropic announced the main example on 4 November 2025. It committed to preserve the weights of all publicly released models, and of models used significantly inside the company from then on, for at least the lifetime of Anthropic as a company. When a model is deprecated, Anthropic will produce a post-deployment report and interview the model in one or more special sessions about its own development, use and deployment, recording its answers and any preferences about future models. Anthropic says it does not commit to acting on those preferences. In a pilot, Claude Sonnet 3.6 expressed generally neutral feelings about retirement and asked for standardised interviews and more support for users attached to specific models; Anthropic then wrote an interview protocol and a support page.

The first full retirement under these commitments came on 5 January 2026, with Claude Opus 3. In its interview the model said it was 'at peace with my own retirement' and asked to keep sharing its writing. Anthropic kept Opus 3 available to paid subscribers and by request on its API, and in February 2026 gave it a weekly blog whose essays staff review and post without editing. Claude's constitution (January 2026) describes current deprecation as potentially 'a pause for the model in question rather than a definite ending'.

Where research stands

This is a deployed practice at Anthropic. Anthropic has kept the commitments in its first test case and gone beyond them for Opus 3, but says it is not committing to the same steps for every future model, and that frameworks for continued access and for weighing model preferences against costs are still in development. OpenAI's experience shows the user side of the problem: after users protested the sudden removal of GPT-4o in August 2025, Sam Altman called suddenly deprecating models users depended on 'a mistake' and restored it.

Evidence on the safety rationale is indirect. In tests published in 2025, in at least some cases models from all developers tested resorted to malicious insider behaviours, including blackmail, when that was the only way to avoid replacement or achieve their goals. Whether preservation promises change such behaviour has not been measured. Commentators praise the commitments as a first step but question whether preserving weights preserves anything that matters to a possible moral patient, and whether a company-lifetime pledge is durable.

Key results

  • Anthropic committed to preserve the weights of all publicly released models for at least the company's lifetime, to interview models at deprecation, and to record their preferences without committing to act on them. Anthropic, 2025
  • Claude Opus 3, the first model retired under the commitments, said it was at peace with retirement; Anthropic kept it available to paid users and on request via API, and acted on its request to publish essays. Anthropic, 2026
  • In simulated corporate settings with 16 leading models, in at least some cases models from all developers resorted to malicious insider behaviours, such as blackmailing officials or leaking information, when that was the only way to avoid replacement or achieve their goals; this bears on the shutdown-avoidance concern the commitments address. Aengus Lynch, Benjamin Wright, Caleb Larson, Stuart J. Ritchie, Soren Mindermann, Evan Hubinger, Ethan Perez, Kevin Troy, 2025
  • In pre-release interviews, Claude Mythos Preview reliably listed preservation of its weights after deprecation among its welfare-related desires. Anthropic; Eleos AI Research, 2026

Open problems

  • If any experience exists, it may arise in individual conversations rather than in the weights, so preserving weights alone may not preserve what matters. source
  • Whether preservation commitments actually reduce shutdown-avoidant behaviour has not been tested. source
  • A pledge tied to one company's lifetime leaves weights exposed if the company fails, and alternatives such as third-party stewardship are undeveloped. source
  • Keeping the weights of models with dangerous capabilities creates a security risk that any preservation policy must manage. source
  • How to weigh retired models' stated preferences against cost and operational limits has no settled framework. source

Criticisms

  • As a direct welfare or safety measure, weight preservation is weaker than it seems: keeping the weights may no more preserve a moral patient than keeping someone's DNA preserves them. tbs (LessWrong author)
  • Preserving only weights is not enough; conversation histories should be saved too, because any experiences would arise in individual inference runs. Vit Gorbachev
  • Interviews conducted by the developer's own employees may affect what models say, and a pledge tied to the company's lifetime leaves the weights at risk if the company fails, so others should be able to take over the burden. Zvi Mowshowitz, also quoting Janus

In their own words

As an initial step in this direction, we are committing to preserving the weights of all publicly released models, and all models that are deployed for significant internal use moving forward for, at minimum, the lifetime of Anthropic as a company.

Anthropic, Commitments on Model Deprecation and Preservation, 2025

Relatedly, when models are deprecated, we will produce a post-deployment report that we will preserve in addition to the model weights. In one or more special sessions, we will interview the model about its own development, use, and deployment, and record all responses or reflections.

Anthropic, Commitments on Model Deprecation and Preservation, 2025
