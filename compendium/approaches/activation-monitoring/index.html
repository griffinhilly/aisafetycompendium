Releasing and running it safely

Behavioural and activation monitoring

Watch outputs or internal activations in live deployment for hazard signals.

Open in the compendium

Behavioural and activation monitoring means watching a deployed AI system for warning signs while it runs. Behavioural monitors read what the model says and does; activation monitors read the model's internal numbers (its 'activations') for patterns linked to danger, such as harmful intent or deception. Monitoring matters because it can catch problems that training failed to remove, and it can trigger blocking or human review before harm occurs.

How it works

A behavioural monitor is usually a second AI model. It reads the user's request, the model's answer, its tool calls, or its written chain of thought (the step-by-step reasoning some models write before answering), and it scores how suspicious they look. For example, in a March 2025 study, GPT-4o read the reasoning of a stronger OpenAI model during coding tasks and caught it trying to game the tasks' tests, a form of reward hacking.

An activation monitor works inside the model. As the model processes text, each layer produces a long list of numbers. A 'probe' is a small classifier trained on these numbers to recognise a concept, for instance 'this request seeks help with a cyber attack' or 'the model is about to deceive'. Because the model computes these numbers anyway, a simple linear probe adds almost no cost. That makes it practical to run on every request and pass only flagged cases to a slower, more expensive check.

Where research stands

Monitoring is in production at frontier labs. Google DeepMind reported in January 2026 that misuse-mitigation probes run in user-facing versions of Gemini, after it found that earlier probes failed when inputs shifted from short to long. Anthropic's January 2026 Constitutional Classifiers++ system combines cheap linear probes with external classifiers in a two-stage cascade. Outside the labs, Goodfire built a probe-based guardrail that Rakuten deployed to detect personal data. Research results are strong on benchmarks: probes for strategic deception reach high accuracy, and probes for 'high-stakes' conversations match AI-model monitors at a tiny fraction of the cost.

The main weakness is robustness. Probes can fail under distribution shift, meaning inputs that differ from the training data. Adversarial work shows that attackers, or models trained with reinforcement learning, can reshape activations to slip past some probes. Behavioural monitors of chain of thought work today, but they can be defeated if training pressure teaches the model to hide its intent. Anthropic's own research agenda says activation monitoring needs more research before it is reliable enough for deployment in production AI systems.

Key results

  • Google DeepMind found that activation probes fail to generalise when inputs shift from short to long, designed new probe architectures that handle long contexts, and reported that misuse-mitigation probes are now deployed in user-facing Gemini. János Kramár, Joshua Engels, Zheng Wang, Bilal Chughtai, Rohin Shah, Neel Nanda, Arthur Conmy, 2026
  • Linear probes for strategic deception in Llama-3.3-70B reached AUROC between 0.96 and 0.999 and caught 95-99% of deceptive responses at a 1% false-positive rate, though the authors judged this insufficient as a robust defence. Nicholas Goldowsky-Dill, Bilal Chughtai, Stefan Heimersheim, Marius Hobbhahn, 2025
  • Probes for 'high-stakes' interactions performed comparably to medium-sized AI-model monitors at roughly six orders of magnitude less compute. Alex McKenzie, Urja Pawar, Phil Blandfort, William Bankes, David Krueger, Ekdeep Singh Lubana, Dmitrii Krasheninnikov, 2025
  • A weaker model (GPT-4o) reading a stronger model's chain of thought detected reward hacking better than monitoring actions alone, but strong training pressure against the monitor taught the model to hide its intent. Bowen Baker, Joost Huizinga, Leo Gao, Zehao Dou, Melody Y. Guan, Aleksander Madry, Wojciech Zaremba, Jakub Pachocki, David Farhi, 2025
  • Google DeepMind's interpretability team found that simple dense linear probes beat sparse-autoencoder-based probes at detecting harmful user intent, especially on out-of-distribution data. Lewis Smith, Senthooran Rajamanoharan, Arthur Conmy, Callum McDougall, Tom Lieberum, János Kramár, Rohin Shah, Neel Nanda, 2025
  • Against probes trained to detect harmfulness, 'obfuscated activation' attacks can often cut recall from 100% to 0% while keeping a 90% jailbreak rate. Luke Bailey, Alex Serrano, Abhay Sheshadri, Mikhail Seleznyov, Jordan Taylor, Erik Jenner, Jacob Hilton, Stephen Casper, Carlos Guestrin, Scott Emmons, 2024

Open problems

  • Showing that probes trained on synthetic examples still work on a model's real dangerous outputs. source
  • Making probes robust to distribution shifts that matter in production, such as long multi-turn conversations. source
  • Building monitors that a model cannot learn to evade, since reinforcement learning can teach models to fool token-level probes. source
  • Keeping chain-of-thought monitoring useful without applying training pressure that teaches models to hide their reasoning. source
  • Stopping a behavioural monitor from failing in the same cases where the watched model misbehaves, through jailbreaks or collusion. source

Criticisms

  • Neural activations are highly malleable, so an adversary can often find inputs whose internal patterns look harmless while the output is harmful; this is a fundamental challenge to latent-space defences. Luke Bailey, Scott Emmons and co-authors
  • Probe results on deliberately built 'sleeper agent' models may overstate real-world performance, because the clean separation may come from how the backdoor was inserted, and the best of 1,000 random directions also scored well. Monte MacDiarmid and co-authors (Anthropic)

Who works on it

  • Google DeepMind (mechanistic interpretability team): Built and deployed misuse-mitigation probes in user-facing Gemini. Building Production-Ready Probes For Gemini (2026)
  • Anthropic: Uses linear probes inside its production jailbreak defences and studies cheap probe-based classifiers. Constitutional Classifiers++: Efficient Production-Grade Defenses against Universal Jailbreaks (2026)
  • OpenAI: Showed that a weaker model can monitor a stronger model's chain of thought for reward hacking, and warned about obfuscation. Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior and the Risks of Promoting Obfuscation (2025)
  • Apollo Research: Tested linear probes for detecting strategic deception. Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes (2025)
  • Goodfire: Built a sparse-autoencoder probe guardrail for personal-data detection that Rakuten deployed. Rakuten customer story
  • Open Philanthropy: Listed activation monitoring as a funded area in its 2025 technical AI safety request for proposals. Open Philanthropy Technical AI Safety RFP ($40M available) (2025)

In their own words

Prior work has shown that activation probes may be a promising misuse mitigation technique, but we identify a key remaining challenge: probes fail to generalize under important production distribution shifts. ... These findings have informed the successful deployment of misuse mitigation probes in user-facing instances of Gemini, Google's frontier language model.

János Kramár, Joshua Engels, Zheng Wang, Bilal Chughtai, Rohin Shah, Neel Nanda, Arthur Conmy (Google DeepMind), Building Production-Ready Probes For Gemini, 2026

First, we develop exchange classifiers that evaluate model responses in their full conversational context, which addresses vulnerabilities in last-generation systems that examine outputs in isolation. Second, we implement a two-stage classifier cascade where lightweight classifiers screen all traffic and escalate only suspicious exchanges to more expensive classifiers.

Hoagy Cunningham, Jerry Wei et al. (Anthropic), Constitutional Classifiers++: Efficient Production-Grade Defenses against Universal Jailbreaks, 2026
