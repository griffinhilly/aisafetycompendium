Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems)

Give AI systems unique IDs so their actions can be traced to the system and the party responsible.

Open in the compendium

Agent identity and accountability means giving AI systems, especially autonomous agents, identifiers that let others see which system acted and who is responsible for it. Without IDs, a website, a bank or an investigator may not know whether it is dealing with an AI, which model it is, or whom to contact to stop it. IDs matter for safety because accountability, incident investigation and shutdown all depend on being able to trace an action back to a system and a person or company.

How it works

In the June 2024 proposal 'IDs for AI Systems', an ID is attached to an instance of a system, such as one chat session or one running agent. The ID links to information that others can look up: which model it is, who deployed it, what safety certifications it has, and its record of incidents. The authors compare this to an aircraft's tail number, which is linked to that aircraft's incident and maintenance history. They argue IDs matter most where agents have large effects, such as making payments or contacting real people.

In practice, agent identity is being built on existing web and enterprise security standards. For example, an agent can cryptographically sign each web request so that the receiving site can check who sent it. Cloudflare launched a 'signed agents' category on this basis in August 2025, listing OpenAI's ChatGPT agent among the first. Inside companies, identity systems such as Microsoft Entra Agent ID give each agent its own identity, with role-based permissions and tracking of what it accesses. A related proposal extends the OAuth and OpenID Connect login standards so a person can delegate limited, auditable authority to an agent.

Where research stands

The idea has moved from research proposal to early deployment in under two years. Academic work, much of it led by Alan Chan with co-authors at the Centre for the Governance of AI, set out the case for agent identifiers, real-time monitoring and activity logs (January 2024), IDs for AI systems (June 2024), and a broader 'agent infrastructure' agenda (January 2025). Industry then shipped identity products: Microsoft Entra Agent ID entered public preview in May 2025, and Cloudflare began verifying signed agents in August 2025. Standards bodies are engaging. The OpenID Foundation published a whitepaper on agent identity in October 2025, and the US National Institute of Standards and Technology's National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence sought public comment on an agent identity and authorisation project from February to April 2026.

What remains unsolved: current standards work for simple agents inside one organisation, but not yet for agents that delegate to other agents or act across many organisations. An ID or signature shows which system acted, not whether it acted well. Decentralised and open-source agents that no single company runs are hard to bring into any ID scheme. The tension between accountability and privacy is unresolved.

Key results

  • The paper proposed IDs for individual instances of AI systems, linked to information such as certifications and responsible parties, and judged them most warranted where systems could have large impact, such as financial transactions or contacting real people. Alan Chan, Noam Kolt, Peter Wills, Usman Anwar, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Nitarshan Rajkumar, Lewis Hammond, David Krueger, Lennart Heim, Markus Anderljung, 2024
  • Agent identifiers, real-time monitoring and activity logging were set out as three complementary 'visibility' measures, each with options that range in intrusiveness, alongside privacy and concentration-of-power concerns. Alan Chan, Carson Ezell, Max Kaufmann et al., 2024
  • Agent infrastructure was framed as having three functions: attribution of actions to agents and their users, shaping interactions between agents, and detecting and remedying harm. Alan Chan, Kevin Wei, Sihao Huang, Nitarshan Rajkumar, Elija Perrier, Seth Lazar, Gillian K. Hadfield, Markus Anderljung, 2025
  • A framework extending OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect lets people delegate scoped, auditable authority to agents while keeping a clear chain of accountability. Tobin South, Samuele Marro, Thomas Hardjono, Robert Mahari, Cedric Deslandes Whitney, Dazza Greenwood, Alan Chan, Alex Pentland, 2025
  • Cloudflare introduced a 'signed agents' category in August 2025, in which an agent's platform cryptographically signs its web requests so sites can verify it; early listed agents included OpenAI's ChatGPT agent and Block's Goose. Cloudflare, 2025
  • The OpenID Foundation found that current login and authorisation standards work for simple single-domain agents but have gaps for recursive delegation and cross-domain trust, and recommended managing agents as first-class identities. OpenID Foundation (Artificial Intelligence Identity Management Community Group), 2025

Open problems

  • Handling chains of delegation, where one agent hands work to another across organisations. source
  • Keeping agents accountable without making every user's activity linkable and identifiable. source
  • Getting adoption by decentralised and open-source agents that no single deployer controls. source

Criticisms

  • Central verification of personal AI agents would require access to users' most private information and could become mass-surveillance infrastructure, especially in authoritarian states. Andrey Seryakov
  • A national design that hides an agent's owner from businesses still lets a state that can compel both key agencies re-identify anyone, because the protection is procedural rather than cryptographic. Yifan He, Zhiguang Shan, Le Luo, Wei Wang

Who works on it

  • Centre for the Governance of AI: Published the core research proposals on IDs and visibility for AI agents. IDs for AI Systems (2024)
  • National Institute of Standards and Technology (National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence): Sought public input on a project applying identity standards to AI agents. Accelerating the Adoption of Software and Artificial Intelligence Agent Identity and Authorization (concept paper) (2026)
  • OpenID Foundation: Its Artificial Intelligence Identity Management Community Group published a whitepaper on agent identity gaps and standards. Identity Management for Agentic AI (2025)
  • Cloudflare: Verifies cryptographically signed AI agent traffic through its signed-agents programme. The age of agents: cryptographically recognizing agent traffic (2025)
  • Microsoft: Launched Entra Agent ID to give enterprise AI agents their own managed identities. Microsoft Introduces Entra Agent ID to Bring Zero Trust to AI Agents (The Letter Two) (2025)

Status

Research proposal (2024 paper), now in early deployment and standards work. Microsoft released Entra Agent ID in public preview in May 2025, and Cloudflare began recognizing cryptographically signed agents in August 2025. In February 2026 NIST's National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence released a concept paper on identity and authorization for software and AI agents.

In their own words

AI systems are increasingly pervasive, yet information needed to decide whether and how to engage with them may not exist or be accessible. ... An investigator may not know whom to investigate when a system causes an incident. It may not be clear whom to contact to shut down a malfunctioning system.

Alan Chan, Noam Kolt, Peter Wills, Usman Anwar, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Nitarshan Rajkumar, Lewis Hammond, David Krueger, Lennart Heim, Markus Anderljung, IDs for AI Systems, 2024

We propose a framework in which IDs are ascribed to instances of AI systems (e.g., a particular chat session with Claude 3), and associated information is accessible to parties seeking to interact with that system.

Alan Chan, Noam Kolt, Peter Wills, Usman Anwar, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Nitarshan Rajkumar, Lewis Hammond, David Krueger, Lennart Heim, Markus Anderljung, IDs for AI Systems, 2024
