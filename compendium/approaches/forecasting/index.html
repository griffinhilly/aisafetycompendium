Building the field

Field-building

Forecasting

Predict capability timelines as an input to prioritisation.

Open in the compendium

AI forecasting tries to predict when AI systems will reach key capabilities, how fast progress will be, and what the effects and risks will be. Safety researchers, funders and governments use these forecasts to decide what to work on first and how urgently. If transformative AI is likely within a few years, work that pays off quickly matters more than slow foundational research, and preparation for a sudden 'takeoff' (a rapid jump in capability) becomes more pressing.

How it works

Forecasters use three main methods. Trend extrapolation measures something over time and projects it forward: Epoch AI found that the compute used to train frontier models grew four to five times a year from 2010 to May 2024, and METR found that the length of tasks AI agents can complete (measured by how long the task takes a human expert) has doubled about every seven months since 2019. Expert surveys ask researchers directly: AI Impacts surveyed 2,778 published AI researchers in late 2023. Forecasting tournaments and prediction markets pool many people's judgments and score them when questions resolve; the Forecasting Research Institute's Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (XPT) in 2022 had domain experts and 'superforecasters' (people with strong records on short-range questions) predict AI and other risks, then argue with each other.

Some groups combine these into explicit models or scenarios. AI 2027, published in April 2025 by the AI Futures Project, is a month-by-month scenario backed by timelines and takeoff models, written to be concrete enough to check and argue with. Epoch's 2023 literature review sorts such work into 'model-based' forecasts (the output of an explicit model) and 'judgment-based' forecasts (aggregates of predictions or the views of notable individuals).

Where research stands

Forecasting is an established part of the field, with dedicated organisations, a strategy and forecasting research stream at MATS, and philanthropic funding (Coefficient Giving's Forecasting Fund gave more than $50 million across 30+ grants before it closed in March 2026; Coefficient still makes forecasting grants through other funds). Surveys show fast-moving expectations: in AI Impacts' 2023 survey the 50% date for machines outperforming humans at every task moved 13 years earlier, to 2047, than in the same survey a year before.

The track record is mixed. When the first 38 XPT questions resolved, both experts and superforecasters had underestimated AI progress, and superforecasters gave only 2.3% to AI reaching gold-medal level at the International Mathematical Olympiad by 2025, which happened in July 2025. Individual forecasters did not beat simple 'no change' or trend-extrapolation rules, though pooled forecasts did better. Short-run accuracy did not predict anyone's views on long-run risk, so good scores on near-term questions say little about whose existential-risk estimates to trust. Explicit timeline models also draw heavy methodological criticism.

Key results

  • Frontier AI models' 50% task-completion time horizon (the length of task, timed on human experts, that models finish half the time) has doubled about every seven months since 2019. Thomas Kwa, Ben West, Joel Becker, et al. (METR), 2025
  • In a survey of 2,778 AI researchers, the aggregate forecast put a 10% chance on machines outperforming humans at every task by 2027 and 50% by 2047, 13 years earlier than the previous year's survey. Katja Grace, Harlan Stewart, Julia Fabienne Sandkühler, Stephen Thomas, Ben Weinstein-Raun, Jan Brauner, Richard C. Korzekwa, 2024
  • Training compute of frontier AI models grew four to five times per year from 2010 to May 2024. Jaime Sevilla, Edu Roldán (Epoch AI), 2024
  • Experts and superforecasters both underestimated AI benchmark progress (average probabilities of 24.6% and 9.7% for what happened), and individual forecasters failed to beat simple no-change and trend-extrapolation rules. Forecasting Research Institute, 2025
  • Experts and superforecasters in the 2022 tournament showed large disagreement and almost no convergence, with the largest disagreement over risks from AI. Ezra Karger, Josh Rosenberg, Zachary Jacobs, et al., 2023

Open problems

  • Finding ways to judge whose long-run AI risk forecasts deserve trust, since short-run accuracy did not track long-run views. source
  • Knowing whether benchmark trends such as task-length doubling carry over to real-world work. source
  • Modelling takeoff once AI speeds up AI research itself, where historical trends may break. source

Criticisms

  • AI 2027's timeline models rest on weak foundations: the 'superexponential' growth curve, to which forecasters gave about 40% weight, is a much more problematic part than the exponential one, the model has very little empirical validation, and several quite different stories fit the same sparse data. One of the model's authors agreed with some of the critiques and promised changes, but disagreed on methodological and philosophical matters. titotal
  • Biology-based timeline forecasts (comparing AI compute to the human brain or evolution) have a long history of failure, and the method is a 'trick that never works'. Eliezer Yudkowsky
  • Even skilled forecasters underestimated AI progress, individual forecasters did not beat simple no-change and trend-extrapolation rules, and short-run accuracy did not line up with any position on long-run risk. Forecasting Research Institute (own findings)

Who works on it

  • Epoch AI: Tracks trends in compute, data, models and benchmarks and reviews timeline forecasts. Literature review of transformative artificial intelligence timelines (2023)
  • AI Impacts: Runs a recurring survey of AI researchers on timelines and risks; its 2023 survey was the largest of its kind. Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI (2024)
  • Forecasting Research Institute: Runs forecasting tournaments with experts and superforecasters and scores their accuracy. Forecasting Existential Risks: Evidence from a Long-Run Forecasting Tournament (2023)
  • METR: Measures the length of tasks AI agents can complete, a widely used capability trend. Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks (2025)
  • AI Futures Project: Wrote the AI 2027 scenario with supporting timelines, takeoff and compute forecasts. AI 2027 (2025)
  • Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI): Surveyed AI and machine learning researchers on progress milestones and human-level machine intelligence. Forecasting AI Progress: Evidence from a Survey of Machine Learning Researchers (2022)
  • MATS: Runs a Strategy and Forecasting research stream covering timelines, takeoff dynamics and risk modelling. MATS (home page)
  • Coefficient Giving: Gave more than $50 million through its Forecasting Fund, which closed in March 2026, and still funds forecasting through other funds such as Navigating Transformative AI. Forecasting (Closed)

In their own words

AI Impacts aims to improve our understanding of the likely impacts of human-level artificial intelligence. ... More credible and clearly communicated views on these issues might help improve estimates of the social returns to AI investment, identify neglected research areas, improve policy, or productively channel public interest in AI.

AI Impacts, About AI Impacts

Over the last few years, we have seen many attempts to quantitatively forecast the arrival of transformative and/or general Artificial Intelligence (TAI/AGI) using very different methodologies and assumptions. ... Model-based timelines are given by the output of an explicit model. In contrast, judgment-based timelines are either aggregates of group predictions on, e.g., prediction markets, or the timelines of some notable individuals.

Keith Wynroe, David Atkinson and Jaime Sevilla (Epoch AI), Literature review of transformative artificial intelligence timelines, 2023
