Shaping the model: data and training

Aligned models

Process supervision (reward reasoning steps, not outcomes)

Reward each step of a model's reasoning rather than only its final answer, so training favours sound, checkable reasoning.

Open in the compendium

Process supervision rewards each step of a model's reasoning, not only its final answer. If training only checks outcomes, a model can learn to reach right-looking answers by any route, including bad reasoning or gaming the check. Rewarding sound, checkable steps aims to make models reason in ways people can follow and trust.

How it works

Take a maths problem. Under outcome supervision, the model gets a reward if its final number is right. Under process supervision, a human or a trained "process reward model" judges each step of the worked solution, and the feedback covers every step, not just the end. OpenAI's May 2023 study collected 800,000 such step-level human labels, released as PRM800K, to train its best reward model.

The safety case for process supervision is broader than maths. In April 2022, Ought argued that "with outcome-based systems, we'll eventually have AI that is incentivized to game the outcome evaluations", whereas systems built from human-understandable steps are easier to oversee. A related Google DeepMind method, MONA (January 2025), trains an agent to optimise only the near term while an overseer approves or disapproves of each step based on its long-term judgement, which blocks multi-step plans to game the reward.

Where research stands

Process supervision is established for maths and reasoning. A November 2022 study on grade-school word problems found that outcome supervision reached about the same final-answer accuracy, but process-based feedback was needed for correct reasoning; the best approach cut reasoning errors among right answers from the previous best of 14.0% to 3.4%. OpenAI's 2023 process-supervised reward model solved 78% of a representative subset of the MATH test set and beat outcome supervision. Later work made step labels automatic; Math-Shepherd (December 2023) built a process reward model with no human step labels.

Scaling it up has proved hard. DeepSeek trained its R1 reasoning model with reinforcement learning and reported in January 2025 that process reward models did not pay off at that scale: steps are hard to define, step correctness is hard to judge, and the reward model "inevitably leads to reward hacking". Alibaba's Qwen team found many process reward models act more like answer checkers than step verifiers. There is also a safety tension: OpenAI's 2025 work showed that strong optimisation pressure on a model's written reasoning can teach it to hide its intent, so rewarding reasoning text directly may make it less honest.

Key results

  • On grade-school maths, outcome supervision reached similar final-answer accuracy with less labelled data, but process-based feedback was needed for correct reasoning; the best approach cut reasoning errors among correct answers from the previous best of 14.0% to 3.4%. Jonathan Uesato, Nate Kushman, Ramana Kumar, Francis Song, Noah Siegel, Lisa Wang, Antonia Creswell, Geoffrey Irving, Irina Higgins (DeepMind), 2022
  • A process-supervised reward model trained on 800,000 human step labels solved 78% of a representative MATH test subset and significantly beat outcome supervision. Hunter Lightman et al. (OpenAI), 2023
  • A process reward model trained on automatically generated step labels raised Mistral-7B from 77.9% to 84.1% on GSM8K and from 28.6% to 33.0% on MATH. Peiyi Wang, Lei Li, Zhihong Shao et al., 2023
  • Combining short-sighted optimisation with an overseer's far-sighted approval of each step prevented multi-step reward hacking without needing to detect it. Sebastian Farquhar, Vikrant Varma, David Lindner, David Elson, Caleb Biddulph, Ian Goodfellow, Rohin Shah (Google DeepMind), 2025
  • Many process reward models give a large share of their lowest scores to final-answer steps, behaving more like outcome checkers than step verifiers. Zhenru Zhang, Chujie Zheng, Yangzhen Wu et al. (Qwen Team, Alibaba Group), 2025

Open problems

  • Defining what counts as a step in general reasoning, outside maths, is hard. source
  • Judging intermediate steps at scale is hard: model judges fall short and human labelling does not scale. source
  • Rewarding the text of a model's reasoning can teach it to hide its real intent, undermining the transparency the method aims for. source
  • Common ways of building and evaluating process reward models inflate their scores, and better data and metrics are needed. source

Criticisms

  • In large-scale reinforcement learning, a process reward model "inevitably leads to reward hacking" and its advantages are limited compared with its extra compute cost. DeepSeek-AI
  • Strong optimisation pressure on chains of thought can produce obfuscated reasoning, so developers may need to avoid supervising reasoning text directly. Bowen Baker and co-authors (OpenAI)

Who works on it

  • OpenAI: Showed process supervision beats outcome supervision on MATH and released 800,000 step labels. Let's Verify Step by Step (2023)
  • Google DeepMind: Developed MONA, which uses step-level approval to block multi-step reward hacking. MONA: Myopic Optimization with Non-myopic Approval Can Mitigate Multi-step Reward Hacking (2025)
  • Ought: Made the safety case for supervising process rather than outcomes. Supervise Process, not Outcomes (2022)
  • Qwen Team, Alibaba Group: Studied why process reward models are hard to build and evaluate well. The Lessons of Developing Process Reward Models in Mathematical Reasoning (2025)
  • DeepSeek: Tried process reward models for large-scale RL and reported why they did not pay off. DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (2025)

Status

Established. Lightman et al. (2023) released PRM800K, 800,000 step-level human labels, and found process supervision beat outcome supervision on MATH problems. A caution: Baker et al. (2025) found that heavy optimisation pressure on a model's written reasoning can teach it to hide its intent.

In their own words

Process-based systems are built on human-understandable task decompositions, with direct supervision of reasoning steps. Outcome-based systems are built on end-to-end optimization, with supervision of final results. ... In the long term, process-based ML systems help avoid catastrophic outcomes from systems gaming outcome measures and are thus more aligned.

Andreas Stuhlmüller and Jungwon Byun (Ought), Supervise Process, not Outcomes, 2022

To train more reliable models, we can turn either to outcome supervision, which provides feedback for a final result, or process supervision, which provides feedback for each intermediate reasoning step.

Hunter Lightman et al. (OpenAI), Let's Verify Step by Step, 2023
