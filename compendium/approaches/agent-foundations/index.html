Theory: the maths of agents and goals

Aligned models

Agent foundations

Formal theory of what an agent, a goal and rational choice even are.

Open in the compendium

Agent foundations is mathematical and philosophical work on basic questions: what an agent is, what it means to have a goal, and how a reasoner should decide. The bet is that we cannot reliably align powerful AI while these ideas are still fuzzy. The Machine Intelligence Research Institute made it its main focus from 2013, and its value is much debated.

How it works

Textbook models of a rational agent treat it as standing outside the world, like a video-game player looking at the screen. It has unlimited computing power, a complete model of its world, and goals that never change. A real AI is different. It runs on hardware inside the world it is reasoning about, it can be copied or modified, it cannot compute everything, and it may build helpers or successors. MIRI researchers Abram Demski and Scott Garrabrant called this "embedded agency" in 2018. They split it into four problems: how such an agent should decide, how it can model a world that contains itself, how it can hand tasks to successors it can trust, and how to keep its own internal parts from pursuing other goals.

Researchers attack these with proofs and toy models. One example is logical uncertainty: how to assign sensible probabilities to mathematical claims an agent has not yet worked out, such as whether a large number is prime. A second is decision theory, the study of how to choose; functional decision theory (2017) says an agent should treat its choice as the output of a fixed procedure that others might predict. A third is abstraction: why different minds tend to carve the world into the same concepts, such as "tree" or "temperature". If that convergence can be proved, humans might be able to check what concepts an AI is using.

The argument for doing this, in John Wentworth's March 2022 framing, is that simple proxies for what we want break under strong optimisation. Only concepts formulated robustly enough to withstand that pressure, which he calls "True Names", are likely to hold up.

Where research stands

The field has produced real mathematical results but few direct uses in today's machine learning. Logical induction (September 2016) gave a computable method that meets a strong standard for reasoning under logical uncertainty. Vanessa Kosoy's learning-theoretic agenda, built on infra-Bayesianism (a theory of reasoning when none of an agent's hypotheses is exactly right), published regret bounds at the Conference on Learning Theory (COLT) in 2025, a mainstream venue. In September 2025 John Wentworth and David Lorell gave conditions under which two agents' internal concepts can be translated into each other.

The field's institutional base has shifted. The Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) focused on this agenda from 2013. In January 2024 it said policy and communications would likely be a higher priority than research. Its Agent Foundations team, including Scott Garrabrant, left at the end of June 2024 to work independently. The work now runs through small groups and individuals: Dovetail, Kosoy's group, independent researchers, and the ILIAD conference series on theoretical alignment (held in 2024 and 2025, with a third edition announced for August 2026). The 2025 Shallow Review lists the Learning-Theoretic Agenda at about 3 full-time researchers, and tiling agents (how an agent can modify itself without losing its goals) at 1 to 10. It remains a small, niche field.

Key results

  • A computable algorithm can assign probabilities to unproven mathematical statements, learn patterns in them before it can prove them, and hold accurate beliefs about its own beliefs. Scott Garrabrant, Tsvi Benson-Tilsen, Andrew Critch, Nate Soares, Jessica Taylor, 2016
  • Functional decision theory, which treats a choice as the output of a fixed procedure, does better than the two standard decision theories on several classic puzzle cases, by the authors' analysis. Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nate Soares, 2017
  • Agents that are part of the world they reason about face four linked open problems: decision theory, embedded world-models, robust delegation and subsystem alignment. Abram Demski, Scott Garrabrant, 2018
  • Regret bounds for learning when an adversary can choose among a set of possible outcome distributions, improving the state of the art for robust bandits and robust online reinforcement learning. Alexander Appel, Vanessa Kosoy, 2025
  • Simple 'natural latent' conditions guarantee that one agent's hidden variables can be translated into another's, and the guarantee survives when the conditions hold only approximately. John Wentworth, David Lorell, 2025

Open problems

  • Give a satisfying account of how an agent that is part of its own world should decide and model that world. source
  • Show how an aligned agent can modify itself or build successors without drifting into misalignment (the tiling problem). source
  • Prove when the concepts inside an AI match human concepts well enough to be translated and checked. source
  • Connect formal results to the neural networks actually being built. source

Criticisms

  • The reviewer found no case where complete formal descriptions of AI systems had helped predict, explain or fix problems in them, and put his credence in this work being very helpful at around 10%. Daniel Dewey (then Open Philanthropy Project)
  • MIRI's own leadership became pessimistic that this research could produce significant results in time, and shifted focus to other priorities. Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI)

Who works on it

  • Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI): Made the agenda its focus from 2013 and published Embedded Agency; its Agent Foundations team left at the end of June 2024. Embedded Agency (2018)
  • Dovetail: Small research group built around Alex Altair's agent-foundations agenda; ran a fellowship in 2024-2025. Report & retrospective on the Dovetail fellowship (2025)
  • Long-Term Future Fund (LTFF): Gave Dovetail a $60,000 grant for its agent-foundations fellowship. Report & retrospective on the Dovetail fellowship (2025)
  • ILIAD (theoretical AI alignment conference series): Runs a 100-plus-person yearly conference on alignment with a mathematical focus; agent foundations is one of its listed topics. Announcing: ILIADIII: AENEID (2026)
  • Survival and Flourishing Fund: Named by the 2025 Shallow Review as a funder of Vanessa Kosoy's learning-theoretic agenda, with ARIA, the UK AI Security Institute and Coefficient Giving. Shallow review of technical AI safety, 2025 (2025)
  • Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving): Its staff member Daniel Dewey published a detailed assessment of MIRI's agent-design research in July 2017. My current thoughts on MIRI's "highly reliable agent design" work (2017)

Status

Described by the Shallow Review as seeking "philosophical clarity and mathematical formalizations"

Start reading

  • Embedded Agency, Abram Demski, Scott Garrabrant (2018). The standard illustrated introduction to the field's core problems.
  • Why Agent Foundations? An Overly Abstract Explanation, John Wentworth (2022). Short case for why robust concepts matter under strong optimisation.
  • Agent foundations: not really math, not really science, Alex Altair (2025). Explains what kind of research this is and how it makes progress.
  • My current thoughts on MIRI's "highly reliable agent design" work, Daniel Dewey (2017). The most careful sceptical review.
  • Shallow review of technical AI safety, 2025 (Theory sections), Shallow Review authors (2025). Current map of who works on which theory agenda, with outputs.

In their own words

Suppose you want to build a robot to achieve some real-world goal for you-a goal that requires the robot to learn for itself and figure out a lot of things that you don't already know. There's a complicated engineering problem here. But there's also a problem of figuring out what it even means to build a learning agent like that.

Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski (MIRI), Embedded Agency (full-text version), 2018

We sure do seem to be working with the wrong basic concepts today when we try to think about what agency is... If AI developers in the future are still working with these confused and incomplete basic concepts as they try to actually build powerful real-world optimizers, that seems like a bad position to be in.

Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski (MIRI), Embedded Agency (full-text version), 2018
