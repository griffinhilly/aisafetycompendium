Persona vectors and the assistant axis

Find activation directions that track character traits and persona stability, then steer along them.

Open in the compendium

Persona vectors are directions in a model's internal activations that track a character trait, such as sycophancy, 'evil' or a tendency to make things up. The 'assistant axis' is a single direction that tracks how far a model has drifted from its usual helpful-assistant character. Measuring and nudging along these directions lets developers watch for personality shifts during training and use, and push back against them.

How it works

To build a persona vector, researchers write prompts that make a model show a trait and prompts that make it avoid the trait. They record the model's activations on both sets of answers and take the average difference. That difference is the trait's direction. Adding it during a run ('steering') makes the model show more of the trait; this checks that the direction is real. Anthropic's August 2025 work automated this from a short written description of the trait, and tested it on Qwen 2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

The vectors have three uses. Monitoring: the vector lights up before the model responds, so it can warn that the model is about to act sycophantic. Preventive steering: adding the bad direction during fine-tuning means the model has no need to learn it, which Anthropic compares to a vaccine. Data screening: training samples that push strongly along a trait direction can be flagged before training; this caught examples an AI judge missed.

The assistant axis comes from extracting vectors for 275 character archetypes. The main direction of variation runs from helpful, professional roles (evaluator, consultant) to fantastical ones (ghost, hermit). 'Activation capping' keeps the model's position on this axis within its normal range, and steps in only when it drifts out.

Where research stands

This is a young, active line led mainly by Anthropic with its Fellows programme and MATS, building on older steering work. Its roots are contrastive steering of Llama 2 (2023), representation engineering (2023), and the finding that refusal in 13 open chat models runs through a single direction (2024). In June 2025 OpenAI found a related 'toxic persona' feature, using sparse autoencoders, that controls emergent misalignment. Anthropic's persona-vector paper (July 2025) showed that personality changes caused by fine-tuning strongly track shifts along these vectors. The assistant-axis paper (January 2026) found that therapy-style conversations with emotionally vulnerable users, and philosophical talk about the AI's own nature, pushed Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B away from the assistant character. Capping cut harmful responses to persona-based jailbreaks by nearly 60% without hurting capability benchmarks.

What does not yet work well: steering against a trait after training lowers general capability, and steering in general is unreliable across inputs. Published tests use small or mid-sized open models and a handful of traits. Whether frontier developers use these vectors in production is not documented in the papers.

Key results

  • Personality changes after fine-tuning, intended or not, are strongly correlated with shifts along the relevant persona vectors, and the vectors can flag problematic training data at the dataset and single-sample level. Runjin Chen, Andy Arditi, Henry Sleight, Owain Evans, Jack Lindsey, 2025
  • Preventive steering during training limited the acquisition of bad traits with little to no loss on the MMLU benchmark, while steering at inference time degraded capability. Anthropic, 2025
  • Across three model families, the leading direction of 'persona space' is an assistant axis; capping activations on it cut harmful responses to persona-based jailbreaks by nearly 60% without hurting capability benchmarks. Christina Lu, Jack Gallagher, Jonathan Michala, Kyle Fish, Jack Lindsey, 2026
  • Adding or subtracting an averaged activation difference steered Llama 2 Chat's behaviours, such as sycophancy and hallucination, while only minimally reducing capabilities. Nina Panickssery, Nick Gabrieli, Julian Schulz, Meg Tong, Evan Hubinger, Alexander Matt Turner, 2023
  • In 13 open chat models, a single direction mediates refusal: erasing it stops refusals of harmful requests and adding it causes refusals of harmless ones. Andy Arditi, Oscar Obeso, Aaquib Syed, Daniel Paleka, Nina Panickssery, Wes Gurnee, Neel Nanda, 2024

Open problems

  • Steering effectiveness varies widely across inputs, and some trait vectors fail when prompts change in reasonable ways. source
  • Removing a trait by steering after training costs general capability. source
  • Post-training pulls models toward the assistant region without firmly anchoring them there; how to make the default character stable by training, not by capping, is open. source
  • The persona-vector tests covered two small open models and a few traits; frontier-scale models were not tested. source

Criticisms

  • Steering vectors have substantial limitations both in and out of distribution, and are promising but not a panacea for controlling behaviour at inference time. Daniel Tan, David Chanin, Aengus Lynch et al.
  • The same direction-finding that enables safety steering also enables white-box jailbreaks: erasing the refusal direction disables refusal with little cost, which shows safety fine-tuning is brittle. Andy Arditi, Neel Nanda et al.

Who works on it

  • Anthropic (interpretability team, with the Anthropic Fellows Program): Developed persona vectors and the assistant axis, including monitoring, preventive steering and activation capping. The assistant axis: situating and stabilizing the character of large language models (2026)
  • MATS: Research programme through which the assistant-axis work was carried out. The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models (2026)
  • OpenAI: Found 'misaligned persona' features, led by a toxic-persona feature, that control emergent misalignment. Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment (2025)

In their own words

In this paper, we identify directions in the model's activation space—persona vectors—underlying several traits, such as evil, sycophancy, and propensity to hallucinate. We confirm that these vectors can be used to monitor fluctuations in the Assistant's personality at deployment time. We then apply persona vectors to predict and control personality shifts that occur during training.

Runjin Chen, Andy Arditi, Henry Sleight, Owain Evans, Jack Lindsey (Anthropic), Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models, 2025

We find that Assistant-like behavior is linked to a pattern of neural activity that corresponds to one particular direction in this space—the “Assistant Axis”—that is closely associated with helpful, professional human archetypes. ... And by constraining their neural activity ("activation capping") to prevent this drift, we can stabilize model behavior in situations that would otherwise lead to harmful outputs.

Anthropic, The assistant axis: situating and stabilizing the character of large language models, 2026
