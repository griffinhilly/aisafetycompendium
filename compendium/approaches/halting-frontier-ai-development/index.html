Halting frontier AI development

Prepare the legal, technical and institutional means for an internationally coordinated halt to the most dangerous AI development.

Open in the compendium

The aim is an agreement between countries to stop the most dangerous frontier AI work, along with the laws, monitoring and institutions that would make such a stop possible. Supporters argue that no one yet knows how to control AI much smarter than humans, so racing ahead risks catastrophe. Critics argue that a halt cannot be enforced, would hand the lead to less careful actors, and would give up large benefits.

How it works

Most current proposals target the computing power used to train the largest models. Training runs are measured in FLOP, the total number of arithmetic operations used. In a draft treaty published in November 2025, researchers at MIRI's technical governance team propose a coalition led by the United States and China. Training runs above 10^22 FLOP would need approval and monitoring, and training above 10^24 FLOP would be banned. Clusters of more than 16 top-end AI chips would have to sit in monitored facilities, and new chips would be tracked from production.

Under the draft, the US and China would first make joint commitments and start tracking chips at home. Next, they would set up secure channels and pre-notify large training runs. Only then would they negotiate binding limits and invite other countries. Barnett and Scher (May 2025) call the prepared capacity an 'Off Switch': the technical, legal and institutional means to restrict dangerous AI development, which could later be used for a coordinated halt.

Outside campaigners want the same thing. The Future of Life Institute's March 2023 letter asked labs to pause for at least six months the training of systems more powerful than GPT-4. Its October 2025 Statement on Superintelligence goes further, calling for a prohibition on developing superintelligence until there is broad scientific agreement that it can be done safely and strong public support.

Where research stands

As of October 2026, no country or group of countries has agreed to halt frontier AI development. The 2023 letter did not lead to a pause. Since then, work has shifted from short pauses to long-term, conditional prohibitions on superintelligence, with detailed treaty drafts and verification plans. The authors of the MIRI draft treaty say it would be technically sufficient if adopted today, but that the political will to adopt it does not yet exist.

The Statement on Superintelligence had over 78,000 signatures by October 2026, including Yoshua Bengio, Geoffrey Hinton and figures from across the political spectrum. In September 2026, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced a US bill to ban artificial superintelligence and to pursue international agreements and export controls toward a global ban. Press coverage judged its chances of passing as low. The hardest open questions are how a country could verify that a rival is complying, and how to keep a halt stable as chips get cheaper and algorithms improve.

Key results

  • Of four strategic scenarios (an internationally coordinated Off Switch, a US national AI project, a light-touch world, and a world of mutual sabotage threats), the authors judge all but the Off Switch path to carry unacceptable risk of catastrophe. Peter Barnett, Aaron Scher (MIRI), 2025
  • A full draft treaty sets out how a US- and China-led coalition could cap training compute, consolidate large chip clusters into monitored facilities, restrict dangerous research and verify compliance with inspections and chip tracking. Aaron Scher, David Abecassis, Peter Barnett, Brian Abeyta (MIRI), 2025
  • The March 2023 open letter asked all AI labs to pause for at least six months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4, and asked governments to step in with a moratorium if labs did not. Future of Life Institute, 2023
  • The Statement on Superintelligence, released on 22 October 2025, calls for a prohibition on developing superintelligence until there is broad scientific consensus that it can be done safely and controllably, and strong public buy-in; it had over 78,000 signatures by late October 2026. Future of Life Institute (host), 2025
  • In September 2026 a US bill proposed banning superintelligence, pausing some advanced AI development until a new federal department sets safety rules, and pursuing international agreements and export controls toward a global ban. Al Jazeera, 2026

Open problems

  • How rivals that distrust each other could verify that no one is secretly training a banned model. source
  • How to keep a compute cap meaningful as algorithms get more efficient and chips get cheaper, so that banned capability needs less and less compute. source
  • How to get the US and China, the two leading AI powers, to agree to restrictions at the same time, when each fears falling behind the other. source
  • What scientific test would show that development can safely resume, so that a conditional halt has a clear end condition. source

Criticisms

  • A pause would likely backfire: alignment research would lose access to more capable models, hardware would keep improving so that lifting the pause could cause a sudden jump in capability, and work would move to less regulated labs and countries. Nora Belrose (guest post on Jacob Steinhardt's Bounded Regret blog, October 2023)
  • A moratorium is unrealistic and would cause real harm by slowing the creation of valuable technology; regulation should target products, not research. Andrew Ng and Yann LeCun (April 2023)
  • Supporters themselves concede the political will for a binding agreement does not yet exist, so the approach depends on a future shift in politics. MIRI technical governance team

Who works on it

  • Machine Intelligence Research Institute (technical governance team): Publishes the research agenda for an internationally coordinated halt and a full draft treaty. An International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation of Artificial Superintelligence (2025)
  • Future of Life Institute: Ran the 2023 pause letter and hosts the 2025 Statement on Superintelligence calling for a prohibition. Statement on Superintelligence (2025)
  • PauseAI: Campaigns for a global treaty pausing the training of the most powerful general-purpose AI systems, overseen by an IAEA-style agency. PauseAI Proposal
  • ControlAI: Campaigns for a prohibition on superintelligence, briefs lawmakers in the US, UK, Canada and Germany, and co-wrote the policy plan A Narrow Path. A Narrow Path (2024)

Status

Research agenda, advocacy campaigns and a first bill. The 2023 letter asked for a pause of at least six months on training systems more powerful than GPT-4. The Statement on Superintelligence (October 2025) calls for "a prohibition on the development of superintelligence" until there is broad scientific consensus that it is safe and strong public buy-in. MIRI researchers proposed a draft international agreement in November 2025. On 23 September 2026 Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, which would ban superintelligence and pause advanced AI development until a federal regulator sets safety standards.

In their own words

Our favored scenario involves building the technical, legal, and institutional infrastructure required to internationally restrict dangerous AI development and deployment (which we refer to as an Off Switch), which leads into an internationally coordinated Halt on frontier AI activities at some point in the future.

Peter Barnett and Aaron Scher (MIRI), AI Governance to Avoid Extinction: The Strategic Landscape and Actionable Research Questions, 2025

Therefore, we call on all AI labs to immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4. This pause should be public and verifiable, and include all key actors. If such a pause cannot be enacted quickly, governments should step in and institute a moratorium.

Future of Life Institute, Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter, 2023
