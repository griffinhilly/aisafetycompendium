Safety-motivated frontier labs

A safety-focused lab builds frontier AI itself, so it can do safety research on the most capable systems and keep influence.

Open in the compendium

This is the strategy of a safety-focused organisation building the most capable AI itself. The case for it is that some safety problems only appear in near-frontier systems, that a frontier company can set industry norms and shape policy, and that if it stood aside, less careful developers would lead. The case against is that it adds to the race it warns about. Anthropic is the clearest example.

How it works

In March 2023 Anthropic set out its reasoning in 'Core Views on AI Safety': it is 'necessary to do safety research on "frontier" AI systems', because many serious problems may only arise with near-human-level systems, and some safety methods only work on large models. It also argued that showing safety research makes models more valuable could push other developers to invest in safety, and that a frontier company could warn policymakers with evidence if risks prove severe. The same essay warned against 'a scenario in which safety-motivated research accelerates the deployment of dangerous technologies.'

Josh Clymer's 2025 plan for a hypothetical responsible developer, 'Magma', makes the trade-off explicit. Before AI greatly speeds up AI research, Magma should 'scale AI capabilities aggressively', spending on safety only what does not meaningfully slow it, to gain resources, compelling demonstrations of risk and government, industry and public influence; as capabilities grow, slowing down becomes increasingly helpful. He calls it 'a dangerous plan for a dangerous world.' Safe Superintelligence Inc. takes a different form of the same bet: a lab with one goal, building safe superintelligence, and a business model meant to shield safety work from short-term commercial pressure.

The frontier position is then used as leverage. In September 2026 Anthropic's chief executive Dario Amodei published 'We Must Pace the Frontier', calling on the industry to slow capability gains so safety can catch up, and Anthropic committed on its own to host embedded outside evaluators.

Where research stands

The strategy is practised and has some visible influence: when Amodei called for pacing in September 2026, OpenAI's chief executive said OpenAI would also invite evaluators in. Outside grades still find the safety-focused leader far from adequate: the Future of Life Institute's Summer 2026 index gave Anthropic the best grade among nine companies, a C+, and a D+ on existential safety.

Whether the strategy is net positive is contested and hard to test, because the counterfactual (the race without a safety-motivated participant) cannot be observed. Supporters point to frontier safety research, policy influence and industry norms. Critics argue that safety-motivated labs legitimise and accelerate the race, draw talent from independent safety work, and come under the same commercial pressure as everyone else. Joe Carlsmith, explaining in November 2025 why he joined Anthropic, concluded the company was probably net positive while listing these objections in detail. Only a few organisations pursue this strategy directly, so this entry lists those with stated, public rationales.

Key results

  • Anthropic argues safety research must be done on frontier systems because the most serious problems may only arise near human-level capability, while warning against safety work that accelerates dangerous deployment. Anthropic, 2023
  • A plan for a responsible frontier developer recommends scaling aggressively until AI meaningfully speeds up AI research, after which slowing down becomes increasingly helpful, and calls itself 'a dangerous plan for a dangerous world'. Josh Clymer, 2025
  • Anthropic's chief executive called for deliberately slowing capability gains through embedded evaluators, coordination among democracies and global agreements, and committed Anthropic to host embedded evaluators unilaterally. Dario Amodei, 2026
  • After Amodei's essay, OpenAI's chief executive agreed the frontier needs pacing and said OpenAI would also invite evaluators in. Anthony Ha (TechCrunch), 2026
  • An independent panel gave the highest-graded company, Anthropic, a C+ overall and a D+ on existential safety. Future of Life Institute, 2026

Open problems

  • How to tell whether a safety-motivated lab's presence slows or speeds the overall race, since the counterfactual cannot be observed. source
  • When a lab that scaled fast should switch to slowing down, and whether it will in practice. source
  • How to keep a safety mission intact under commercial pressure and fundraising needs. source
  • How a single lab can turn its position into industry-wide limits without government backing. source

Criticisms

  • Frontier labs' main output speeds up capabilities, insiders have little power to slow them, and 'It's perniciously easy to "safetywash" despite every intention to the contrary.'. Joe Rogero (LessWrong)
  • Safety-motivated labs can worsen race dynamics, accelerate timelines and absorb too much of the safety community's talent; Carlsmith lists these as serious objections even while joining Anthropic. Joe Carlsmith (summarising critics)
  • Proposals from leading labs to pace the frontier may mainly serve those labs and amount to regulatory capture. Brian Merchant, as reported by TechCrunch

Who works on it

  • Anthropic: Builds frontier models on the stated rationale that safety research and influence require frontier access. Core Views on AI Safety: When, Why, What, and How (2023)
  • Safe Superintelligence Inc.: A lab whose single goal is building safe superintelligence, advancing capabilities while trying to keep safety ahead. Safe Superintelligence Inc. (mission statement)
  • Redwood Research: Published Josh Clymer's plan for how a responsible frontier developer should allocate effort between scaling and safety. Planning for Extreme AI Risks (2025)

Status

Practised by at least one lab; contested. Clymer's plan for a responsible lab includes scaling capabilities aggressively, and he calls it a dangerous plan for a dangerous world.

In their own words

A major reason Anthropic exists as an organization is that we believe it's necessary to do safety research on "frontier" AI systems. ... Many of our most serious safety concerns might only arise with near-human-level systems, and it's difficult or intractable to make progress on these problems without access to such AIs.

Anthropic, Core views on AI safety: When, why, what, and how, 2023

This heuristic recommends that Magma should initially scale at roughly the maximum speed Magma can do so. ... Advancing capabilities quickly helps Magma acquire key resources sooner, including: More capable AI labor. More compelling demos of risks. More relevant safety research. More investment and compute. More government, industry, and public influence.

Josh Clymer (Redwood Research blog; also on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum), Planning for Extreme AI Risks, 2025
