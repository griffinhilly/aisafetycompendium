AI and people's own judgement

Disempowerment formalism

Create formal notions of empowerment and disempowerment that are conceptually and operationally sound.

Open in the compendium

This approach tries to define human empowerment and disempowerment precisely enough to calculate. Without such a definition, "AI must not disempower people" cannot be written into a training objective, an evaluation or a regulation. A good formalism would let researchers say how much control people have over outcomes, who has it, and whether an AI system is raising or lowering it.

How it works

Much formal work starts from "empowerment", an information-theory measure of how much control an agent's actions give it over what it later senses: roughly, how many distinct future states it can reliably bring about. Applied to people, an AI is empowering if it widens the set of outcomes a person can choose and reach.

Jobst Heitzig and Ram Potham's 2025 proposal shows what a full version involves. It scores each person's power by how many possible goals they could reach with reasonable certainty, given what they know, their limited rationality and what others do. It then adds up power across people with a formula that counts gains for the least powerful more heavily, and penalises risky gambles. The AI is asked to "softly" maximise this score, choosing good actions without pushing to the extreme, which limits the harm if the score misses something important about power.

A second line models groups instead of individuals. RAND's April 2026 model uses social choice theory, the mathematics of group decisions. It asks which coalitions of people (and AI systems) can decide an outcome, and tracks three metrics: the spread of decisive coalitions, the smallest decisive coalition, and who sits in it. In this model, agency is fully lost when a single minimal coalition decides every choice.

Where research stands

This is a small, niche area with a handful of active researchers. The 2023 Shallow Review of alignment agendas listed a "disempowerment formalism" project by Damiano Fornasiere and Pietro Greiner, about two full-time staff funded with $60,300 through Manifund, with no outputs listed. The 2025 edition has no entry for it. The topic continued under other names: the 2025 review lists Heitzig and Potham's power metric under "behavior alignment theory", and a 2026 follow-up extends a paper accepted to the Algorithmic Decision Theory 2026 conference. RAND added a collective, coalition-based model in April 2026 and has said it is developing "agency audits" for new models.

Heitzig and Potham state that their objective still needs testing "in large, safety-critical, multi-agent environments". Related practical work uses simpler single-number empowerment measures, which already help train assistants in narrow settings such as coding.

Key results

  • An axiomatic, inequality- and risk-averse aggregate of long-term human power can be computed by backward induction or approximated with multi-agent reinforcement learning, and softly maximising it may be safer than direct utility-based objectives. Jobst Heitzig, Ram Potham, 2025
  • Certain desirable properties of a human-empowerment objective force specific mathematical forms and narrow the allowed parameter ranges. Jobst Heitzig, Ram Potham, 2026
  • A social-choice model identifies three pathways of collective agency erosion (human disenfranchisement, AI enfranchisement and AI agenda control) and a formal end state in which one minimal coalition decides all choices. Alvin Moon, Benjamin Boudreaux (RAND), 2026
  • Adding a human-empowerment term to reinforcement learning let an assistant help without inferring the human's goal; the authors show cases where it overcomes failures caused by ambiguous or misspecified goals, and test it in a simulated teleoperation user study. Yuqing Du, Stas Tiomkin, Emre Kiciman, Daniel Polani, Pieter Abbeel, Anca Dragan, 2020
  • A formal definition of agency-preserving AI-human interaction, with simple simulations, shows agency loss in learning agents and argues long-term agency should be optimised explicitly. Catalin Mitelut, Ben Smith, Peter Vamplew, 2023

Open problems

  • No formal human-power objective has been tested in large, safety-critical, multi-agent environments. source
  • Turning coalition-based metrics into practical "agency evaluations" for real AI deployments has not yet been done. source
  • Formal measures must separate helping a person reach their goals from changing what their goals are, which current definitions do not do cleanly. source
  • Computing empowerment exactly is hard in realistic settings, so methods rely on cheaper proxy measures. source

Criticisms

  • Empowerment-style concepts rest on intuitions about free will that become less coherent the better an AI models a person, so a formalism may not capture what we care about. Steven Byrnes

Who works on it

  • Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research: Jobst Heitzig, with independent researcher Ram Potham, develops axiomatic human-power objectives for AI. Model-Based Soft Maximization of Suitable Metrics of Long-Term Human Power (2025)
  • RAND (Center for the Geopolitics of Artificial General Intelligence, with RAND Global and Emerging Risks): Built a social-choice model and metrics of collective agency erosion and is developing "agency audits". A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency (2026)
  • Manifund: Funded the 2023 "disempowerment formalism" project ($60,300, per the 2023 Shallow Review). Shallow review of live agendas in alignment & safety (2023)

Status

The named entry was dropped from the 2025 edition of the Shallow Review. Related formal work continues under other labels: that edition lists Heitzig and Potham's long-term human power objective under "Behavior alignment theory", and RAND published "A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency" on 20 April 2026.

Start reading

  • Empowerment -- an Introduction, Christoph Salge, Cornelius Glackin, Daniel Polani (2013). Accessible introduction to the information-theoretic empowerment measure.
  • Model-Based Soft Maximization of Suitable Metrics of Long-Term Human Power, Jobst Heitzig, Ram Potham (2025). A detailed attempt to turn aggregate human power into an AI objective, with algorithms to compute it.
  • A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency, Alvin Moon, Benjamin Boudreaux (2026). Collective, coalition-based formalism with policy recommendations.
  • Will We Know If AI Takes Over? Q&A with Benjamin Boudreaux, RAND (2026). Plain-language explainer of the RAND model and the planned agency audits.

offer formal and operational notions of (dis)empowerment which are conceptually satisfactory and operationally implementable.

technicalities (Gavin Leech) and Stag (LessWrong / AI Alignment Forum), Shallow review of live agendas in alignment & safety, 2023

This paper explores the idea of promoting both safety and wellbeing by forcing AI agents explicitly to empower humans and to manage the power balance between humans and AI agents in a desirable way. Using a principled, partially axiomatic approach, we design a parametrizable and decomposable objective function that represents an inequality- and risk-averse long-term aggregate of human power.

Jobst Heitzig, Ram Potham, Model-Based Soft Maximization of Suitable Metrics of Long-Term Human Power, 2025
