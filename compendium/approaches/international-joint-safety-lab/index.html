International joint safety lab (CERN-style)

Countries pool people and computing in one shared institution to do frontier AI or AI safety research.

Open in the compendium

A 'CERN for AI' would have many countries pool money, researchers and computing power in one shared institution, modelled on the European particle physics laboratory. Some versions focus only on safety research; others would make the joint project the leading developer of advanced AI. Supporters argue it would ease the race between companies and nations and give safety work the scale it lacks; critics argue it is politically unrealistic and would not control the labs that matter.

How it works

There are two main versions. In the narrow one, an 'AI Safety Project' (Ho and colleagues, July 2023) would bring leading researchers together and give them computing power and access to advanced models to work on technical safety, modelled on CERN and the ITER fusion project. In October 2023, Google DeepMind's chief executive Demis Hassabis said he would like to see a CERN-style body for AI safety research.

In the broad version, the joint project builds frontier AI itself. Miles Brundage (December 2024) describes a coalition of countries and companies that would pool resources into a single, possibly physically spread-out, civilian AI effort. It would start with the US and close allies, including chip supply-chain countries such as Japan, South Korea and the Netherlands, and would grow over time; Brundage calls the question of China's involvement critical and hard, given deep US-China distrust. Its first job would be very secure chips and data centres. Because it would lead the field, it could slow down for safety without fear of being overtaken. The Multinational AGI Consortium proposal (Hausenloy, Miotti and Dennis, October 2023) goes further: one secure institution would be the only body allowed to build advanced AI, backed by a global ban on other development.

Where research stands

No international joint AI safety lab exists as of October 2026; the idea remains at the proposal stage. The nearest real structures are looser. In November 2024 AI safety institutes and offices from nine countries and the European Commission launched the International Network of AI Safety Institutes to cooperate on research, testing and guidance. The European Commission launched RAISE, a virtual institute pooling compute and talent for AI-driven science, in November 2025, but it targets scientific applications rather than safety.

The main unresolved questions are political. Ho and colleagues note that a joint project could pull safety researchers away from the frontier labs, and that labs may not grant enough access to their models. The UN High-level Advisory Body (September 2024) called CERN a more fitting analogy than the IAEA for pooling resources, but noted limits. Critics argue a new state-backed lab would trail the frontier companies by several generations, and that research without enforcement power does not address the real bottleneck.

Key results

  • An AI Safety Project modelled on CERN and ITER could increase the scale, resources and coordination of safety research, but may draw researchers away from frontier labs and struggle to get adequate model access. Lewis Ho, Joslyn Barnhart, Robert Trager, Yoshua Bengio, Miles Brundage and others, 2023
  • A coalition of countries and companies could pool resources into one civilian AI effort that becomes the leading developer, giving it room to slow down for safety. Miles Brundage, 2024
  • A Multinational AGI Consortium would be the only entity allowed to build advanced AI, enforced by a global moratorium, with benefits shared among members. Jason Hausenloy, Andrea Miotti, Claire Dennis, 2023
  • AI safety institutes and offices from nine countries and the European Commission launched a network in November 2024 to cooperate on safety research, joint testing and guidance. International Network of AI Safety Institutes, 2024
  • The UN advisory body judged CERN, which pools funding, talent and infrastructure, a more fitting analogy for sharing the benefits of AI, while noting limits to the comparison because AI needs a more distributed approach. UN High-level Advisory Body on Artificial Intelligence, 2024

Open problems

  • How a joint lab would get enough access to the most advanced models, given companies' commercial interests and the risk of weight theft. source
  • How to keep the joint lab secure against espionage without the secrecy that would cripple its research. source
  • Whether and when to bring in China, a question Brundage calls critical and hard. source
  • How such a lab would relate to the private companies that currently lead AI, and whether it would replace, complement or regulate them. source

Criticisms

  • A state-backed catch-up lab would trail frontier companies by two or three generations and would not reduce risk; states should instead push for a treaty with red lines and an IAEA-style verifier. Charbel-Raphaël (LessWrong, July 2026)
  • A joint project could pull safety researchers away from frontier labs, where being close to development may be valuable. Lewis Ho and colleagues (2023)
  • The MAGIC proposal's own authors do not address political feasibility or the legal means to enforce its ban on other development. Jason Hausenloy, Andrea Miotti, Claire Dennis (2023)

Who works on it

  • International Network of AI Safety Institutes: A looser network of national safety institutes cooperating on research, joint testing and guidance. Mission Statement (2024)
  • Google DeepMind: Its chief executive Demis Hassabis has called for a CERN-style body for AI safety research. Google DeepMind Chief Believes AI Risk Is Similar To Climate Crisis (IBTimes, reporting a Guardian interview) (2023)
  • UN High-level Advisory Body on Artificial Intelligence: Assessed the CERN analogy for pooling AI resources in its final report. Governing AI for Humanity: Final Report (2024)

Status

Proposal stage, discussed in essays and papers. The Ho et al. paper models its AI Safety Project on ITER and CERN. Brundage describes a single, possibly physically decentralised, civilian AI effort.

In their own words

The idea behind such a project is to pool many countries' and companies' resources into a single (possibly physically decentralized) civilian AI development effort, with the purpose of ensuring that this technology is designed securely, safely, and in the global interest.

Miles Brundage, My recent lecture at Berkeley and a vision for a "CERN for AI", 2024

An AI Safety Project ... could bring together leading researchers and engineers, and provide them with access to computing resources and advanced AI models for work on technical mitigations of AI risks, potentially working with parallel industry efforts. It would promote AI safety R&D by increasing its scale, resourcing and coordination.

Lewis Ho, Joslyn Barnhart, Robert Trager, Yoshua Bengio, Miles Brundage, Allison Carnegie, Rumman Chowdhury, Allan Dafoe, Gillian Hadfield, Margaret Levi, Duncan Snidal, International Institutions for Advanced AI, 2023
