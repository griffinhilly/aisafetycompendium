Society: defence and who holds power

Societal resilience

Improved epistemics and decision quality

Strengthen forecasting and institutional decision-making as a general safety lever.

Open in the compendium

This approach aims to improve how people and institutions form beliefs and make decisions, often with AI tools for forecasting, fact-checking, deliberation and negotiation. The safety case is simple: even well-built AI can go badly if the governments, labs and publics steering it misread the situation or fail to coordinate. Better shared understanding is a general lever that helps with many risks at once.

How it works

Work falls into two broad kinds. "Epistemic" tools help people see what is true and what is likely: AI forecasters that give calibrated probabilities on future events, fact-checkers, and tools that trace where a claim came from. "Coordination" tools help groups with different interests reach agreement: AI-assisted deliberation, negotiation support, and systems that check whether parties keep agreements.

A concrete example is Community Notes on X, where volunteers add context to misleading posts and a note is shown only if raters who usually disagree both find it helpful. In 2025 researchers from X's Community Notes team, MIT and other universities proposed letting AI models draft notes, while humans keep the final say on which notes appear; human feedback would then train the models. Forecasting is another: in 2024 a retrieval-augmented language model system came close to the crowd average of competitive human forecasters. ForecastBench, run by the Forecasting Research Institute, tracks how AI forecasters compare with expert "superforecasters" on questions whose answers are not yet known.

The idea predates current tools. Dan Hendrycks, Nicholas Carlini, John Schulman and Jacob Steinhardt's 2021 research agenda lists "systemic safety", including better decision-making by the institutions that steer AI, as one of four core problems.

Where research stands

The field is real but under-developed. In October 2026 a "State of the Field" report, based on about 65 in-depth interviews, found that building a prototype is usually much easier than getting it used: projects stall on trust, procurement and fitting into existing workflows. Forecasting is one of the most established niches. The forecasting company Mantic raised $25 million in September 2026, after beating all 676 human participants in the summer 2026 Metaculus Cup. AI performance on ambiguous, long-horizon questions is less clear.

Funding has grown. Coefficient Giving's Forecasting team ran a request for proposals on AI for forecasting and sound reasoning, with grants of $100,000 to $1 million, and Coefficient Giving lists "AI for decision-makers" as a Project Tailwind initiative. Forethought argues the work is undervalued in existential-risk circles and should be deliberately sped up. The main worries are dual use and dependence: the same tools can help a small group gain strategic advantage, and measuring AI persuasion currently seems to be ahead of defending against it.

Key results

  • A core ML safety agenda lists "systemic safety", including ML for informed decision-making (such as forecasting events) to improve governance decisions, as one of four research problems. Dan Hendrycks, Nicholas Carlini, John Schulman, Jacob Steinhardt, 2021
  • A retrieval-augmented language-model system neared, and in some settings beat, the crowd aggregate of competitive human forecasters on questions after its training cut-off. Danny Halawi, Fred Zhang, Chen Yueh-Han, Jacob Steinhardt, 2024
  • On a contamination-free benchmark of future events, expert human forecasters still outperformed the best language model in 2024. Ezra Karger, Houtan Bastani, et al., Philip E. Tetlock, 2024
  • Community Notes could let both humans and AI models write notes while diverse human raters decide which are shown, with their feedback used to improve the models. Haiwen Li, Soham De, Manon Revel, et al., Michiel A. Bakker, 2025
  • Epistemic, coordination and risk-targeted AI applications can be accelerated through data, scaffolding and compute allocation, and existential-risk work should shift toward this. Lizka Vaintrob, Owen Cotton-Barratt (Forethought), 2025
  • Across about 65 field-strategy interviews, the most consistent finding was that adoption, not prototyping, is the bottleneck for AI epistemics and coordination tools. Ben_N, AlexCsaky, 2026

Criticisms

  • Commercial incentives may produce these tools anyway, so the counterfactual value of safety-motivated work is unclear. 80,000 Hours, weighing objections in its problem profile
  • Better forecasting and planning tools may speed up dangerous AI capabilities and hype. 80,000 Hours
  • Tools that improve forecasting or negotiation also help bad actors and could give some groups an illegitimate strategic edge, in extreme cases enabling a power grab. 80,000 Hours

In their own words

Even if we create reliable ML systems, these systems will not exhibit or ensure safety if the institutions that steer ML systems make poor decisions. ... This is why we suggest creating tools to help decision-makers handle ML systems in highly uncertain, quickly evolving, turbulent situations.

Dan Hendrycks, Nicholas Carlini, John Schulman, Jacob Steinhardt, Unsolved Problems in ML Safety, 2021

People are more likely to handle novel challenges well if they can see them coming clearly, and have good ideas about what could be done about them. ... High quality forecasts, especially of novel technological developments and their strategic implications, could help us to anticipate and prepare for key challenges.

Lizka Vaintrob and Owen Cotton-Barratt (Forethought), AI Tools for Existential Security, 2025
