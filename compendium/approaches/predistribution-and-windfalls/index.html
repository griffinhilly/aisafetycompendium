Predistribution and windfall mechanisms

Decide who holds equity, access and governance rights before the gains arrive, not after.

Open in the compendium

This approach decides, before AI's economic gains arrive, who will share in them and who gets a say. "Windfall" mechanisms commit AI firms in advance to hand over part of any enormous profits. "Predistribution" goes further and spreads ownership, access and opportunity early, so that extreme concentration never forms. The safety link is that concentrated AI wealth could become concentrated, entrenched political power.

How it works

The best-known windfall proposal is the Windfall Clause from the Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI) and the Future of Humanity Institute (2020). An AI company would sign a binding commitment now to donate a growing share of profits only if they ever became extreme. In the authors' example schedule, giving starts at 1% of marginal profits once profits pass 0.1% of world output, and rises to half of marginal profits at 10 to 100% of world output. Because the trigger is so remote, the commitment costs a firm little today.

Sam Altman's 2021 essay "Moore's Law for Everything" proposed an American Equity Fund, paid for by a yearly 2.5% tax on the value of large companies (in shares) and on privately held land, with dividends paid to every adult citizen. In October 2025 OpenAI's restructuring left its non-profit, the OpenAI Foundation, holding equity then worth about $130 billion, with an initial $25 billion commitment to health and "AI resilience".

Predistribution is set against these after-the-fact payouts. In 2024 Saffron Huang of the Collective Intelligence Project and Sam Manning of GovAI argued that a Windfall Clause only starts once a company already holds extreme power. They favour spreading the ability to contribute to and benefit from AI value creation early, so that equality of opportunity is secured in advance.

Where research stands

These are established proposals with almost no implementation. No AI company has signed a Windfall Clause. The closest real structures are corporate: OpenAI's non-profit stake and its public-benefit-corporation form. Policy research is growing. The Windfall Trust, a project the Future of Life Institute began spinning out as its own non-profit in late 2024, now runs AI economic scenario exercises, keeps a catalogue of 48 policy options, and has published a scenario analysis for the UK.

Evidence on cash transfers, the main redistributive tool, is mixed. A large US experiment gave 1,000 low-income people $1,000 a month for three years. Recipients worked 1 to 2 hours less per week, and their well-being rose in the first year but then fell back to control-group levels. Such findings shape debate on universal basic income, but they say little about a world where AI replaces most labour. The core open question is political: who could enforce a pledge against a company that has become more powerful than the bodies meant to hold it to account?

Key results

  • Proposes an ex ante commitment by AI firms to donate a significant share of any extremely large profits that depend on transformative AI breakthroughs. Cullen O'Keefe, Peter Cihon, Ben Garfinkel, Carrick Flynn, Jade Leung, Allan Dafoe, 2020
  • Argues that windfall and post-AGI basic-income schemes act too late, after power has concentrated, and that predistribution of opportunity is a stronger lever. Saffron Huang, Sam Manning, 2024
  • Proposes an American Equity Fund financed by a 2.5% yearly tax on large companies' market value and on private land, paid out to all adult citizens. Sam Altman, 2021
  • OpenAI's recapitalisation left its non-profit holding equity worth about $130 billion, with an initial $25 billion commitment to health and AI resilience. OpenAI, 2025
  • Unconditional transfers of $1,000 a month for three years reduced recipients' work by 1 to 2 hours a week and raised well-being only temporarily. Eva Vivalt, Elizabeth Rhodes, Alexander W. Bartik, David E. Broockman, Patrick Krause, Sarah Miller, 2024

Criticisms

  • A Windfall Clause triggers only once a company has extreme monopoly power, by which time economic power has likely become political power that can blunt the pledge. Saffron Huang and Sam Manning, writing for the Collective Intelligence Project
  • After-the-fact payouts adjust downstream numbers while leaving the forces that generate inequality untouched. Saffron Huang and Sam Manning, writing for the Collective Intelligence Project
  • An equity tax would push companies to return value to shareholders instead of reinvesting it in growth, and to try to move offshore. Sam Altman, on the weaknesses of his own proposal

Who works on it

  • Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI): Published the Windfall Clause proposal with the Future of Humanity Institute. The Windfall Clause: Distributing the Benefits of AI for the Common Good (2020)
  • Collective Intelligence Project: Argues for predistribution and co-runs a funding call that includes economic power and benefit-sharing. Predistribution over Redistribution: Beyond the Windfall Clause (2024)
  • Windfall Trust: Policy research non-profit running AI economic scenario exercises and a catalogue of policy options. Windfall Trust (policy options catalogue and UK scenario analysis) (2026)
  • Future of Life Institute: Started the Windfall Trust project, aimed at a global safety net funded by AI gains, and in late 2024 began spinning it out as a separate non-profit. The Windfall Trust (2024)
  • OpenAI Foundation: Non-profit holding a large equity stake in OpenAI Group PBC, committed to spend on health and AI resilience. Built to benefit everyone (2025)
  • OpenResearch: Its staff supported a three-year randomised guaranteed-income study, according to the paper's acknowledgements. The Employment Effects of a Guaranteed Income: Experimental Evidence from Two U.S. States (2024)

Status

Established proposals, not implemented.

In their own words

This paper offers one potentially attractive tool for addressing such issues: the Windfall Clause, which is an ex ante commitment by AI firms to donate a significant amount of any eventual extremely large profits.

Cullen O'Keefe, Peter Cihon, Ben Garfinkel, Carrick Flynn, Jade Leung, Allan Dafoe (FHI / GovAI), The Windfall Clause: Distributing the Benefits of AI for the Common Good, 2020

While economic redistribution might be essential to ensure widespread prosperity in a world with advanced AI systems, we argue that focusing on predistribution - proactively ensuring widespread opportunity to benefit from AI - can reduce the likelihood that AI exacerbates inequality in the first place.

Saffron Huang and Sam Manning (Collective Intelligence Project), Predistribution over Redistribution: Beyond the Windfall Clause, 2024
