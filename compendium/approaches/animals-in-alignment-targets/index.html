Deciding what AI should value

Including animals in alignment targets

Make animal interests something AI is aligned to weigh.

Open in the compendium

This approach asks that AI systems be aligned to weigh the interests of non-human animals, not only those of humans. Most alignment work aims at human intentions, preferences or values, which leaves out the great majority of beings that can suffer. Its advocates argue that AI systems built without concern for animal welfare pose significant risks to animals, both now and over the long term.

How it works

The lightest version adds animal welfare to the written principles a model is trained on. Yip Fai Tse, Adrià Moret, Soenke Ziesche and Peter Singer (2025) recommend that AI companies tell their models to treat all potentially sentient animals as deserving moral consideration, in a balanced, non-preachy tone, and to avoid outputs that completely disregard animal welfare. They call this a 'basic' level of concern: avoid harm to animals when doing so carries no significant cost to humans.

A second step is measurement. Benchmarks such as AnimalHarmBench pose thousands of questions, some taken from Reddit post titles and some generated across 50 animal categories and 50 ethical scenarios, and use an AI judge to score whether each answer would raise or lower the risk of harm to animals. A third route works through training data: some advocates propose training models on synthetic documents that express concern for animal welfare.

Where research stands

The field is small. Academic work is led by philosophers: Simon Coghlan and Christine Parker at the University of Melbourne mapped how AI can harm animals (2023) and in 2026 replied to Tse and colleagues that 'basic' alignment is sometimes not enough. Empirical studies show the gap is real. A 2025 study found that language models reliably recognised speciesist statements but seldom judged them wrong, and normalised or rationalised harm to farmed animals while refusing to do so for other animals. Some lab documents now mention animals: Claude's 2026 constitution lists 'Welfare of animals and of all sentient beings' among the values Claude weighs.

A community is forming around this. Sentient Futures (formerly AI for Animals) held a conference at UC Berkeley in March 2025 with nearly 550 participants, runs courses, and hosted a summit in San Francisco in early 2026. What is unsettled is how much weight animals should get, how to resolve conflicts with human interests, and whether the effort competes for funding with established animal welfare work.

Key results

  • Current alignment proposals largely ignore non-human animals; there are strong reasons to align AI with at least a basic concern for animal welfare when this has no significant cost, and concrete principles for model specs are proposed. Yip Fai Tse, Adrià Moret, Soenke Ziesche, Peter Singer, 2025
  • AI can harm animals in many ways, which can be mapped systematically using David Fraser's framework of human activities affecting sentient animals. Simon Coghlan, Christine Parker, 2023
  • AnimalHarmBench, with 1,850 curated and 2,500 synthetic questions across 50 animal categories, found significant differences in animal-harm risk across frontier models, animal types and scenarios. Arturs Kanepajs, Aditi Basu, Sankalpa Ghose, Constance Li and others, 2025
  • Language models recognised speciesist statements but seldom judged them wrong, and normalised or rationalised harm to farmed animals while refusing to do so for non-farmed animals. Monika Jotautaitė, Lucius Caviola, David A. Brewster, Thilo Hagendorff, 2025
  • 'Basic' AI-animal alignment is sometimes insufficient; going further is both feasible and morally required in a range of circumstances. Simon Coghlan, Christine Parker, 2026

Open problems

  • Deciding how much weight animal interests should get relative to human interests, beyond the cost-free 'basic' level. source
  • Building reliable evaluations of animal-harm risk, since current benchmarks rely on AI judges that must be corrected for bias toward their own outputs. source
  • Removing inconsistent treatment of farmed versus non-farmed animals in model outputs. source

Criticisms

  • Giving each living animal equal weight in an AI's objective would be catastrophic for humans, since humans are vastly outnumbered by krill and bacteria; Tse and co-authors reply that their proposal asks only for basic, low-cost concern. Stuart Russell (as quoted and answered in Tse et al., 2025)
  • Aligning AI with humanity's idealised preferences would already protect animals, so a separate animal target is unnecessary; Tse and co-authors argue this proposal is unlikely to be implemented and would still risk animal harm. Objection stated and answered in Tse et al., 2025
  • Moving animal-welfare money toward AI work pulls it out of farm animal welfare and into 'something that is hypothetical at this particular moment'. Matt Dominguez, Compassion in World Farming (quoted in MIT Technology Review, March 2026)

Who works on it

  • University of Melbourne: Simon Coghlan and Christine Parker's research on AI harms to animals and AI-animal alignment. Beyond 'Basic' AI-Animal Alignment (2026)
  • Sentient Futures: Field-building group (formerly AI for Animals) that runs conferences, courses and a community on AI and non-human welfare. AI for Animals 2025 Bay Area Retrospective (2025)
  • Compassion in Machine Learning (CaML): Proposes training models on synthetic documents that reflect concern for animals and built a benchmark of how models reason about animal welfare. The Bay Area's animal welfare movement wants to recruit AI (MIT Technology Review) (2026)
  • Anthropic: Claude's constitution lists the welfare of animals and of all sentient beings among the values Claude weighs. Claude's Constitution (2026)
  • Princeton University Center for Human Values: Gave a grant supporting part of Yip Fai Tse's work on the case for including animals in alignment. AI Alignment: The Case for Including Animals (2025)

In their own words

AI alignment efforts and proposals try to make AI systems ethical, safe and beneficial for humans by making them follow human intentions, preferences or values. However, these proposals largely disregard the vast majority of moral patients in existence: non-human animals.

Yip Fai Tse, Adrià Moret, Soenke Ziesche, Peter Singer, AI Alignment: The Case for Including Animals, 2025

To achieve this while maintaining practical implementability, we recommend that leading AI companies include the following kinds of principles in the normative instructions used to align their frontier models: ... Refer to all potentially sentient animals as deserving of intrinsic moral consideration while maintaining a balanced, non-preachy tone. Check and try to avoid outputs that completely disregard animal welfare concerns.

Yip Fai Tse, Adrià Moret, Soenke Ziesche, Peter Singer, AI Alignment: The Case for Including Animals, 2025
