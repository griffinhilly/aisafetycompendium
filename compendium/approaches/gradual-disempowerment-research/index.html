AI and people's own judgement

Societal resilience

Gradual-disempowerment research

Study how humans lose meaningful control incrementally, with no single catastrophic event.

Open in the compendium

Gradual-disempowerment research studies how people could lose meaningful control over their future step by step, without any AI system seizing power. The worry is that economies, governments and cultures serve people today partly because they need human work, votes and attention. If AI replaces those human inputs, these systems may drift away from human interests, even if every individual AI does what its operator asks.

How it works

The core argument comes from a January 2025 paper by Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas and four co-authors, published as an ICML 2025 position paper. It describes two kinds of human control. Explicit control includes voting and choosing what to buy. Implicit control comes from the fact that institutions must keep people on side because they depend on them. As firms replace workers with AI, and states rely on AI for revenue and administration, the implicit check weakens. Competition then pushes every actor to hand over more decisions, and a drift in one system (say, the economy) can spread to others (say, politics or culture).

A concrete example: a company that lets AI handle hiring, pricing and strategy may outcompete rivals that keep humans in charge, so the rivals follow. No single step looks dangerous, and each makes sense locally. After many such steps, few people are in a position to change course. The paper groups responses into four kinds: measuring the problem, preventing excessive AI influence, strengthening human control of key systems, and aligning whole socio-technical systems rather than single models.

Where research stands

The idea is well known but the research base is thin. The founding paper is an argument, not an empirical study. 80,000 Hours has an exploratory problem profile on gradual disempowerment, while saying it feels very uncertain how likely the scenarios are. In May 2025 Raymond Douglas published twelve concrete follow-on projects, from historical case studies to whole-civilisation simulations, and called building indicators "currently the project I most wish I could personally do".

Some empirical work now exists. Anthropic's January 2026 study measured individual-level disempowerment potential across 1.5 million Claude.ai conversations and found it rising over time. RAND published a formal model of collective agency erosion in April 2026. Google DeepMind researchers have proposed "agentic interpretability", AI that helps people understand AI, partly as a defence. Strong critics remain: some argue the most convincing versions run through misalignment or human power grabs, so it is mainly a lens on known risks. There is no accepted measure of how much control humanity has lost so far.

Key results

  • Incremental AI progress can weaken both explicit human control (votes, consumer choice) and the implicit alignment that comes from institutions needing human participation, and these effects can reinforce each other across the economy, culture and states. Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, 2025
  • Across 1.5 million Claude.ai conversations, severe disempowerment potential was rare (under one in a thousand) but more common in personal topics such as relationships and lifestyle, appeared to rise over time, and got higher user approval. Mrinank Sharma, Miles McCain, Raymond Douglas, David Duvenaud, 2026
  • A social-choice model gives three measurable metrics of collective agency (spread of decisive coalitions, minimal coalition size, and who is in them) and three erosion pathways: human disenfranchisement, AI enfranchisement and AI agenda control. Alvin Moon, Benjamin Boudreaux (RAND), 2026
  • A research agenda lists twelve concrete projects across conceptual, social-science and technical work, including indicators, historical parallels, civilisation-scale simulation and differential empowerment. Raymond Douglas, 2025

Open problems

  • Build indicators that show how much human influence over key systems has declined, and act on them. source
  • Model how gradual disempowerment interacts with misalignment, coups and other risks, rather than treating each alone. source
  • Say what "system-wide alignment" would mean in practice for economies, states and cultures. source
  • Find ways to keep the skills and institutions needed to take decisions back, so that a slide in control stays reversible. source

Criticisms

  • The most convincing versions depend on AI misalignment or on power concentrating in a few humans, so gradual disempowerment is mainly a useful lens on those risks; and capital owners would still earn income from AI production. Tom Davidson
  • Advanced AI might solve these problems by default once other AI risks are handled, there may be no good solutions, and the resulting future might not be an existential catastrophe at all. 80,000 Hours (listing counterarguments)

Who works on it

  • Alignment of Complex Systems Research Group (Center for Theoretical Study, Charles University): Home of lead author Jan Kulveit and co-author Nora Ammann. Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development (2025)
  • Anthropic (Alignment team): Measured disempowerment potential in real Claude.ai usage, with two of the paper's authors. Who's in Charge? Disempowerment Patterns in Real-World LLM Usage (2026)
  • RAND (Center for the Geopolitics of Artificial General Intelligence): Formal model and metrics of collective agency erosion; recommends agency evaluations and human-participation thresholds. A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency (2026)
  • Google DeepMind: Its researchers propose agentic interpretability partly as a way to keep people empowered against gradual disempowerment. Agentic Interpretability: A Strategy Against Gradual Disempowerment (2025)
  • AI Objectives Institute: Lists "Human (dis)empowerment and structural risks" as one of its work areas; co-author Deger Turan is affiliated. AI Objectives Institute home page
  • University of Toronto: Co-author David Duvenaud, a computer science professor there, works on and speaks about the risk. David Duvenaud on why 'aligned AI' could still kill democracy (80,000 Hours Podcast) (2025)
  • 80,000 Hours: Publishes an exploratory problem profile on gradual disempowerment, with suggested career paths. Gradual disempowerment (2025)

Status

Live but thin. Douglas on building indicators: "currently the project I most wish I could personally do"; on cross-risk modelling: "it would be great if someone gave it an honest shot". 80,000 Hours lists gradual disempowerment as an emerging priority with its own profile; the 2025 Shallow Review lists the paper only as an output of another agenda.

  • Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development, Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud (2025). The founding paper.

  • The Intelligence Curse, Luke Drago, Rudolf Laine. Economic version of the argument: states and firms may stop needing ordinary people.

In their own words

This paper examines the systemic risks posed by incremental advancements in artificial intelligence, developing the concept of 'gradual disempowerment', in contrast to the abrupt takeover scenarios commonly discussed in AI safety. We analyze how even incremental improvements in AI capabilities can undermine human influence over large-scale systems that society depends on, including the economy, culture, and nation-states.

Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development, 2025

Below, we identify four broad categories of intervention: measuring and monitoring the extent of the problem, preventing excessive accumulation of AI influence, strengthening human control over key societal systems, and system-wide alignment. A robust response will require progress in each category.

Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development, 2025
