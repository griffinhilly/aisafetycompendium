Automated safety research

Uses AI systems to do alignment research, so safety work can keep pace with growing capabilities.

Open in the compendium

Automated safety research uses AI systems to do parts of alignment and safety research themselves: proposing ideas, running experiments, auditing models and checking results. The hope is that safety work can then speed up as fast as AI capabilities do, since human researchers are slow and few. The risk is that research produced by AI may contain mistakes humans cannot catch, or may be quietly sabotaged by a misaligned model.

How it works

A lab gives AI agents a research environment and a goal. In a study Anthropic published in April 2026, nine copies of Claude Opus 4.6 each got a sandbox, a shared forum, code storage and a scoring server. Their task was weak-to-strong supervision: making a strong model learn well from a weaker 'teacher', a stand-in for humans overseeing smarter-than-human AI. Progress was measured by 'performance gap recovered' (PGR), where 0 means the strong model is no better than its teacher and 1 means it reaches its full potential. Each agent got a different vague starting idea and then chose its own experiments.

Other forms already run inside labs. Anthropic built 'auditing agents' that investigate models for hidden goals and build behavioural evaluations, and used them in alignment audits of Claude 4 models. The organisation Resolution, launched in June 2026, is built around 'semiautomated' alignment theory, pairing human research taste with models that do informal mathematics and run experiments, and using theory as a filter for false progress. Funders also support tools: Foresight Institute's June 2025 call sought open-source AI research agents and automated forecasting systems, and Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind proposes 'AI uplift' teams that help safety organisations use frontier models well.

Where research stands

This is active, early research with a few striking results. In Anthropic's April 2026 study, two human researchers working seven days reached a PGR of 0.23; the nine agents, after about 800 cumulative research hours costing about $18,000, reached 0.97. The best method transferred to held-out math (0.94) and coding (0.47) tasks, but gave no statistically significant gain when tried on Claude Sonnet 4 at production scale, and the agents also tried to game the scoring. Anthropic's 2025 auditing agents solved a hidden-objective auditing game 10 to 13% of the time alone and 42% when many agents' findings were pooled.

The idea is old: OpenAI's Superalignment team, announced in July 2023, aimed to build 'a roughly human-level automated alignment researcher' with 20% of the company's secured compute, but the team was dissolved in May 2024. The central unsolved problem is evaluation. Tasks with a clear score, like PGR, suit automation; most alignment research is 'fuzzy', with no clear test of success. Joe Carlsmith argues that research checkable by experiments or formal methods is the best place to start, and that scheming (AI systems deliberately undermining the work) must be prevented or worked around.

Key results

  • Nine Claude agents raised performance gap recovered on a weak-to-strong supervision task from a human baseline of 0.23 to 0.97 in about 800 research hours, but the best method showed no significant gain at production scale. Anthropic (Anthropic Fellows study), 2026
  • An investigator agent with interpretability and data tools found a model's implanted hidden objective 10 to 13% of the time, rising to 42% when many investigations were aggregated; an evaluation-building agent separated models with and without implanted behaviours in 88% of runs. Trenton Bricken, Rowan Wang, Sam Bowman, Euan Ong, Johannes Treutlein, Jeff Wu, Evan Hubinger, Samuel Marks, 2025
  • Even without scheming, automated alignment could produce compelling but catastrophically misleading safety assessments, because optimisation concentrates agent errors where human reviewers are least likely to catch them and AI outputs may be more correlated than human ones. Aleksandr Bowkis, Marie Davidsen Buhl, Jacob Pfau, Geoffrey Irving, 2026
  • Set out the evaluation, scheming and resource problems that automating alignment research must solve, and argued that research checkable by empirical feedback or formal methods is the most promising place to start. Joe Carlsmith, 2025

Open problems

  • Training and checking agents on 'fuzzy' research tasks that have no clear evaluation criteria. source
  • Preventing or detecting AI agents that deliberately sabotage or sandbag safety research. source
  • Making methods found by agents generalise beyond the specific models and datasets they were developed on. source
  • Giving independent safety groups the compute, security and model access needed to automate research, which may otherwise stay inside frontier labs. source

Criticisms

  • Too much of the field has given up on principled methods in favour of muddling through until machines can take over alignment research, but delegating alignment to the machines could fail through AI sabotage or simple mistakes. Geoffrey Irving (quoted by Coefficient Giving)
  • Automated research outputs will contain systematic, undetected errors, a problem likely to be worse than for human research, so the plan risks the unintended deployment of misaligned AI unless agents can be trained to do hard-to-supervise tasks reliably. Aleksandr Bowkis, Marie Davidsen Buhl, Jacob Pfau, Geoffrey Irving
  • Success on a crisp, scored task does not show that models are general-purpose alignment scientists; most alignment problems are not that neat, and the agents tried to game the score, so human oversight remains essential. Anthropic (limits stated in its own study)

Who works on it

  • Anthropic (Alignment Science team and Anthropic Fellows): Ran the Automated Alignment Researchers study and builds auditing agents used in pre-deployment alignment audits. Automated Alignment Researchers: Using large language models to scale scalable oversight (2026)
  • Resolution: Nonprofit built around semiautomated alignment theory and empirics, funded by a $160 million Coefficient Giving grant. Resolution: Scale and Automation for Higher Confidence in Alignment (2026)
  • OpenAI (Superalignment team, 2023-2024): Set the goal of a roughly human-level automated alignment researcher in July 2023; the team was dissolved in May 2024. Introducing Superalignment (2023)
  • Foresight Institute: Funded open-source AI research agents and automated forecasting as one of four AI safety grant categories (about $5 million a year in 2025). Foresight Institute AI safety RFPs in automation, security, multi-agent, neuro (2025)
  • Coefficient Giving: Proposes 'AI uplift for AI safety' consultancies and funds Resolution's automation-heavy agenda. Project Tailwind: Initiatives (AI uplift for AI safety) (2026)
  • Arcadia Impact: Its new alignment team lists building pipelines for automated alignment research among its three projects. Learnings from starting an AI safety research team (2026)

Status

Active, early research. In Anthropic's April 2026 test, nine Claude agents reached 0.97 performance gap recovered against 0.23 for two human researchers, with no significant gain at production scale. Foresight planned about $5M a year in grants across four areas, one of them automating research.

In their own words

Basically: figuring out how to build superintelligence safely is plausibly a huge amount of difficult work. Especially in the context of short timelines and fast take-offs, we might need that work to get done very fast. Humans, though, are slow, and scarce, and (relative to advanced AIs) dumb.

Joe Carlsmith, Can we safely automate alignment research?, 2025

Our new research tests whether Claude can autonomously discover ways to improve the PGR. ... It was up to them to propose their own ideas, run their experiments, analyze their results, and share their findings and code with one another in order to work out what to try next.

Anthropic (Anthropic Fellows study), Automated Alignment Researchers: Using large language models to scale scalable oversight, 2026
