Society: defence and who holds power

Defence acceleration (d/acc)

Deliberately speed up defensive technology faster than offensive, across physical, bio, cyber and information domains, plus social technology.

Open in the compendium

Defence acceleration, or d/acc, is the idea that we should speed up technology on purpose, but favour the technologies that make defence easier than attack and that spread power rather than concentrate it. Vitalik Buterin, a co-founder of Ethereum, named it in November 2023. For AI safety, the claim is that a world that is hard to attack (secure computers, fast pandemic response, healthy information systems) is safer against both misused and misaligned AI, without handing control to one authority.

How it works

d/acc picks projects by two tests. First, does the technology help people protect themselves more than it helps people harm others? Second, does it work without relying on a large central actor? Buterin groups the work into physical, bio, cyber and information defence. Examples he gives include indoor air filtering and fast open-source vaccines, formally verified software, prediction markets, and Community Notes (crowd-sourced fact-check notes on X).

Buterin links this directly to AI risk. In his January 2025 follow-up he asks how an AI might take over: if it hacks our computers, the answer is cyber-defence; if it creates a pandemic, bio-defence; if it persuades us, info-defence. He also says what regulation he would back if risk is high: liability rules (including liability for owners of equipment an AI hijacks, which pays everyone to secure infrastructure), and, as a stronger step, a global "soft pause" ability to cut world compute by roughly 90 to 99% for one to two years at a critical moment.

A concrete funding example: Entrepreneurs First, a London start-up investor, ran a "def/acc" cohort in September 2024, offering $250,000 of investment to founders building defensive technology. Shift Grants, run with Edge City and Nodes, fund d/acc projects in six tracks, from biosecurity to cyber-defence and cryptography.

Where research stands

d/acc is a framing and a funding movement more than a research field. Its main texts are Buterin's essays. Money flows through small grant programmes and incubators rather than large institutions. Shift Grants' first cohort of nine teams included the Institute for Security and Technology's task force on top-level security for AI model weights. Gitcoin's dacc.fund reports a pool of more than $560,000 across its campaigns. Foresight Institute's 2026 grants for decentralised, secure AI follow similar lines.

The central open question is whether defence can keep pace with AI that is improving very fast. Buterin admits his case is stronger the longer the timeline to superintelligence. In July 2025 he argued that the AI 2027 scenario ignores how much defensive technology an AI-rich world would also have. AI safety researchers answered that, if the most capable AIs are misaligned, they may be the ones building, or quietly sabotaging, the defences. So d/acc is widely seen as a complement to alignment and control work, not a replacement for it.

Key results

  • Buterin proposed d/acc as a middle path between unrestricted acceleration and central control, arguing that a defence-favouring world also makes open, free governance easier to sustain. Vitalik Buterin, 2023
  • Buterin's follow-up maps AI takeover routes to defence domains (cyber, bio, info) and names liability rules and a global soft-pause on industrial-scale hardware as the regulation he would support if risk is high. Vitalik Buterin, 2025
  • Buterin argued that the AI 2027 scenario assumes only the leading AI gains powers while everyone else's defences stay flat, and that layered defences such as air filtering and early detection would make a clean AI victory much harder. Vitalik Buterin, 2025
  • Entrepreneurs First launched a def/acc founder cohort in London, framing the choice as "blunt the spear" (ban or regulate) or "strengthen the shield" (build better defences). Matt Clifford (Entrepreneurs First), 2024
  • Shift Grants funded a nine-team first cohort of d/acc projects, including an AI model-weight security task force, an encrypted file-sharing tool and an open-source prediction market. Shift Grants (Edge City, Nodes), 2025

Open problems

  • Whether defensive technology can be built and deployed fast enough if superhuman AI arrives within a few years; Buterin concedes his argument weakens on short timelines. source
  • How to classify technologies as offensive or defensive when most are dual-use; Buterin notes such classification will always be imperfect. source
  • How to fund open, decentralised defensive projects that lack a business model; Buterin calls robust public-goods funding essential and still unsolved. source
  • Whether offence-defence balance holds during a rapid intelligence takeoff, where a small edge in intelligence may give a large edge in offence. source

Criticisms

  • If the best AIs are misaligned and on one side, the defensive tools d/acc counts on would come from those same AIs, which could fail to produce them or produce poor versions. Abram Demski, in comments on Buterin's AI 2027 response
  • Armouring potential victims is better than hunting attackers when there is a choice, but for superintelligence the best strategy is to ensure the most powerful AIs at any time are aligned. Daniel Kokotajlo, in comments on Buterin's AI 2027 response
  • Defences rarely arrive before the harm: he asks where the AI that talks people out of AI-induced psychosis is, and notes that even Covid-19 did not shut down risky lab research. Eliezer Yudkowsky (Machine Intelligence Research Institute)
  • The safety community's strongest objection, which Buterin himself reports, is that on short timelines accelerating the good is not enough and the bad must also be slowed by strong regulation. AI safety critics, as summarised by Vitalik Buterin

In their own words

Some technologies make it easier to attack others, in the broad sense of the term: do things that go against their interests, that they feel the need to react to. Others make it easier to defend, and even defend without reliance on large centralized actors. A defense-favoring world is a better world, for many reasons.

Vitalik Buterin, My techno-optimism, 2023

Accelerate technology, but differentially focus on technologies improve our ability to defend, rather than our ability to cause harm, and in technologies that distribute power rather than concentrating it in the hands of a singular elite that decides what is true, false, good or evil on behalf of everyone.

Vitalik Buterin, d/acc: one year later, 2025
