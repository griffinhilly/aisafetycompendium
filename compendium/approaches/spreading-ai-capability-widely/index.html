Society: defence and who holds power

Societal resilience

Spreading AI capability widely (diffusion)

Spread AI capability and control across many people and groups, so no single actor holds most of the power.

Open in the compendium

This approach spreads AI capability, and control over it, across many people, companies and countries so that no single actor can dominate. Supporters see it as the main defence against AI-enabled power grabs and against a future in which ordinary people lose their economic and political leverage. Critics answer that spreading the most powerful systems also spreads the ability to cause catastrophe, so the debate turns on what exactly is shared, with whom and when.

How it works

Diffusion comes in several forms. The strongest is releasing model weights openly, so anyone can run and modify a model; Meta made this case in July 2024, arguing open source keeps power from being "concentrated in the hands of a small number of companies". A milder form shares access to frontier systems with many independent parties: Forethought's 2025 report on AI-enabled coups recommends sharing frontier capabilities with multiple stakeholders and buying critical government AI from several providers. Other forms target infrastructure and loyalty: building compute that communities own, such as Foresight Institute's 2026 "local compute" grants, and AI "representatives" aligned to individual users rather than to a company.

Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine's 2025 essay series "The Intelligence Curse" gives the economic rationale. Countries rich in oil often neglect their citizens because their wealth does not depend on them. Likewise, once AI makes human labour unnecessary, states and firms may lose their incentive to invest in ordinary people. Their remedy has three parts: harden the world against catastrophes, "diffuse" AI into the hands of regular people, and democratise institutions.

A concrete example of public diffusion is Apertus, a fully open multilingual model from the Swiss AI Initiative (EPFL, ETH Zurich and the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre), with its training data, code and weights all documented.

Where research stands

Diffusion is happening in practice through open-weight models and national "sovereign AI" projects, but its safety case is still disputed. Even leading open-weight advocates have hedged: Meta's July 2025 "Personal Superintelligence" statement says it will need to be "careful about what we choose to open source" as systems become more capable. A 2023 report led by Elizabeth Seger concluded that some highly capable models should not be open-sourced, at least initially. It proposed other ways of sharing them instead.

Among safety researchers the live question is which kinds of diffusion are most useful. Ryan Greenblatt argued in May 2025 that local AI and open source are very hard to make competitive with big AI companies, and that forcing open-sourcing at high capability levels would be very dangerous. He favours targeted measures instead: mandatory interoperability so many actors can fine-tune and align frontier models, and aligned AI advisers for individuals. Funders such as Longview Philanthropy now support "building compute in democracies" as a middle path.

Key results

  • Once AI removes the need for human labour, powerful actors may lose their incentive to care about regular people; diffusing AI to individuals is one of three proposed remedies. Luke Drago, Rudolf Laine, 2025
  • Sharing access to frontier AI widely reduces the risk that a small group gains exclusive access to coup-enabling capabilities. Tom Davidson, Lukas Finnveden, Rose Hadshar (Forethought), 2025
  • Open foundation models offer benefits including distributing decision-making power, but current research cannot yet establish their marginal misuse risk. Sayash Kapoor, Rishi Bommasani, et al., Arvind Narayanan, 2024
  • For some highly capable future models, open-sourcing may pose risks extreme enough to outweigh its benefits; alternative sharing methods can preserve many open-source goals. Elizabeth Seger, et al., 2023
  • Targeted diffusion (mandatory interoperability for fine-tuning and alignment, plus aligned AI representatives for individuals) may beat broad diffusion or open-sourcing. Ryan Greenblatt, 2025

Criticisms

  • Forcing AI companies to open-source at high capability levels seems hard and very dangerous for misalignment, bioweapons and the pace of take-off. Ryan Greenblatt
  • Some highly capable models should not be open-sourced, at least initially, because the risks may outweigh the benefits. Elizabeth Seger and co-authors
  • Concentration could reduce some risks: with fewer actors there is less race pressure and fewer hands on dangerous capabilities. 80,000 Hours, setting out arguments against its own view

Who works on it

  • Forethought: Recommends sharing frontier capabilities with multiple stakeholders as a defence against AI-enabled coups. AI-Enabled Coups: How a Small Group Could Use AI to Seize Power (2025)
  • Meta: Releases open-weight Llama models and argues open source spreads power; hedged in 2025 on open-sourcing superintelligence. Open Source AI Is the Path Forward (2024)
  • Swiss AI Initiative (EPFL, ETH Zurich, CSCS): Built Apertus, a fully open, documented and reproducible foundation model for public use. Apertus (project page) (2026)
  • Foresight Institute: Funds compute that individuals or communities own and control, through its AI Nodes grants. AI for Science & Safety Nodes: Request for Proposals (2026)
  • Longview Philanthropy: Funds building compute in democracies as one of twelve areas against power concentration. Request for proposals on extreme power concentration (2026)

Status

Proposals in an essay series and a research report. The Intelligence Curse lists 'diffuse' as one of three goals. The Forethought report urges sharing capabilities with multiple independent stakeholders.

In their own words

To break the intelligence curse, we should chart a different path on the tech tree, building technology that lets us: ... Diffuse AI, to get it in the hands of regular people. In the short-term, build AI that augments human capabilities. In the long-term, align AI directly to individual users and give everyone control in the AI economy.

Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine, The Intelligence Curse (introduction), 2025

Another crucial way to empower multiple actors is to share access to frontier AI capabilities widely. This reduces the risk that a single person or small group gains exclusive access to the most powerful capabilities and leverages them to stage a coup.

Tom Davidson, Lukas Finnveden, Rose Hadshar (Forethought), AI-Enabled Coups: How a Small Group Could Use AI to Seize Power, 2025
