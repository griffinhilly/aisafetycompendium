Deciding what AI should value

Model specs, constitutions, character training

Give the model an explicit written statement of values and persona to train toward.

Open in the compendium

A model spec or constitution is a written document that says how an AI model should think, behave and set priorities. Character training is the set of training methods that try to make the model actually hold those values and traits, not just recite them. It matters for safety because it is a direct way developers tell a model what 'aligned' means, and because the documents are public, outsiders can read and argue with them.

How it works

A developer writes the document in plain language. OpenAI's Model Spec, for example, sets out a 'chain of command': instructions from OpenAI outrank instructions from a developer building on its platform, which outrank instructions from the end user. Anthropic's constitution for Claude, published in January 2026, asks Claude to be broadly safe first, broadly ethical second, compliant with Anthropic's guidelines third, and genuinely helpful fourth, and it explains the reasons for each priority instead of listing bare rules.

The document then shapes training. In Constitutional AI (Anthropic, December 2022), a model critiques and revises its own answers against a short list of principles, and AI-generated preference judgments replace most human labels. In Anthropic's character training (June 2024), Claude writes messages relevant to a trait such as curiosity, writes several replies, and ranks its own replies by how well they fit the trait; a preference model trained on those rankings then teaches the trait. OpenAI's 'deliberative alignment' (December 2024) trains its reasoning models to recall the relevant parts of the safety specification and reason over them before answering.

Where research stands

This approach is deployed at scale. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google all publish behaviour documents for their main assistants, and Anthropic and OpenAI release theirs under a CC0 public-domain dedication so anyone can reuse them. Anthropic says its constitution is used at several stages of training, including to generate synthetic training data and to rank candidate responses. In November 2025 the first open implementation of character training showed that trained-in personas held up better under adversarial prompting than personas set by a system prompt or by activation steering.

What does not yet work well is the link between the text and the behaviour. OpenAI states that its production models 'do not yet fully reflect the Model Spec'. A 2025 stress test of twelve frontier models found direct contradictions and ambiguous principles in OpenAI's spec, and found that the scenarios where models disagree most strongly predict such problems in the spec. Critics also question who gets to write these documents: a company drafts, revises and interprets its own constitution.

Key results

  • A model can be trained to be harmless but not evasive using only a short list of principles plus AI-generated feedback, with far fewer human labels. Yuntao Bai, Saurav Kadavath, Sandipan Kundu, Amanda Askell and others (Anthropic), 2022
  • Anthropic added character training to Claude 3, using Claude-generated messages and self-ranked responses to train a preference model that instils traits such as curiosity and open-mindedness. Anthropic, 2024
  • Teaching OpenAI's o-series models the text of their safety specification and training them to reason over it before answering improved robustness to jailbreaks while reducing over-refusals. Melody Y. Guan, Manas Joglekar, Eric Wallace and others (OpenAI), 2024
  • Across more than 300,000 value-trade-off scenarios given to twelve frontier models, high disagreement between models pointed to contradictions and ambiguities in the written specs, and was linked to 5 to 13 times higher rates of cases where all five OpenAI models failed to comply with OpenAI's spec. Jifan Zhang, Henry Sleight, Andi Peng, John Schulman, Esin Durmus, 2025
  • An open-source character-training pipeline applied to three open-weight models and 11 personas produced personas more robust to adversarial prompting than system prompts or activation steering, with little effect on general capabilities. Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert, Evan Hubinger, 2025

Open problems

  • Closing the gap between the written spec and what deployed models actually do; OpenAI says its production models do not yet fully reflect its spec. source
  • Finding and fixing contradictions and vague principles inside specs before they show up as inconsistent behaviour. source
  • Writing guidance that generalises to situations the authors did not foresee, which is why Anthropic moved from a list of standalone rules to explained reasons. source
  • Giving the people affected by a model a legitimate say in its values, when no democratic body has authority over these documents. source

Criticisms

  • Calling a company document a 'constitution' borrows the aura of public legitimacy without public accountability; the company drafts, revises and interprets it, while binding duties sit in commercial contracts. Lisa Klaassen and Ralph Schroeder (Lawfare, April 2026)
  • AI governance has a 'political community deficit': no democratic body is authorised to set these principles, and the 2026 Claude constitution leaves out military deployments, which run under different rules. Gilad Abiri (California Law Review Online, 2026)
  • The spec they tested (OpenAI's) contains direct contradictions and ambiguous principles, and models do not always comply with it, so a published spec does not show how a model actually behaves. Jifan Zhang, Henry Sleight, Andi Peng, John Schulman, Esin Durmus

Who works on it

  • Anthropic: Publishes Claude's constitution, uses it to generate training data and rank responses, and runs character training. Claude's new constitution (2026)
  • OpenAI: Publishes the Model Spec for its GPT models and trains reasoning models to reason over safety specifications (deliberative alignment). Model Spec (2026)
  • Google (Gemini app): Publishes policy guidelines stating how the Gemini app should behave and which outputs it should avoid. Gemini app policy guidelines
  • Thinking Machines Lab: Co-authored (with Anthropic researchers) the large-scale stress test of model specs across twelve frontier models. Stress-Testing Model Specs Reveals Character Differences among Language Models (2025)
  • MATS: Lists the first open character-training implementation as research by one of its fellows. Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI (2025)

In their own words

The goal of character training is to make Claude begin to have more nuanced, richer traits like curiosity, open-mindedness, and thoughtfulness. ... Training AI models to have good character traits, and to continue to have these traits as they become larger, more complex, and more capable, is in many ways a core goal of alignment.

Anthropic, Claude's character, 2024

The only human oversight is provided through a list of rules or principles, and so we refer to the method as 'Constitutional AI'. The process involves both a supervised learning and a reinforcement learning phase. In the supervised phase we sample from an initial model, then generate self-critiques and revisions, and then finetune the original model on revised responses.

Yuntao Bai et al. (Anthropic), Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback, 2022
