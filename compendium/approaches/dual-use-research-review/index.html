Holding AI companies to account

Constraining

Dual-use research review

Funders and institutions screen research for misuse risk before they fund or allow it.

Open in the compendium

Dual-use research review means that funders and research institutions check proposed research for misuse risk before they fund or allow it. 'Dual use' describes work that can help or harm, such as research that could advance medicine but also be misapplied. The system is most developed in the life sciences, and the safety question for AI is whether a similar review should cover AI research and AI models that could give serious help to people seeking to cause harm.

How it works

In the United States, review is tied to federal funding. Under the policy approved by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy on 20 July 2026, the 'United States Government Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research', each party in the research process must review proposed federally funded work and attest that it is not in a prohibited high-risk category before funding or work proceeds. Institutions receiving federal life-sciences funding must set up an Institutional Review Entity, and work that may be high-risk gets a risk-benefit assessment and mitigation plan before a single independent third-party review board serving all agencies. The new policy judges research by the risk of its potential consequences rather than by a fixed list of regulated agents.

AI research has lighter, mostly voluntary versions. Stanford's Ethics and Society Review makes researchers in an AI funding programme describe societal risks, including dual use, and mitigation plans before they receive grants. The Partnership on AI's 2021 publication recommendations ask conferences and journals to add downstream consequences to peer review and to create separate risk-based review processes. The 'Responsible AI x Biodesign' statement, published in March 2024 and now signed by about 200 scientists, commits signatories among other things to evaluate protein-design software for safety and security risks and to limit release when meaningful risks remain.

Where research stands

Formal review exists in biology but has been in flux. The 2024 US policy on dual use research of concern and pathogens with enhanced pandemic potential was overtaken by events: Executive Order 14292 of 5 May 2025 superseded it, and on 7 May 2025 the National Institutes of Health rescinded its implementation notice and paused research meeting the order's definition. The replacement policy was approved on 20 July 2026. It applies to federally funded research.

For AI the approach is early and largely proposed. A National Academies workshop in April 2025 noted that dual-use policies 'focus on physical laboratory work' and do not cover information produced by computational and AI-driven studies well. Researchers have proposed adapting the dual-use framework to large language models and to biological AI models, but the new US policy covers only federally funded life-sciences research.

Key results

  • A White House policy approved on 20 July 2026 replaced the 2024 dual-use policy, requiring attestation and review before federally funded high-risk life-sciences research proceeds and creating an independent third-party review body. White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (page hosted by ASPR, HHS), 2026
  • NIH rescinded its implementation of the 2024 policy on 7 May 2025 after an executive order superseded it, and paused research meeting the order's definition until a new policy was issued. National Institutes of Health, 2025
  • In its first year, a funding-linked ethics review of 41 AI research proposals at Stanford flagged dual use among its most common concerns; 58% of surveyed researchers said it influenced their project design. Michael S. Bernstein, Margaret Levi, David Magnus, Betsy Rajala, Debra Satz, Charla Waeiss, 2021
  • Current dual-use policies focus on physical laboratory work and do not effectively address risks from information produced by computational and AI-driven biology; workshop participants suggested tiered oversight and early risk evaluation. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, 2025
  • About 200 protein-design scientists committed to evaluate their AI software for safety and security risks and to limit release when meaningful risks remain. Responsible AI x Biodesign signatories, 2024

Open problems

  • Extending review from physical lab work to computational research, AI models and the data they produce. source
  • Covering research that is not federally funded, including most frontier AI development by private companies. source
  • Defining which AI research or models count as dual-use 'of concern' without sweeping in ordinary work. source
  • Building methods to evaluate the dual-use capabilities of AI models trained on biological data. source

Criticisms

  • Pausing research while policy is rewritten has an impact disproportionate to the problem and could drive the 'best and brightest' away from the field; 'Of particular concern in the Executive Order is the pausing of research while new policies are developed.'. American Society for Microbiology
  • The dual-use framework's known drawbacks in biology mean that adapting it to AI requires careful balance between oversight and research freedom. Alexei Grinbaum and Laurynas Adomaitis
  • The 2025 executive order left implementation deadlines, definitions and coordination with existing guidelines unclear; "The Executive Order still requires clarification in several areas." American Society for Microbiology

Who works on it

  • White House Office of Science and Technology Policy: Issued the July 2026 US policy that governs review of high-risk federally funded life-sciences research. United States Government Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research (2026)
  • National Institutes of Health: Implements federal dual-use research policy for its grants; rescinded its 2024-policy implementation in May 2025. Implementation Update: Improving the Safety and Security of Biological Research (NOT-OD-25-112) (2025)
  • National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine: Convened a workshop on risks of publishing computational and AI-driven biological research. Disseminating In Silico and Computational Biological Research: Navigating Benefits and Risks (2025)
  • Stanford University (Ethics and Society Review): Runs the Ethics and Society Review, which makes risk reflection, including dual use, a condition of AI research funding. ESR: Ethics and Society Review of Artificial Intelligence Research (2021)
  • Partnership on AI: Recommended risk-based review processes at AI conferences and journals. Managing the Risks of AI Research: Six Recommendations for Responsible Publication (2021)
  • American Society for Microbiology: Engages on federal oversight of risky life-sciences research and criticised the 2025 research pause. Pausing Research Not a Path to Better Biosafety & Biosecurity (2025)

Status

Exists in biology. NIH withdrew its January 2025 implementation notice in May 2025 after a new executive order. A new US policy on high-risk life sciences research, approved on 20 July 2026, replaces the 2024 policy.

In their own words

"Dual use research of concern (DURC)" is life sciences research that, based on current understanding, can be reasonably anticipated to provide knowledge, information, products, or technologies that could be misapplied to do harm with no, or only minor, modification to pose a significant threat with potential consequences to public health and safety ... or national security.

White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, United States Government Policy for Oversight of Dual Use Research of Concern and Pathogens with Enhanced Pandemic Potential, 2024

If the research is assessed to be within scope of Category 1 or Category 2, the research institution, through an IRE, conducts risk-benefit assessments and develops a draft risk mitigation plan for the conduct and communication of research. The PI or research institution submits the risk-benefit assessment and a draft risk mitigation plan to the federal funding agency.

White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, United States Government Policy for Oversight of Dual Use Research of Concern and Pathogens with Enhanced Pandemic Potential, 2024
