Looking inside the model

Evaluating

Mechanistic interpretability

Reverse-engineer the network's internals into human-readable circuits and algorithms.

Open in the compendium

Mechanistic interpretability tries to reverse-engineer a trained neural network: to find the internal parts it uses and the step-by-step algorithm those parts carry out. For safety, the hope is to check what a model is actually doing inside, rather than trusting only its outputs, so that hidden goals or deception could be caught before deployment.

How it works

A language model is a huge grid of numbers. When it reads text, each layer produces a list of numbers called activations. Researchers look for 'features' (patterns in the activations that stand for a concept, such as 'Texas') and 'circuits' (chains of features connected by the model's weights that together perform a task).

A well-known early example is a 2022 study of GPT-2 small on 'indirect object identification': completing a sentence with the name of the person who receives something, rather than the person who gives it. The researchers used causal interventions (copying or switching off internal parts and watching the output change) to find the circuit behind this: 26 attention heads in 7 groups.

One method Anthropic now uses is circuit tracing. Researchers train a simpler 'replacement model' whose parts are easier to read, run one prompt through it, and draw an 'attribution graph' showing which features pushed which. They then test the story by changing those features in the real model. In a March 2025 study of Claude 3.5 Haiku, this showed the model working out 'Texas' as a middle step when asked for the capital of the state containing Dallas, and choosing a rhyme word before writing a line of poetry.

Where research stands

The field has moved from toy models and small circuits to partial maps of production models. Anthropic's attribution graphs give useful insight on a minority of prompts: the authors report satisfying insight for about a quarter of the prompts they tried, and they stress that the replacement model captures the original only imperfectly. Open tools now exist: in May 2025 Anthropic open-sourced its circuit-tracing library with a Neuronpedia viewer for open-weight models such as Gemma-2-2b. OpenAI has tried a different route, training models whose weights are mostly zero so their circuits are smaller and easier to read; scaling this beyond tens of millions of non-zero parameters is still unsolved.

Ambitions have split. Anthropic's CEO set a goal in April 2025 that interpretability can reliably detect most model problems by 2027. Google DeepMind's interpretability team said in December 2025 that it had moved away from complete reverse-engineering toward 'pragmatic' work on concrete safety problems, starting with the simplest methods that work. Training programmes have also shifted: MATS reduced the share of interpretability research in its Winter 2025 cohort. Money has grown at the same time: the interpretability company Goodfire raised $150 million in February 2026.

Key results

  • A circuit of 26 attention heads in 7 classes explains how GPT-2 small completes sentences that need the indirect object's name, found with causal interventions. Kevin Wang, Alexandre Variengien, Arthur Conmy, Buck Shlegeris, Jacob Steinhardt, 2022
  • Attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku showed real two-step reasoning (Dallas, then Texas, then Austin) and planning of rhyme words before a line is written. Jack Lindsey et al. (Anthropic), 2025
  • The same study found cases where the model's written reasoning did not match its internal computation, showing that internal tracing can expose made-up explanations. Jack Lindsey et al. (Anthropic), 2025
  • Training transformers with mostly zero weights produced task circuits whose neurons map to natural concepts, at some cost in capability. Leo Gao, Achyuta Rajaram, Jacob Coxon, Soham V. Govande, Bowen Baker, Dan Mossing (OpenAI), 2025
  • Switching off neurons tied to spurious concepts (such as Bible verses) fixed Llama-3.1-8B's error on 'Which is bigger, 9.8 or 9.11?' and raised accuracy on similar comparisons from 55% to 76%. Transluce, 2024

Open problems

  • Replacement models used for circuit tracing do not fully reproduce the original model, so the resulting graphs are hypotheses, not proof. source
  • Methods do not yet show that a dangerous mechanism is absent; finding nothing may mean the tool missed it. source
  • The field still needs clearer concepts, better methods and ways to aim them at specific safety goals, per a 29-author survey of open problems. source
  • Interpretable-by-design models (such as weight-sparse transformers) have not been scaled beyond tens of millions of non-zero parameters while staying readable. source

Criticisms

  • Large models are complex systems whose behaviour emerges from many weak interactions, so compressing them into human-readable mechanisms will lose what matters; top-down representation engineering is the better bet. Dan Hendrycks and Laura Hiscott (AI Frontiers, May 2025)
  • Interpretability is unlikely to give high-reliability detection of deceptive AI; it should be one layer in a defence-in-depth portfolio, not the thing that saves us. Neel Nanda (May 2025)
  • Complete reverse-engineering is only one bet among several; work should aim at concrete safety tasks and start with the simplest methods, such as prompting, steering and probing. Google DeepMind mechanistic interpretability team (December 2025)

Who works on it

  • Anthropic (interpretability team): Develops circuit tracing and attribution graphs and applies them to Claude models. On the Biology of a Large Language Model (2025)
  • Google DeepMind (mechanistic interpretability team): Publishes open interpretability tools for Gemma and argues for a pragmatic, safety-task-driven agenda. A Pragmatic Vision for Interpretability (2025)
  • OpenAI: Trains weight-sparse models whose circuits are small enough to read. Weight-sparse transformers have interpretable circuits (2025)
  • Goodfire: Interpretability company whose researchers co-authored Stochastic Parameter Decomposition, which splits a model's weights into sparsely used parts. Stochastic Parameter Decomposition (2025)
  • Transluce: Nonprofit lab that built AI-written descriptions of every MLP neuron in Llama-3.1-8B and a tool to inspect and steer them. Monitor: An AI-Driven Observability Interface (2024)
  • Decode Research (Neuronpedia): Built the Neuronpedia front end for Anthropic's open-source circuit-tracing library. Open-sourcing circuit tracing tools (2025)
  • MATS: Research training programme that has run many interpretability projects; it cut interpretability's share in Winter 2025. MATS Winter 2025 (2025)
  • Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving): Its 2025 technical AI safety request for proposals (about $40M) included interpretability areas such as finding feature representations and interpretability benchmarks. Open Philanthropy Technical AI Safety RFP - $40M Available (2025)

Status

Named in 6 of the 7 source groups. For its Winter 2025 cohort, MATS "deliberately rebalanced its research portfolio, reducing the proportion of interpretability research".

In their own words

Mechanistic interpretability seeks to reverse engineer neural networks, similar to how one might reverse engineer a compiled binary computer program. After all, neural network parameters are in some sense a binary computer program which runs on one of the exotic virtual machines we call a neural network architecture.

Chris Olah (Anthropic, Transformer Circuits Thread), Mechanistic Interpretability, Variables, and the Importance of Interpretable Bases, 2022

Features are the fundamental unit of neural networks. They correspond to directions. ... Features are connected by weights, forming circuits. A "circuit" is a computational subgraph of a neural network. It consists of a set of features, and the weighted edges that go between them in the original network.

Chris Olah, Nick Cammarata, Ludwig Schubert, Gabriel Goh, Michael Petrov, Shan Carter (Distill), Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits, 2020
