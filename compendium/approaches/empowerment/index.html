Theory: the maths of agents and goals

Aligned models

Empowerment (information-theoretic)

Measure how much future control an agent has, and reward an assistant for increasing the human's control.

Open in the compendium

Empowerment is a measure of how much control an agent has over its future: how many different outcomes it can reliably bring about through its own actions. Researchers have turned it around for safety, asking an AI assistant to increase the human's empowerment instead of guessing the human's goal. The appeal is that an assistant can help someone keep their options open without knowing exactly what they want.

How it works

Picture a person in a hallway blocked by heavy boxes. An assistant that tries to guess where the person is going may guess wrong. An assistant that raises the person's empowerment simply clears the boxes, so the person can reach many more places. Then the person chooses. The idea comes from Alexander Klyubin, Daniel Polani and Chrystopher Nehaniv at the University of Hertfordshire in 2005. They defined empowerment using information theory, the mathematics of signals and noise. It is the channel capacity between an agent's actions and what it later senses. Channel capacity here means the most information the agent's actions can reliably put into its future observations. Many distinct, predictable results mean high empowerment. Being stuck, or acting with random effects, means low empowerment.

In assistance work, the AI's reward includes a bonus for the human's empowerment. A June 2020 study (Du and colleagues, NeurIPS 2020) tested this in a gridworld and in a shared-control version of the Lunar Lander game with 20 participants. Stabilising the craft emerged from the empowerment bonus without hand-written rules, and participants reported more stability. Computing true empowerment is very expensive, so that study used a cheap stand-in: how varied the possible end states were.

Where research stands

Empowerment is a well-defined quantity with a twenty-year record as an intrinsic motivation, meaning a reward an agent gives itself for exploring. Its use as an assistance target is newer and growing. In November 2024 a Berkeley and Princeton team scaled empowerment-based assistance to Overcooked, a cooperative cooking game, by estimating empowerment with contrastive successor representations, a learned summary of where actions lead. In October 2025 the same group fine-tuned language models to maximise a simulated programmer's empowerment, using only offline text. In an 18-person study, users preferred this assistant 78% of the time over a strong baseline. Jobst Heitzig of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research and Ram Potham have proposed richer measures of long-term human power that are fair across many people.

The main weakness is that more control is not always what people want. In the 2020 Lunar Lander study, a copilot that weighted empowerment too heavily overrode the pilot and just hovered. Critics note that people sometimes choose to limit their own options, and that a plain control measure says nothing about which kinds of control matter. The evidence cited here comes from games, a coding simulator and small user studies.

Key results

  • Empowerment was defined as the channel capacity of an agent's action-to-sensor loop, a single task-independent measure of control. Alexander S. Klyubin, Daniel Polani, Chrystopher L. Nehaniv, 2005
  • An assistant rewarded for human empowerment helped more than goal-inference assistants when the goal set was large or wrong, and in a 20-person Lunar Lander study raised the average landing success rate from 17% to 33%. Yuqing Du, Stas Tiomkin, Emre Kiciman, Daniel Polani, Pieter Abbeel, Anca Dragan, 2020
  • Contrastive successor representations estimate empowerment at scale, and an assistant built on them beat prior methods and worked in the cooperative game Overcooked. Vivek Myers, Evan Ellis, Sergey Levine, Benjamin Eysenbach, Anca Dragan, 2024
  • Language models fine-tuned to maximise a user's empowerment were preferred 78% of the time in an 18-person study and raised simulated coding success by 192% on average over a supervised baseline. Evan Ellis, Vivek Myers, Jens Tuyls, Sergey Levine, Anca Dragan, Benjamin Eysenbach, 2025
  • A long-term, inequality- and risk-averse aggregate of human power can be designed by a partially axiomatic approach and computed by backward induction or approximated with multi-agent reinforcement learning. Jobst Heitzig, Ram Potham, 2025

Open problems

  • Compute or estimate empowerment cheaply in large, realistic settings without crude proxies. source
  • Balance empowerment against the person's actual goals, so the assistant does not override them to keep options open. source
  • Aggregate empowerment fairly across many people whose control can conflict. source
  • Distinguish the kinds of control that matter to people from control in general. source

Criticisms

  • Maximising empowerment can conflict with what people want, such as an environmentalist who volunteers to be tied to a tree in a protest, so human empowerment is not a safe optimisation target on its own. Stephen Zhao and Steven Byrnes (comments on 'Empowerment is (almost) all we need')
  • When the empowerment weight was set too high, the copilot overrode the human pilot and focused only on hovering. Yuqing Du et al. (the method's own authors)

Who works on it

  • University of Hertfordshire (Daniel Polani's group): Originated empowerment and co-authored the Assistance via Empowerment study. Empowerment -- an Introduction (2013)
  • UC Berkeley (Anca Dragan and Sergey Levine's groups): Developed empowerment-based assistance from gridworlds to language-model agents. Training LLM Agents to Empower Humans (2025)
  • Princeton University (Benjamin Eysenbach's group): Co-developed scalable empowerment estimates using contrastive representations. Learning to Assist Humans without Inferring Rewards (2024)
  • Microsoft Research: A researcher co-authored the Assistance via Empowerment paper. AvE: Assistance via Empowerment (2020)
  • Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research: Jobst Heitzig develops fair, long-term metrics of human power as an AI objective. Model-Based Soft Maximization of Suitable Metrics of Long-Term Human Power (2025)

Status

Established. Empowerment is a single number for how much control an agent has, with no breakdown of which kinds of control matter. Assistance work first tested it in small simulations (a gridworld and a shared-control lander game, 2020), then in the cooperative game Overcooked (NeurIPS 2024) and in language-model coding assistants with an 18-person user study (October 2025).

Start reading

  • Empowerment -- an Introduction, Christoph Salge, Cornelius Glackin, Daniel Polani (2013). The standard introduction to the concept.
  • AvE: Assistance via Empowerment, Yuqing Du et al. (2020). Proposes empowerment as an assistance target, with a clear hallway example.
  • Empowerment is (almost) all we need, Jacob Cannell (2022). A case for empowerment as an alignment target, with sharp objections in the comments.
  • Training LLM Agents to Empower Humans, Evan Ellis et al. (2025). By its authors' account, the first to train language-model agents to empower humans.

In their own words

This book chapter is an introduction to and an overview of the information-theoretic, task independent utility function "Empowerment", which is defined as the channel capacity between an agent's actions and an agent's sensors. It quantifies how much influence and control an agent has over the world it can perceive.

Christoph Salge, Cornelius Glackin, Daniel Polani, Empowerment -- an Introduction, 2013

We propose a new paradigm for assistance by instead increasing the human's ability to control their environment, and formalize this approach by augmenting reinforcement learning with human empowerment. This task-agnostic objective preserves the person's autonomy and ability to achieve any eventual state.

Yuqing Du, Stas Tiomkin, Emre Kiciman, Daniel Polani, Pieter Abbeel, Anca Dragan, AvE: Assistance via Empowerment, 2020
