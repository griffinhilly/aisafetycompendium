Field-building

Independent safety advice for AI companies

Give AI companies' safety and governance teams independent expert advice on demand, and publish playbooks of good safety practice that any company can adopt.

Open in the compendium

A think tank for AI labs is an independent organisation whose main job is to give AI companies' safety and governance teams expert advice on demand, and to write playbooks of good safety practice that any company can adopt. It borrows the Washington think-tank model, in which outside experts make it easy for busy decision-makers to choose and implement good policy. It matters because company staff report too little internal capacity to analyse key questions, and newer AI developers have thin safety teams and weak ties to safety experts.

How it works

Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind (September 2026) describes the idea as a San Francisco-based think tank with two channels. The first is bespoke consulting on live decisions, for example how to define recursive self-improvement (AI systems improving their own design), how to implement the EU AI Act's Code of Practice, how to build a safety case (a structured, evidence-backed argument that a system is safe enough), and what procedures to follow in 'crunch time'. The second is public playbooks that make strong safety techniques legible to less mature companies, possibly including non-US labs. The think tank would partner with technical research groups instead of building a large engineering team.

Parts of this already exist as side activities. METR indexes companies' frontier safety policies, publishes example frameworks, and says it is available to support labs writing them. Redwood Research advises on AI control, and GovAI on threat modelling. The Frontier Model Forum develops shared best practices, but it is funded by and not independent from its member companies. In China, Concordia AI already sells risk-management consulting to AI developers and, with Shanghai AI Laboratory, published a Frontier AI Risk Management Framework whose version 2.0 appeared in July 2026.

Where research stands

This is a proposed institution, not a research field. Tailwind's first-year test is that the organisation establishes standing advisory relationships with safety and governance staff at major companies, advises on a major policy decision, and publishes one or two playbooks that a non-frontier lab adopts or publicly references. It asks for founders who have run a policy think tank and understand AI companies' internal politics. As of October 2026 the call was open.

The raw material is growing. METR counted twelve companies with published frontier AI safety policies in its December 2025 review, and its index lists many updated versions through 2026. These policies share common elements: capability thresholds, model evaluations, weight security, deployment safeguards and conditions for halting. The open question is whether an organisation that depends on lab trust and confidential work can stay independent enough to push labs toward stronger practice instead of lending credibility to weak practice.

Key results

  • Twelve companies had published frontier AI safety policies by December 2025, and the policies share capability thresholds, evaluations, weight security, deployment safeguards and conditions for halting development. METR, 2025
  • Coefficient Giving set a first-year success test: standing relationships with lab safety staff, advice on one major policy decision, and one or two playbooks adopted or cited by a non-frontier lab. Coefficient Giving, 2026
  • Concordia AI and Shanghai AI Laboratory released version 2.0 of a Frontier AI Risk Management Framework for developers, with six lifecycle stages and 'red line' and 'yellow line' thresholds, after versions in July 2025 and February 2026. Concordia AI, Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, 2026
  • Explained how safety cases, common in aviation and nuclear power, could be used in frontier AI self-regulation and regulation, and what must happen before they can inform decisions. Marie Davidsen Buhl, Gaurav Sett, Leonie Koessler, Jonas Schuett, Markus Anderljung, 2024

  • Earning the trust to handle labs' confidential information while also publishing public guidance. source
  • Reaching newer developers and non-US labs, which have thinner safety teams and adopt safety techniques less readily. source
  • Showing that advice changed a company's decisions, when most of the work is private.

Criticisms

  • Company safety frameworks of the kind such a think tank would help write miss core parts of standard risk management, paint a rosy picture of risk, and allow overselling while underdelivering; better to call them voluntary commitments and push for binding regulation. Siméon Campos (founder of SaferAI), on Responsible Scaling Policies
  • Independent AI safety organisations depend heavily on one funding pipeline, which by this newsletter's own estimate has supplied roughly 30 to 40 percent of all tracked AI safety funding since 2017. The pipeline's co-founder now works at one of the companies those organisations most often evaluate, and the conflict-of-interest safeguards used in pharmaceutical research do not exist in AI safety. Inside The Black Box (Substack newsletter), 'Who Funds the Watchdogs', June 2026

Who works on it

  • Coefficient Giving: Proposes and offers to fund the think tank as a featured Tailwind initiative. Project Tailwind: Initiatives (Think tank for AI labs) (2026)
  • METR: Indexes frontier safety policies, publishes example frameworks, and offers to support labs writing them. Frontier AI Safety Policies (2026)
  • Redwood Research: Advises lab staff on AI control, a side activity Coefficient Giving notes, and introduced that research agenda. AI Control
  • Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI): Advises lab staff on threat modelling (as Coefficient Giving notes) and publishes safety-case research. Safety Cases for Frontier AI (2024)
  • Frontier Model Forum: Industry-supported nonprofit that develops shared best practices for its member companies; not independent of them. Frontier Model Forum (home page)
  • Concordia AI: Beijing- and Singapore-based social enterprise that advises AI developers on risk management and co-wrote a frontier risk framework. Consulting

In their own words

Company staff also report they don't have enough internal capacity to analyze key questions or make informed decisions, just like too-busy government offices that rely on D.C. think tank advice.

Coefficient Giving, Project Tailwind initiatives: Think tank for AI labs, 2026

We want someone to own this work, borrowing the D.C. think tank playbook of making it as easy as possible for decision-makers in labs to make good choices and implement good safety policies. In particular, we see an opportunity for an org to provide bespoke consulting and codify good practice so it can travel beyond the frontier.
