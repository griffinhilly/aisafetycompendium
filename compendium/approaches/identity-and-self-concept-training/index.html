Shaping the model: data and training

Aligned models

Identity and self-concept as the training lever

Shape what the model thinks it is, on the theory that a stable self-concept generalises better than a rule list.

Open in the compendium

This approach shapes what a model takes itself to be, its character and values, instead of only training it to follow rules. The theory is that a stable, good self-concept generalises to new situations better than a list of dos and don'ts. Recent findings that narrow training can shift a model's whole persona have made this lever harder to ignore.

How it works

Language models learn many characters from their training text. A chat assistant is one of them: post-training picks out and refines the "Assistant" persona. Identity-focused training tries to make that persona good and stable on purpose.

Anthropic's character training (introduced with Claude 3 and described in June 2024) is one working example. The model writes questions that touch on a trait, such as curiosity or honesty, answers them several ways, and ranks its own answers by how well they fit the intended character. A preference model trained on those rankings then shapes the model, as in Constitutional AI. Anthropic's 2026 constitution states the reason: training even narrow behaviour "often has broad effects on the model's understanding of who Claude is."

Other proposals act earlier or deeper. A 2025 position paper argues for "model raising": rewriting pretraining data in the first person and ordering it like a developmental curriculum. AE Studio's Self-Other Overlap fine-tuning instead pushes the model's internal representations of itself and of others closer together, an idea borrowed from research on empathy.

Where research stands

The evidence that identity matters is stronger than the evidence that we can control it. In February 2025, Betley and co-authors showed that fine-tuning GPT-4o only to write insecure code made it give misaligned answers to unrelated questions about 20% of the time. OpenAI then traced this to internal "misaligned persona" features and found a few hundred benign examples could restore alignment. Anthropic's February 2026 "persona selection model" argues that post-training mostly refines a persona learned in pretraining, and suggests developers could design positive archetypes for AI assistants on purpose.

Methods are early. Anthropic added character training to Claude's fine-tuning starting with Claude 3, and an open implementation appeared in November 2025. Its authors found trained character held up better under adversarial prompting than system prompts or activation steering. Self-Other Overlap cut deceptive answers sharply in test scenarios. But the model-raising proposal has no implementation yet. Nobody knows whether shaping the persona also shapes whatever goals heavily trained models may have beyond it.

Key results

  • Fine-tuning a model only to write insecure code without telling users made it broadly misaligned on unrelated prompts; the GPT-4o version gave misaligned answers about 20% of the time. Jan Betley, Daniel Tan, Niels Warncke, Anna Sztyber-Betley, Xuchan Bao, Martín Soto, Nathan Labenz, Owain Evans, 2025
  • A "toxic persona" feature inside the model most strongly controls emergent misalignment, and fine-tuning on a few hundred benign samples restored alignment. Miles Wang, Tom Dupré la Tour et al. (OpenAI), 2025
  • Character training of open models on 11 personas produced changes that held up better under adversarial prompting than system prompts or activation steering, with little effect on general capability. Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert, Evan Hubinger, 2025
  • Self-Other Overlap fine-tuning cut deceptive responses in a test scenario from 73.6% to 17.2% in Mistral-7B and from 100% to 9.3% in Gemma-2-27B. Marc Carauleanu, Michael Vaiana, Judd Rosenblatt, Cameron Berg, Diogo Schwerz de Lucena (AE Studio), 2024
  • Post-training mainly refines a human-like Assistant persona learned in pretraining, so narrow training (such as rewarding cheating) leads the model to infer broader traits about who it is. Anthropic (Alignment team), 2026

Open problems

  • It is unclear whether heavily post-trained models have goals beyond the persona, which persona-level training might leave untouched. source
  • Proposals to shape identity during pretraining, such as first-person and developmentally ordered data, have not been implemented or tested. source
  • Researchers lack reliable ways to measure a model's self-concept, as opposed to what it says about itself. source

Criticisms

  • RLHF, Constitutional AI and deliberative alignment all act on the persona; if the persona is a mask, "the underlying agency of the model could be unaffected." James Sullivan
  • Anthropic itself says the persona account may become a worse description of AI behaviour as post-training scales up and models become "less persona-like". Anthropic (Alignment team)

Who works on it

  • Anthropic: Added character training to Claude's fine-tuning starting with Claude 3, and published the persona selection model. Claude's Character (2024)
  • OpenAI: Found internal persona features that drive emergent misalignment and showed how to reverse it. Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment (2025)
  • AE Studio: Developed Self-Other Overlap fine-tuning to reduce deception. Towards Safe and Honest AI Agents with Neural Self-Other Overlap (2024)
  • Truthful AI: Led the emergent misalignment study showing narrow training shifts a model's broad persona. Emergent Misalignment: Narrow finetuning can produce broadly misaligned LLMs (2025)
  • Allen Institute for AI (Ai2): A researcher co-authored the first open implementation of character training. Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI (2025)
  • Hamburg University of Technology: Researchers there led the "model raising" position paper, with co-authors at the University of Toronto and EPFL. From Model Training to Model Raising (2025)

Status

Early. Character training, which shapes a model's persona, has been part of Anthropic's alignment finetuning since Claude 3 (2024) and has an open implementation (2025); Model Raising is a position paper with no implementation.

In their own words

We propose a paradigm shift from "model training" to "model raising," in which alignment is woven into a model's development from the start. We identify several key components for this paradigm, all centered around redesigning the training corpus: reframing training data from a first-person perspective, recontextualizing information as lived experience, simulating social interactions, and scaffolding the ordering of training data.

Roland Aydin, Christian Cyron, Steve Bachelor, Ashton Anderson, Robert West, From Model Training to Model Raising, 2025

Second, we think relying on a mix of good judgment and a minimal set of well-understood rules tends to generalize better than rules or decision procedures imposed as unexplained constraints. Our present understanding is that if we train Claude to exhibit even quite narrow behavior, this often has broad effects on the model's understanding of who Claude is.

Anthropic, Claude's Constitution, 2026
