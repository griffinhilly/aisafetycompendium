Building the field

Field-building

Research training and upskilling programmes

Run courses, bootcamps and mentored fellowships that train newcomers for AI safety research, engineering and policy work.

Open in the compendium

Research training programmes teach newcomers the skills for AI safety work and give them a first mentored project. They range from free online courses to coding bootcamps to paid, full-time research fellowships with an experienced mentor. They are one of the main ways into the field. Employers use them to find and test candidates, and many alumni join safety teams or found new organisations.

How it works

For most people the first step is a course, such as BlueDot Impact's free online courses of 25 to 40 hours, which start with AGI strategy and then specialise. BlueDot says more than 10,000 people have taken its courses since 2022. The Center for AI Safety (CAIS) publishes a textbook and online course, AI Safety, Ethics and Society, and gives researchers free access to its compute cluster. For engineering skills, ARENA runs 4 to 5 week in-person coding bootcamps in London two or three times a year. It covers participants' travel and housing and posts its curriculum online.

Fellowships pair a fellow with a mentor on a real research project. MATS runs 12-week fellowships in Berkeley and London with mentoring, research funding and housing, and a chance of a 6 to 12 month funded extension. LASR Labs puts teams of three or four with a supervisor for 13 weeks to take a project from proposal to paper, with a £15,000 stipend. Pivotal (15 weeks, London), ERA (10 weeks, Cambridge) and Principles of Intelligence (about three months, for researchers from complex-systems sciences) follow the same model. SPAR is part-time and remote: mentees give 5 to 40 hours a week for three months, ending in a Demo Day with a careers fair. The Anthropic Fellows Program gives four months of mentored research on Anthropic's priority questions, with about $15,000 a month of compute per fellow.

For policy careers, the Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI) runs three-month fellowships in the UK and Washington, DC, and Talos Network gives seven weeks of training for European AI governance careers, with an optional six-month paid placement. Coefficient Giving funds many of these programmes, including ARENA. In September 2026 Kairos, the organisation that runs SPAR, announced $50 million from Coefficient Giving over two years.

Where research stands

MATS says that since late 2021, 631 researchers have been through its programme, producing more than 240 papers. It says 75% of alumni now work in AI alignment, transparency or security, and 10% have co-founded safety organisations or teams, such as Apollo Research. Anthropic says more than 200 fellows have taken part in its programme since early 2025; over 65% published work from the fellowship and over 35% later joined Anthropic. Kairos reports that SPAR received 6,000 applications for its latest round (as of September 2026), up from 2,400 six months earlier.

Most outcome data comes from the programmes themselves. MATS's 2024 survey reached 46% of alumni from its first four cohorts; 78% of those 72 respondents worked on alignment or control or did alignment research independently. BlueDot says 25% of its graduates land impactful roles within six months. Jobs and mentors after training are now the limiting factor: in late 2025 interviews with 23 hiring managers, research leads and funders, MATS found that organisations lack senior staff to supervise juniors and so hire very selectively. Interviewees said three-month projects are often too short to tell independent researchers from those who need close supervision. They expect AI automation to cut demand for junior technical roles within two to five years. Programmes for non-researchers are a second gap: an August 2025 post counted over 20 full-time upskilling fellowships, only 3 of them for non-researchers.

Key results

  • MATS says that since late 2021, 631 researchers have taken part in its programme, producing more than 240 papers, and that 75% of alumni now work in AI alignment, transparency or security and 10% have co-founded safety organisations or teams. MATS, 2026
  • In a 2024 survey of 72 alumni from MATS's first four cohorts (46% of them), 78% were working on alignment or control or doing alignment research independently, and 78% said their publication possibly or probably would not have happened without MATS. MATS (utilistrutil and co-authors), 2024
  • Anthropic says more than 200 people have been Anthropic fellows since early 2025, that over 65% published work from the fellowship, and that over 35% later joined Anthropic full-time. Anthropic, 2026
  • 80,000 Hours' career review says, without giving a source, that BlueDot Impact's courses had reached more than 7,000 people, nearly 1,000 of whom now hold full-time AI safety roles, with a team of seven at the time. Aaron Gertler (80,000 Hours), 2026
  • Kairos reports that SPAR received 6,000 applications in its latest round, up from 2,400 six months earlier, and that SPAR has quintupled in size since Kairos took it over. Kairos, 2026
  • Interviews with 23 hiring managers, research leads and funders found that AI safety organisations are limited by too few senior mentors, which makes hiring hyper-selective and makes fellowships important hiring pipelines that let employers watch candidates over long periods. John Teichman (MATS Research), 2026

  • Building training routes for managers, communicators, operators and other non-researchers, not only researchers. source
  • Teaching research taste: choosing promising directions and scoping projects without close guidance, which employers call the most essential and elusive skill. source
  • Measuring counterfactual impact: most evidence is opt-in surveys and self-ratings collected by the programmes themselves. source
  • Training for a job market that AI automation is reshaping, where demand for junior technical execution may fall within two to five years. source

Criticisms

  • The pipeline is over-optimised for researchers: of over 20 full-time upskilling fellowships, only 3 are for non-researchers, and research fellowships' acceptance rates below 5% push applicants to prove themselves as researchers. Chris Clay (EA Forum, August 2025)
  • Short, pre-tested coding exercises do not teach the open-ended work of real research, so a bootcamp like ARENA mainly works as a signal for selective fellowships. TheManxLoiner, a MATS research manager writing personally (LessWrong, January 2026)

Who works on it

  • MATS (ML Alignment & Theory Scholars): 12-week mentored research fellowships in Berkeley and London; 631 researchers since late 2021. MATS Alumni Impact Analysis (2024)
  • BlueDot Impact: Free AI safety, governance and biosecurity courses taken by over 10,000 people; in-person programmes and grants. About (2026)
  • ARENA (Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator): 4 to 5 week in-person coding bootcamps at LISA in London, with a public curriculum. ARENA 7.0 Impact Report (2026)
  • SPAR: Part-time remote three-month research programme pairing mentees with professionals; run by Kairos. SPAR (home page) (2026)
  • LASR Labs (London AI Safety Research): 13-week supervised team research programme in London focused on loss-of-control risk; three NeurIPS 2025 main-conference papers. LASR Labs (home page) (2026)
  • Pivotal Research: 15-week mentored AI safety research fellowship in London. Pivotal Research (home page) (2026)
  • ERA (Cambridge ERA:AI Fellowship): 10-week full-time fellowship in Cambridge, UK, on technical safety and governance research. ERA (home page) (2026)
  • Principles of Intelligence: About three-month fellowship pairing roughly 20 researchers from complex-systems sciences with AI safety mentors. Principles of Intelligence (home page) (2026)
  • Anthropic: Anthropic Fellows Program: four months of mentored, funded safety research; over 200 fellows since early 2025. Anthropic Fellows Program for AI safety research: applications open for our next cohorts (2026)
  • Centre for the Governance of AI: Three-month Summer and Winter fellowships in the UK and a DC fellowship to move people into AI governance. Open Positions (2026)
  • Talos Network: Seven-week training for European AI governance careers, with optional six-month placements. Talos Network (home page) (2026)
  • Center for AI Safety: AI Safety, Ethics and Society textbook and course, free compute cluster, and field-building programmes. Field Building Projects (2026)
  • Coefficient Giving: Funds ARENA and many other programmes; in September 2026 Kairos (SPAR) announced $50 million from it over two years. Kairos has raised $50M to build talent infrastructure for AI safety (and we're hiring!) (2026)

Status

Large and growing: by their own counts, 631 MATS fellows since late 2021 and over 10,000 BlueDot course-takers since 2022; $50M from Coefficient Giving to Kairos (announced Sept 2026); jobs and mentors after training, not trainees, are now the bottleneck.

Fellows conduct research for 12 weeks at our offices in Berkeley, CA and London, UK, with the opportunity to apply for an additional 6–12 month funded extension.
