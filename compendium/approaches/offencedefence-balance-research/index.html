Offence–defence balance research

Model how AI shifts the relative cost of attack versus defence in a domain.

Open in the compendium

Offence–defence balance research asks whether a new AI capability helps attackers or defenders more, and how that changes as AI improves. The answer shapes much of AI safety strategy. If AI favours defence, spreading it widely is safer; if it favours offence, restricting it, or racing to deploy it for defenders first, matters more.

How it works

The "offence–defence balance" is an idea from international relations: roughly, how costly it is to attack compared with how costly it is to defend. Researchers model this for one domain at a time, such as cyber security or disease outbreaks. In 2019 Ben Garfinkel and Allan Dafoe built a simple model of what happens when both sides scale up the same capability. In cyber security, if both sides can find more software flaws, attackers benefit first, because they find flaws defenders missed. Later, defenders find and fix nearly all of them, and the advantage flips. They call this "offensive-then-defensive scaling".

Other work weighs specific factors. Andrew Lohn of the Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) collected the published arguments for offensive and defensive advantage in cyber conflict and asked how each might change with AI. The Center for a New American Security (CNAS) argued in September 2025 that rising inference costs could make it too expensive for defenders to apply the best AI everywhere, while attackers only need to target a part. A practical example of acting on this research is Anthropic's Project Glasswing (April 2026), which gave defenders early access to a model with strong flaw-finding abilities before such abilities spread.

Where research stands

The field is active but has few firm conclusions. The main finding so far is that there is no single answer: CSET's 2025 review concluded the cyber domain is "too multifaceted" for a general verdict, and listed forty-four distinct effects. Models like Garfinkel and Dafoe's give useful intuitions but rest on simplified assumptions. Historical forecasting is poor: the same authors note that before the First World War most analysts missed how far machine guns and barbed wire favoured defence.

The question is now urgent in practice. In April 2026 Anthropic reported that an unreleased model had found thousands of serious, previously unknown software flaws, and said such abilities would soon spread. Its Economics and Policy fellowship lists "analysing the offense–defense balance for AI-enabled cyber and bio capabilities as models scale" as an open research question for its January 2027 cohort.

Key results

  • When both sides scale up a capability, offence tends to gain at low investment levels and defence at high levels ("offensive-then-defensive scaling"), shown for ground invasions and software-flaw discovery. Ben Garfinkel, Allan Dafoe, 2019
  • Whether publishing AI research helps attackers or defenders depends on the field; lessons from software-vulnerability disclosure, which often favours defence, cannot be assumed to carry over to AI. Toby Shevlane, Allan Dafoe, 2020
  • Simple mathematical models suggest faster patch adoption helps defenders far more than faster patch development, since about 80 percent of disclosed flaws already have a patch on day one. Andrew J. Lohn, Krystal A. Jackson (CSET), 2022
  • A review of the literature found no single answer on whether AI favours cyber offence or defence and listed forty-four distinct ways AI could shift the balance. Andrew J. Lohn, 2025
  • AI has historically helped cyber defenders, but rising inference costs, full automation of attacks and reliability problems could tilt frontier AI toward attackers. Caleb Withers (CNAS), 2025
  • Current research is not enough to establish the marginal risk (extra risk over existing tools) of open model weights for misuse such as cyberattacks. Sayash Kapoor, Rishi Bommasani, et al., Arvind Narayanan, 2024

Criticisms

  • The balance cannot be summed up in one verdict per domain; AI will help some parts of offence and defence, hurt others and leave some unchanged. Andrew J. Lohn (CSET)
  • To defend indefinitely against misaligned AI, aligned defensive AI would need "broadly speaking all the capabilities" of the misaligned systems, so safety cannot come from simply never building dangerous capabilities. Chris van Merwijk, in the LessWrong post 'An AI defense-offense symmetry thesis'
  • Attempts to forecast shifts in the balance are often unsuccessful, and even shifts that new technologies have already caused can be hard to detect. Ben Garfinkel and Allan Dafoe

Who works on it

  • Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI): Published the offence–defence scaling model and related work on AI misuse. How Does the Offense-Defense Balance Scale? (2019)
  • Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET), Georgetown University: Models and reviews how AI shifts the cyber offence–defence balance. The Impact of AI on the Cyber Offense-Defense Balance and the Character of Cyber Conflict (2025)
  • Center for a New American Security (CNAS): Analyses how frontier AI could tilt cyber operations toward attackers and what policy can do. Tipping the Scales (2025)
  • The Anthropic Institute (Anthropic Fellows Program, Economics & Policy): Lists offence–defence analysis for AI-enabled cyber and bio capabilities as a fellowship research question. Anthropic Fellows Program, The Anthropic Institute (Economics & Policy) (2026)
  • Anthropic (Project Glasswing): Gave critical-software defenders early access to a frontier model to find and fix flaws before such capabilities spread. Project Glasswing (2026)

Start reading

  • Artificial Intelligence, Foresight, and the Offense-Defense Balance, Ben Garfinkel, Allan Dafoe (War on the Rocks) (2019). Short, readable introduction to the concept and its limits.
  • Considerations Influencing Offense-Defense Dynamics From Artificial Intelligence, Giulio Corsi, Kyle Kilian, Richard Mallah (2024). A taxonomy of the factors that decide whether an AI system mainly threatens or protects.
  • The Impact of AI on the Cyber Offense-Defense Balance and the Character of Cyber Conflict, Andrew J. Lohn (2025). The most complete review for the cyber domain.
  • Tipping the Scales, Caleb Withers (CNAS) (2025). Policy-oriented case that frontier AI may favour attackers, with recommendations.
  • How Does the Offense-Defense Balance Scale?, Ben Garfinkel, Allan Dafoe (2019). The formal model behind offensive-then-defensive scaling.

In their own words

One of the most important open questions is whether applications of AI ... will tend to be more useful for conducting offensive or defensive military operations. If AI favors the offense, then a significant body of international relations theory suggests that this could have destabilizing effects.

Ben Garfinkel and Allan Dafoe (War on the Rocks), Artificial Intelligence, Foresight, and the Offense-Defense Balance, 2019

We ask how the offense-defense balance scales, meaning how it changes as investments into a conflict increase. ... Simple models of ground invasions and cyberattacks that exploit software vulnerabilities suggest that, in both cases, growth in investments will favor offense when investment levels are sufficiently low and favor defense when they are sufficiently high.

Ben Garfinkel and Allan Dafoe (GovAI), How Does the Offense-Defense Balance Scale? (Journal of Strategic Studies), 2019
