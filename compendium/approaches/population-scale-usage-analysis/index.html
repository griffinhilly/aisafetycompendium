Deployment-usage analysis at population scale

Read anonymised real conversations in aggregate to see what the system is actually doing to people.

Open in the compendium

Population-scale usage analysis studies millions of real conversations with an AI assistant, in aggregate and with privacy protections, to learn what the system is actually doing to people. Pre-release tests can only check for harms someone thought of in advance; usage analysis can reveal patterns nobody predicted, such as coordinated misuse or a slow rise in unhealthy reliance. It matters for safety because it is one of the few ways to measure real-world effects rather than lab behaviour.

How it works

Researchers do not read individual chats. Instead, an AI model reads each conversation and writes a short, anonymised summary of it, for example 'user asks for help drafting a message to a family member'. Software then groups similar summaries into clusters and reports only clusters large enough that no individual can be singled out. Anthropic's Clio system, described in December 2024, works this way, and analysts explore the resulting map of topics rather than raw text.

The same pipeline can answer targeted safety questions. A team can define a harm, such as an assistant validating a user's distorted belief, train a classifier to spot it, run it over a large sample, and report how often it happens and whether the rate is changing. It can also find misuse that looks harmless one conversation at a time but shows up in aggregate, such as networks of accounts running the same scheme.

Where research stands

This approach is active but concentrated in a few labs, because only the companies that run the products hold the data. Anthropic has published several studies, including Clio, a February 2025 study mapping over four million conversations to economic tasks, a June 2025 study of emotional-support conversations, and a January 2026 study of 'disempowerment' patterns across 1.5 million conversations. OpenAI has published a September 2025 working paper on how people use ChatGPT and, with MIT Media Lab, a March 2025 study of emotional use and well-being. In October 2025 OpenAI released estimates of how many weekly users showed signs of mental-health emergencies, which news reports described as an initial analysis. Outside researchers can use opt-in public datasets such as WildChat. In August 2026 Anthropic reported that research groups at Stanford, Oxford and METR had each studied about 250,000 Claude conversations through its privacy-preserving analysis tool, seeing only aggregated outputs, never raw chats.

The methods work well for describing broad patterns. Two problems remain. First, the privacy protections rest on AI models filtering personal data, and a 2026 attack paper showed they can be defeated. Second, results come from one company's platform and are hard for outsiders to check; Stanford's 2025 transparency index found post-deployment usage and impact among the areas where developers are least transparent.

Key results

  • Clio surfaced usage patterns across one million Claude.ai conversations without human reviewers reading raw chats, and was used to find coordinated abuse and watch for unknown risks around launches and major events. Alex Tamkin, Miles McCain, Kunal Handa et al. (Anthropic), 2024
  • Across 1.5 million Claude.ai conversations, severe 'situational disempowerment potential' appeared in fewer than 1 in 1,000 conversations, was more common in personal topics such as relationships, rose over time, and drew higher user approval ratings. Mrinank Sharma, Miles McCain, Raymond Douglas, David Duvenaud, 2026
  • Of about 4.5 million screened Claude.ai conversations, 2.9% were affective (emotional or personal support), companionship and roleplay together were under 0.5%, and Claude pushed back in fewer than 10% of supportive conversations. Anthropic, 2025
  • An automated analysis of nearly 40 million ChatGPT interactions found emotionally engaged use to be rare and concentrated in a small group of heavy users; a linked trial of nearly 1,000 people tied prolonged daily use to worse well-being. OpenAI and MIT Media Lab, 2025
  • OpenAI estimated in October 2025 that about 0.07% of weekly ChatGPT users showed possible signs of psychosis- or mania-related emergencies and that more than a million users a week sent messages with explicit indicators of potential suicidal planning or intent. Dawn, 2025
  • Hierarchical summarisation of computer-use sessions let a monitor catch harms that only appear in aggregate, and Anthropic's safeguards staff judged 96% of interaction summaries sufficiently accurate for harm determination. Theodore Sumers et al. (Anthropic), 2025

Open problems

  • Making the privacy protections robust, since an attacker who plants crafted chats can make an insight system leak a target user's sensitive data. source
  • Letting outside researchers verify lab findings without exposing users' conversations. source
  • Generalising beyond a single platform, since each study sees only one company's users. source
  • Separating short-term user approval from long-term harm, since conversations with more disempowerment potential got higher ratings. source
  • Moving from correlation to cause when linking usage patterns to well-being outcomes. source

Criticisms

  • Privacy protections in these systems are heuristic and rely on language models to filter personal data; the CLIOPATRA attack recovered synthetic medical histories from Clio in up to 65% of cases. Meenatchi Sundaram Muthu Selva Annamalai, Emiliano De Cristofaro, Peter Kairouz
  • Post-deployment usage and impact of flagship models is among the areas where developers are least transparent, so outsiders cannot easily check usage-based safety claims. Stanford Center for Research on Foundation Models (Foundation Model Transparency Index)

Who works on it

  • Anthropic: Built Clio and publishes usage studies on economic tasks, emotional support and disempowerment. Clio: Privacy-Preserving Insights into Real-World AI Use (2024)
  • OpenAI: Publishes analyses of ChatGPT usage, including a working paper by OpenAI economists that classifies a representative sample of conversations. How People Use ChatGPT (NBER Working Paper 34255) (2025)
  • MIT Media Lab: Ran a randomised trial on ChatGPT use and well-being alongside OpenAI's usage analysis. Early methods for studying affective use and emotional wellbeing in ChatGPT: An OpenAI and MIT Media Lab Research collaboration (2025)
  • Allen Institute for AI: Released WildChat, a public opt-in dataset of about one million real ChatGPT conversations. WildChat: 1M ChatGPT Interaction Logs in the Wild (2024)
  • METR: One of three outside groups (with Stanford and Oxford researchers) given privacy-filtered access to Claude conversation samples. Enabling independent research on how people use Claude (Anthropic) (2026)
  • Stanford Center for Research on Foundation Models: Scores developers on disclosure of usage data and downstream impact. The 2025 Foundation Model Transparency Index (2025)

Status

Active, and mostly gated behind lab-internal data access; in August 2026 Anthropic reported a pilot in which research groups at Stanford, Oxford and METR ran their own studies through Anthropic Insights, seeing only aggregated outputs.

  • Who's in Charge? Disempowerment Patterns in Real-World LLM Usage, Mrinank Sharma, Miles McCain, Raymond Douglas, David Duvenaud (2026). Usage analysis aimed at a subtle, non-catastrophic harm.
  • How People Use ChatGPT, Aaron Chatterji et al. (2025). OpenAI's own study of a representative sample of ChatGPT conversations.
  • CLIOPATRA: Extracting Private Information from LLM Insights, Meenatchi Sundaram Muthu Selva Annamalai, Emiliano De Cristofaro, Peter Kairouz (2026). The main evidence that the privacy layer can fail.

In their own words

How are AI assistants being used in the real world? ... we present Clio (Claude insights and observations), a privacy-preserving platform that uses AI assistants themselves to analyze and surface aggregated usage patterns across millions of conversations, without the need for human reviewers to read raw conversations.

Alex Tamkin et al. (Anthropic), Clio: Privacy-Preserving Insights into Real-World AI Use, 2024

We focus on situational disempowerment potential, which occurs when AI assistant interactions risk leading users to form distorted perceptions of reality, make inauthentic value judgments, or act in ways misaligned with their values.

Mrinank Sharma, Miles McCain, Raymond Douglas, David Duvenaud, Who's in Charge? Disempowerment Patterns in Real-World LLM Usage, 2026
