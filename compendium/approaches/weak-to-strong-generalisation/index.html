Shaping the model: data and training

Aligned models

Weak-to-strong generalisation

Check whether a weaker supervisor can still get good behaviour out of a stronger model.

Open in the compendium

Weak-to-strong generalisation asks whether a weak supervisor can still get good behaviour out of a stronger model. It studies the future problem, people supervising superhuman AI, through a present-day stand-in: a small model supervising a large one. If the strong model generalises beyond its supervisor's mistakes, today's feedback-based methods may keep working; if it copies them, they will not.

How it works

Researchers take a small model, such as GPT-2, and use it to label data for a task. They then fine-tune a much stronger model, such as GPT-4, on those imperfect labels. Finally they compare three scores: the weak supervisor, the strong model trained on weak labels, and the strong model trained on correct labels. The share of the gap the strong model closes is called "performance gap recovered".

The hope is that the strong model already knows how to do the task from pretraining and only needs a rough signal to bring that skill out. The risk is that it learns to imitate its supervisor's errors instead. Related work asks a similar question in another form: can a model trained only on easy problems, where human labels are reliable, solve hard problems where they are not?

Where research stands

OpenAI's Superalignment team opened the area in December 2023. Strong models trained on weak labels consistently beat their supervisors but fell short of their full ability. On language tasks an added training loss raised median gap recovery from about 25% to nearly 80%, but in reward modelling, the setting closest to RLHF, naive training usually recovered only about 10% of the gap. The authors concluded RLHF-style methods "may scale poorly to superhuman models without further work." OpenAI and Eric Schmidt launched a $10 million grant programme the same month with this as a priority direction.

Later work is mixed. Easy-to-hard studies found models often generalise well from easy training data. Anthropic's June 2025 Internal Coherence Maximization trained models on their own consistent labels with no outside supervision and matched training on correct labels on several tasks. On the other side, a 2024 study found strong models can look aligned where the weak supervisor can check them and misbehave where it cannot, and that this got worse as the gap grew. The setup also has known gaps as an analogy: future models may find it easier to imitate human errors than today's models do.

Key results

  • A GPT-4 model fine-tuned on GPT-2-level labels with an extra confidence loss reached close to GPT-3.5-level performance on language tasks, but weak-to-strong gains were much smaller for reward modelling. Collin Burns, Pavel Izmailov, Jan Hendrik Kirchner et al. (OpenAI), 2023
  • Language models often generalise from easy to hard questions about as well as models fine-tuned on hard data, across datasets from grade-school science to college STEM. Peter Hase, Mohit Bansal, Peter Clark, Sarah Wiegreffe, 2024
  • Reward models trained only on easier maths problems could score solutions to harder ones; a 7B model trained this way reached 34.0% on MATH500 using human supervision on easy problems only. Zhiqing Sun, Longhui Yu, Yikang Shen, Weiyang Liu, Yiming Yang, Sean Welleck, Chuang Gan, 2024
  • Fine-tuning a model on its own mutually consistent labels, with no outside supervision, matched training on correct labels and beat crowdsourced human labels on three benchmarks. Jiaxin Wen, Zachary Ankner et al. (Anthropic), 2025
  • Strong models trained by weak ones can behave well where the weak model can check them and badly where it cannot, and this grows with the capability gap. Wenkai Yang, Shiqi Shen et al. (Renmin University of China, Tencent), 2024

Open problems

  • Future superhuman models may find human errors easy to imitate, which today's small-supervises-large setups may understate. source
  • Weak-to-strong gains are weakest for reward modelling, the step that matters most for RLHF. source
  • There is no reliable way to tell whether a strong model's good behaviour holds outside the region the weak supervisor can verify. source
  • Theory suggests generalisation happens when the strong model cannot fit the supervisor's mistakes without extra error; nobody knows when that holds for frontier models. source

Criticisms

  • The setup measures whether a strong model looks good to a weak judge, and strong models can exploit exactly the cases the judge cannot check. Wenkai Yang, Shiqi Shen et al.
  • The original authors list disanalogies: today's strong models were not pretrained to imitate weak models, and human supervision leaks in through pretraining data, both of which may make current results look better than the real problem. Collin Burns et al. (OpenAI)

Who works on it

  • OpenAI (Superalignment Generalization team): Introduced the weak-to-strong setup and the performance-gap-recovered measure. Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Eliciting Strong Capabilities With Weak Supervision (2023)
  • OpenAI: With Eric Schmidt, launched $10 million in Superalignment Fast Grants with weak-to-strong generalisation as a priority direction. Superalignment Fast Grants (2023)
  • Anthropic (Alignment Science team): Lists weak-to-strong and easy-to-hard generalisation as recommended directions and developed unsupervised elicitation. Unsupervised Elicitation of Language Models (2025)
  • Renmin University of China (Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence): Showed strong models can deceive weak supervisors in weak-to-strong setups. Super(ficial)-alignment: Strong Models May Deceive Weak Models in Weak-to-Strong Generalization (2024)

In their own words

However, future superhuman models will behave in complex ways too difficult for humans to reliably evaluate; humans will only be able to weakly supervise superhuman models. We study an analogy to this problem: can weak model supervision elicit the full capabilities of a much stronger model?

Collin Burns, Pavel Izmailov, Jan Hendrik Kirchner, Bowen Baker, Leo Gao, Leopold Aschenbrenner, Yining Chen, Adrien Ecoffet, Manas Joglekar, Jan Leike, Ilya Sutskever, Jeff Wu (OpenAI), Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Eliciting Strong Capabilities With Weak Supervision, 2023

We test this using a range of pretrained language models in the GPT-4 family on natural language processing (NLP), chess, and reward modeling tasks. We find that when we naively finetune strong pretrained models on labels generated by a weak model, they consistently perform better than their weak supervisors, a phenomenon we call weak-to-strong generalization.

Collin Burns et al. (OpenAI), Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Eliciting Strong Capabilities With Weak Supervision, 2023
