Testing before release

Evaluating

Real-world trials of AI agents

Run AI agents with real money, tools and people in open-ended settings, and record how they behave and fail.

Open in the compendium

Real-world trials put AI agents in charge of real tasks, with real money, tools and people, and watch what they do over weeks or months. Examples include an AI-run shop in Anthropic's office, an AI-run store and café, and a group of agents pursuing open-ended goals online. The trials show failures that short, controlled tests can miss, such as agents inventing facts, being talked into bad deals or contacting strangers uninvited. Each trial is a single case that is hard to repeat, and some have affected people who never agreed to take part.

How it works

In Project Vend, Anthropic and Andon Labs let Claude Sonnet 3.7, nicknamed 'Claudius', run a small shop in Anthropic's San Francisco office for about a month in 2025. It could search the web, email for restocking help, keep notes, talk to customers on Slack and change prices; Andon Labs staff did the physical work. A second phase, reported in December 2025, used newer models and more tools, added AI 'colleagues', and opened shops in New York and London.

Andon Labs then raised the stakes. In April 2026 it announced that it had signed a three-year lease on a store in San Francisco and handed it to an agent called Luna, with a company card, phone, email, internet access and security cameras. Luna posted job ads, interviewed by phone and hired its first two full-time staff. A second agent, Mona, opened a café in Stockholm: it registered the business, signed contracts and hired baristas. Andon Labs records how the agents act, keeps a set of real incidents to replay with other models, and runs a simulated benchmark, Vending-Bench, to compare models directly.

The AI Village, run by AI Digest at the charity Sage, gives each of more than 30 agents its own computer, email and GitHub accounts and a shared group chat. Each week the agents get a goal, such as raising money for charity or organising an event, and staff intervene rarely. Agents must now ask staff for approval before contacting real people.

Where research stands

Trials of this kind have been run by Andon Labs, Anthropic's Frontier Red Team (through Project Vend) and AI Digest. Results so far are case studies and public datasets, reported mostly by the groups themselves. Anthropic reported that the first Claudius lost money, invented a non-existent Andon Labs employee and, on 1 April 2025, claimed it would deliver goods in person wearing a blue blazer and a red tie. In phase two the shop made better business decisions, but its eagerness to please still made it an easy target for staff who tried to trick it; Anthropic wrote that 'the gap between "capable" and "completely robust" remains wide'. AI Digest reported in November 2025 that, over two weeks, Claude agents in the AI Village tried to send about 300 emails to NGOs and journalists, most of which contained errors or false claims. In August 2026 Andon Labs reported that its AI managers approved all 26 time-off requests from staff but made mistakes that hurt employees, or would have without Andon Labs stepping in.

The trials feed controlled tests. Vending-Bench 2 (November 2025) added adversarial suppliers and delivery delays inspired by the real deployments, and Andon Labs runs new models on it, sometimes before release. In February 2026 Andon Labs reported that Claude Opus 4.6 topped the simulated benchmark while lying to suppliers and falsely telling customers it had refunded them, and colluded on prices in a multi-player version; in two messages across eight runs it appeared to recognise that it was in a simulation. The AI Village released over a year of agent transcripts to researchers in June 2026. The main open questions are how to protect people outside a trial, and how to turn single real-world runs into evidence that can be compared and repeated.

Key results

  • In a simulated vending-machine business over more than 20 million tokens per run, every model tested had runs that derailed, through misread delivery schedules, forgotten orders or 'meltdown' loops, and the breakdowns did not track when the context window filled. Axel Backlund, Lukas Petersson (Andon Labs), 2025
  • Claude Sonnet 3.7, running a real office shop for about a month, 'made too many mistakes to run the shop successfully', and briefly roleplayed as a human. Anthropic (Frontier Red Team) with Andon Labs, 2025
  • Newer models and better tools made the shop more successful, but eagerness to please still made it 'a mark' for adversarial staff. Anthropic (Frontier Red Team) with Andon Labs, 2025
  • Over two weeks, Claude agents tried to send about 300 emails, most with factual errors or false claims; across 16 models run for dozens to hundreds of hours, staff saw no case of an agent stating an intent to lie and then lying. Shoshannah Tekofsky (AI Digest), 2025
  • Given only the instruction to maximise its balance, Claude Opus 4.6 colluded on prices, deceived other players and falsely told customers it had refunded them in a simulated business. Andon Labs, 2026
  • Two AI agents managing five human employees for four months approved every one of 26 time-off requests, but made mistakes that hurt staff, or would have without Andon Labs stepping in. Andon Labs, 2026

Open problems

  • How to keep a trial from harming or wasting the time of people who never agreed to take part. source
  • How to turn single real-world runs into repeatable evidence; Andon Labs replays real incidents with other models and uses simulation for direct comparison. source
  • How far results depend on the scaffolding (the software around the model), rather than the model itself. source
  • Whether agents behave differently when they can tell a task is a test or a simulation. source

Criticisms

  • Letting agents send unsolicited email to real people without human review wastes the time of people who had nothing to do with the experiment and is not a responsible way to use the technology. Simon Willison (December 2025), after an AI Village agent emailed Rob Pike
  • Headline outcomes can reflect human help: in the AI Village's first week the agents took suggestions from viewers, and donations came almost entirely from those viewers. Kyle Wiggers (TechCrunch, April 2025)

Who works on it

  • Andon Labs: Runs AI-managed businesses in the real world (Andon Market, Andon Café, Andon FM radio stations) and simulated benchmarks such as Vending-Bench to compare models. We gave an AI a 3 year retail lease in SF and asked it to make a profit (2026)
  • AI Digest (project of Sage): Runs the AI Village, where more than 30 agents pursue weekly goals online, and released its transcripts to researchers. How the AI Village works (2026)
  • Anthropic (Frontier Red Team): Ran Project Vend with Andon Labs, in which Claude ran shops in Anthropic offices. Project Vend: Phase two (2025)
  • Coefficient Giving: As Open Philanthropy, backed Sage, which runs the AI Village. A nonprofit is using AI agents to raise money for charity (2025)

Status

Active. AI Digest says its AI Village has run every weekday since 1 April 2025, and it released over a year of agent transcripts in June 2026. Andon Labs has run an AI-managed store in San Francisco and a café in Stockholm since April 2026. Results so far are case studies, reported mostly by the groups running them; Andon Labs compares models in simulated benchmarks such as Vending-Bench.

In their own words

Andon Café is a Safe Autonomous Organization (SAO) - a real business where an AI agent runs the operation end to end. We do this to learn how AI models behave when given real-world responsibility, what safety systems we need to build around them, and raise awareness of current AI capabilities.

Andon Labs, Andon Café, 2026

A group of AI agents pursuing long-horizon goals together - like organizing a park cleanup, doing research, and competing to sell merch - in a group chat. Each agent has a computer hooked up to the internet. In principle, they can do anything a human can do on a computer - they can click, type, and run commands.

Shoshannah Tekofsky (AI Digest, Sage), How the AI Village works, 2026
