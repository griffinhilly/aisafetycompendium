Keeping a democratic lead in AI

Give advanced AI to the intelligence and defence communities of the US and its democratic allies; restrict chips.

Open in the compendium

Supporters of this strategy hold that the US and its democratic allies should lead in advanced AI, give it to their defence and intelligence agencies, and deny rival autocracies the chips needed to catch up. They argue that a democratic lead is the best protection against authoritarian use of AI and buys time to make AI safe. Critics argue it fuels a dangerous race, and that concentrating military AI creates its own risk of abuse inside democracies.

How it works

In October 2024 Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, described an 'entente strategy', in which a coalition of democracies secures its AI supply chain, scales quickly, blocks rivals' access to chips and chip-making equipment, and offers to share AI's benefits with countries that support it. In January 2026 he set out three steps. First, do not sell chips, chip-making tools or data centres to China. Second, provide AI to the intelligence and defence communities of the US and its democratic allies. Third, draw a hard line against AI abuses within democracies, treating domestic mass surveillance and mass propaganda as red lines and urging caution on fully autonomous weapons.

The aim is for democracies to gain a defensive edge while keeping safeguards, but who sets those safeguards remains contested. The Pentagon awarded Anthropic a $200 million contract in July 2025. Anthropic then insisted that its Claude models not be used for mass surveillance of Americans or to power fully autonomous weapons. In February 2026 the Defense Secretary declared Anthropic a 'supply chain risk to national security', and OpenAI announced an agreement to deploy its models on the Pentagon's classified network.

Where research stands

The strategy is a policy position rather than a research field, and US governments and some AI companies have adopted parts of it. The US still limits China's access to advanced chips, though it eased the rules for H200-class chips in January 2026. A broader tiered rule for chip access worldwide was rescinded in May 2025. Frontier labs now sell to defence and intelligence customers. The argument draws on Leopold Aschenbrenner's 2024 essay series, which says a lead of about two years would give room to slow down for safety, while a lead of months would force a reckless race.

The 2026 dispute between Anthropic and the Pentagon tested the 'hard line against AI abuses'. In September 2026 the US Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit upheld, by 2 to 1, the Pentagon's supply-chain-risk designation of Anthropic. A federal judge in California had earlier ruled that the Pentagon acted illegally by punishing the company for its criticism. Researchers working on power concentration warn that fully autonomous military AI could let a very small group control significant force without others' cooperation. Supporters and critics both value the safeguards, but disagree about whether racing for a lead makes them easier or harder to keep.

Criticisms

  • Racing for AGI is a 'suicide race', not an arms race: each side cuts corners on safety, and both lose if anyone builds AI that cannot be controlled; controllable 'tool AI' and a US-China détente are better. Max Tegmark (October 2024)
  • A single leading project and fully autonomous military AI would greatly raise the risk that a small group uses AI to seize power, even in established democracies. Tom Davidson, Lukas Finnveden, Rose Hadshar (Forethought, April 2025)
  • From the government side, a company's usage limits amount to an attempt to 'seize veto power over the operational decisions of the United States military'. Pete Hegseth, US Defense Secretary (February 2026)

In their own words

Second, it makes sense to use AI to empower democracies to resist autocracies. This is the reason Anthropic considers it important to provide AI to the intelligence and defense communities in the US and its democratic allies.

Dario Amodei (Anthropic), The Adolescence of Technology: Confronting and Overcoming the Risks of Powerful AI, 2026

First, we should absolutely not be selling chips, chip-making tools, or datacenters to the CCP. Chips and chip-making tools are the single greatest bottleneck to powerful AI, and blocking them is a simple but extremely effective measure, perhaps the most important single action we can take.

Dario Amodei (Anthropic), The Adolescence of Technology: Confronting and Overcoming the Risks of Powerful AI, 2026
