AI and people's own judgement

Societal resilience

Indicators of human versus AI influence

Build metrics that track how much of economic, cultural and political life humans still steer.

Open in the compendium

This approach builds measurements of how much economic, cultural and political life is still steered by people rather than AI systems. If humanity loses control gradually, no single event will raise the alarm, so the only warning may come from trends in numbers. Good indicators would let researchers and governments see the shift early and decide whether to act.

How it works

The Gradual Disempowerment paper (January 2025) proposes indicators in three areas. Economic: the "AI share of GDP, as a distinct category from either labor or capital", "the fraction of major corporate decisions made primarily by AI systems" and "the scale of unsupervised AI spending". Cultural: "the proportion of widely-consumed content created primarily by humans versus AI" and how common human-AI relationships are. Political: "the complexity of legislation (as a proxy for human comprehensibility)" and the role of AI in legal processes, policy and security.

Some partial measures already exist. Anthropic's Economic Index sorts Claude conversations into "automation" (Claude completes a task with little input) and "augmentation" (the person works with Claude, learns or iterates), and reports the shares over time. The company Graphite estimates the share of new web articles that are mainly AI-written by sampling Common Crawl, a public archive of web pages, and running AI-text detectors. RAND's April 2026 model proposes coalition-based metrics for group decisions: how many humans are needed to decide an outcome, and whether AI systems sit in the deciding group.

Where research stands

Indicators are wanted and named but not built. 80,000 Hours relays the call to "Develop metrics to track human and AI influence in economic, cultural, and political systems", and Raymond Douglas called indicator work the gradual-disempowerment project he most wished he could do himself. No widely accepted metrics for tracking this erosion exist. RAND recommends that bodies such as the US National Institute of Standards and Technology treat the human make-up of decisive groups as a measurable dimension of AI risk.

The partial measures show both promise and limits. Anthropic's data show automation briefly overtook augmentation on Claude.ai in its September 2025 report, then fell back by November 2025, which shows such indicators can move fast and need regular updates. Graphite's estimate of AI-written articles depends on AI-text detectors, and it does not measure AI drafts that humans then edit. A related proposal, the AI Power Disparity Index (September 2025), would measure power among groups of actors in the AI ecosystem, not between humans and AI.

Key results

  • Directive conversations, where users hand Claude a whole task, rose from 27% (late 2024) to 39% of Claude.ai usage, and automation exceeded augmentation for the first time; API traffic was 77% automation. Anthropic, 2025
  • By November 2025 the directive share had fallen 7 percentage points to 32%, and augmented conversations rose to 52% against 45% automated. Anthropic, 2026
  • Sampling 55,400 English articles from Common Crawl and averaging three AI-text detectors, about half of new web articles have been mainly AI-generated since early 2025 (49.9% in the first quarter of 2026). Jose Luis Paredes, Gregory Druck, Bevin Benson, Ethan Smith (Graphite), 2026
  • Three coalition-based metrics (distribution of decisive coalitions, minimal coalition size and minimal coalition make-up) can track human agency across many kinds of group decision and detect nonlinear acceleration. Alvin Moon, Benjamin Boudreaux (RAND), 2026
  • The Gradual Disempowerment paper sets out a list of economic, cultural and political indicators, and notes that many systems lack external reference points against which to measure alignment. Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, 2025

Open problems

  • For many of the systems to be measured there are no external reference points against which to judge their alignment, which makes indicators hard to build. source
  • Content-share estimates do not measure AI drafts that humans edit, and detectors may struggle as models improve, so these estimates are uncertain. source
  • Researchers still need to define thresholds: at what level of AI share does a trend become dangerous or hard to reverse. source

Criticisms

  • A rising AI share of output need not mean all humans lose power, because people who own capital would still receive the income from AI production; the economic trend points to concentration among humans. Tom Davidson

Who works on it

  • Anthropic: Publishes the Economic Index, tracking automation versus augmentation in Claude usage over time. Anthropic Economic Index report: Uneven geographic and enterprise AI adoption (2025)
  • RAND (Center for the Geopolitics of Artificial General Intelligence): Proposed coalition-based metrics of collective human agency and longitudinal monitoring. A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency (2026)
  • Graphite: Estimates the share of new web articles that are mainly AI-generated. AI Now Writes as Many Online Articles as Humans (2026)
  • Alignment of Complex Systems Research Group (Charles University): Its researchers co-wrote the indicator list in the Gradual Disempowerment paper. Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development (2025)

Status

Wanted and named; metrics proposed, none yet widely accepted.

In their own words

To effectively address gradual disempowerment, we need to be able to detect and quantify it. This is challenging partly because, for many of the systems we would hope to measure, we lack external reference points to measure their degree of alignment.

Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development, 2025

Beyond traditional measures like labor share of GDP, we should also measure AI share of GDP, as a distinct category from either labor or capital. ... Key indicators might include the complexity of legislation (as a proxy for human comprehensibility); the role of AI systems in legal processes, policy formation, and security apparatuses ...

Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud, Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development, 2025
