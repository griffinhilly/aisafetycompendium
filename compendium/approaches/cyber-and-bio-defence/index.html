Society: defence and who holds power

Societal resilience

Cyber and bio defensive capacity building

Build defence in the specific domains AI most amplifies offence.

Open in the compendium

Cyber and bio defensive capacity building means funding the people, tools and infrastructure that protect against AI-enabled attacks in the two domains where AI is expected to boost attackers most: computer security and biological threats. It complements efforts to stop models from helping attackers, on the view that such prevention will sometimes fail. The work is concrete and funded, through government prize challenges, lab programmes, philanthropic grants and fellowships.

How it works

In cyber security, the main lever is giving defenders AI tools that find and fix software flaws, before attackers get similar tools. The US Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) ran the AI Cyber Challenge (AIxCC) from 2023 to 2025 with the health research agency ARPA-H. Teams built automated systems to secure open-source code that critical infrastructure depends on; DARPA then released several of the systems as open source. Frontier labs now run their own programmes: OpenAI's Cybersecurity Grant Program and its Trusted Access for Cyber scheme, and Anthropic's Project Glasswing, which gives critical-software maintainers early access to a strong flaw-finding model.

In biology, the emphasis is "defence in depth": layers of protection that work whatever the pathogen is. One fellowship programme describes four pillars: protective equipment, buildings hardened against airborne disease, detection that does not depend on knowing the pathogen in advance, and fast medical countermeasures. Groups such as Blueprint Biosecurity work on parts of this, for example respirator stockpiles and ultraviolet air disinfection. The International Biosecurity and Biosafety Initiative for Science (IBBIS) builds free tools to screen DNA synthesis orders.

Talent programmes feed both domains. MATS, an AI safety research fellowship, runs a Biosecurity track and a Systems Security track (security for the infrastructure that advanced AI runs on). Pivotal Research launched a 15-week Frontier Biodefense Fellowship in London in 2026.

Where research stands

Cyber defence has moved fastest. AIxCC finished at DEF CON in August 2025: finalist systems found 54 of the planted vulnerabilities and patched 43, and also found 18 real ones that were reported to maintainers. In April 2026 Anthropic reported a model that had found thousands of serious, previously unknown flaws, and committed up to $100 million in credits for defenders. In February 2026 OpenAI committed $10 million in API credits for cyber defence. The bottleneck has shifted from finding flaws to fixing them: open-source maintainers say the flood of AI-generated reports adds to an already heavy load.

Bio defence is earlier and much smaller. A June 2026 post introducing the Frontier Biodefense Fellowship cites an estimate that around 160 people work full time on catastrophic biological risk, and perhaps fewer than 30 on defence in depth. Its authors expect a "window of vulnerability" of roughly 3 to 15 years, in which the ability to cause harm arrives before the ability to defend at scale. They also note this urgency could be overstated if AI helps defensive researchers first.

Key results

  • DARPA's AI Cyber Challenge offered a cumulative $29.5 million in prizes to teams building AI systems that secure open-source critical-infrastructure code. DARPA, 2023
  • In the AIxCC final, competitor systems found 54 planted vulnerabilities and patched 43 across 54 million lines of code, and discovered 18 real vulnerabilities; four of the seven systems were released open source. DARPA, 2025
  • Anthropic gave about a dozen launch partners and over 40 critical-software organisations early access to a frontier model for defensive work, with up to $100 million in credits and $4 million to open-source security groups. Anthropic, 2026
  • OpenAI shifted its cyber grant programme from a $1 million research fund to large-scale deployment for defenders, committing $10 million in API credits in February 2026. OpenAI, 2026
  • Biodefence is severely understaffed (about 160 people full time on catastrophic biological risk, perhaps fewer than 30 on defence in depth), which motivated a dedicated fellowship. Tobias H (Pivotal Research), 2026

Open problems

  • How under-resourced open-source maintainers can fix the volume of flaws that AI tools now find. source
  • Whether the AI-and-biology offence–defence balance really favours attackers, or whether defensive researchers gain first. source
  • How to scale pathogen-agnostic protection such as respirators and air treatment to whole populations in a crisis. source
  • How to set common international standards for screening DNA synthesis orders. source

Criticisms

  • AI flaw-finding without matching fixing capacity shifts the burden onto volunteer maintainers, and $4 million in donations is small next to the problem. Steven J. Vaughan-Nichols (The Register), quoting cURL lead developer Daniel Stenberg
  • Routing open-source security through a proprietary model risks locking open-source projects into one company's tools. Steven J. Vaughan-Nichols (The Register)
  • The case for urgent biodefence may be overstated if frontier models help defensive researchers before they help bad actors. Pivotal Research, in its own fellowship rationale

Who works on it

  • Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA): Ran the AI Cyber Challenge to build AI systems that secure critical open-source software. AIxCC final competition results (2025)
  • Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H): Collaborated with DARPA on AIxCC, and with DARPA added $1.4 million in prizes to move its tools into real-world critical-infrastructure software, including health care. AIxCC final competition results (2025)
  • Anthropic (Project Glasswing): Gives defenders of critical software early access to a frontier model and funds open-source security groups. Project Glasswing (2026)
  • OpenAI (Cybersecurity Grant Program): Funds and gives model access to cyber defenders. OpenAI Cybersecurity Grant Program (2026)
  • Linux Foundation: Launch partner in Project Glasswing; part of Anthropic's open-source security donations goes through it. Project Glasswing (2026)
  • MATS: Research fellowship with Biosecurity and Systems Security tracks. MATS Program tracks (2026)
  • Pivotal Research: Runs the Frontier Biodefense Fellowship on protective equipment, biohardening and detection. Biodefense Fellowship (2026)
  • Blueprint Biosecurity: Non-profit advancing pathogen-agnostic countermeasures such as respirator stockpiles and ultraviolet air treatment. Blueprint Biosecurity programs (2026)
  • International Biosecurity and Biosafety Initiative for Science (IBBIS): Builds free, open-source DNA synthesis screening tools and supports common international screening standards. IBBIS tools and standards (2026)
