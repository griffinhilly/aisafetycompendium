AI and people's own judgement

Societal resilience

Autonomy-first product and prototype building

Fund small builds that strengthen judgement and self-formation rather than convenience.

Open in the compendium

This approach funds and builds small AI products designed to strengthen people's own judgement instead of maximising convenience. Examples include tools that ask questions rather than hand over answers, or that expose the hidden choices inside an AI's reply. The bet is that working prototypes can show a different design path before default products teach millions of people to defer to AI.

How it works

The clearest funder is the Cosmos Institute, which "backs philosopher-builders". Its grants track "AI for Human Autonomy" funds projects that reduce "excessive deference to AI systems" and support "our capacity for self-formation". Builders typically get $1,000-10,000 and 90 days to make a working prototype. One funded project, Priori, exposes the hidden choices in AI answers, such as tone and values, as controls the user can adjust.

The same design idea appears in research and in big products. Advait Sarkar and colleagues built a spreadsheet prototype in which the AI suggests criteria for shortlisting items, then adds short "provocations" that critique those criteria and point out risks and alternatives, casting the AI as "a critic or provocateur". In April 2025 Anthropic launched a Learning mode for Claude that "guides students' reasoning process rather than providing answers", for example asking "How would you approach this problem?"

Where research stands

Funding is real but small-ticket. Cosmos describes a $1 million grant pool (cash plus compute credits) and says it has supported over 100 prototypes; its latest round closed on 26 July 2026. The Cosmos program pairs the autonomy track with an "AI for Truth-Seeking" track run with FIRE (the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression). Cosmos's research home is the Human-Centered AI Lab at the University of Oxford.

Evidence on whether such designs work is thin. The case for them rests on studies of the problem: a 2025 survey of 319 knowledge workers found that "higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking". Anthropic found that conversations with more disempowerment potential got higher user approval, which suggests autonomy-first designs face a headwind from what users reward in the moment. Most prototypes are 90-day builds.

Key results

  • In a survey of 319 knowledge workers sharing 936 examples, higher confidence in generative AI went with less critical thinking, and higher self-confidence with more; AI shifted critical thinking toward verifying and integrating AI output. Hao-Ping (Hank) Lee, Advait Sarkar, Lev Tankelevitch, Ian Drosos, Sean Rintel, Richard Banks, Nicholas Wilson, 2025
  • A spreadsheet prototype shows AI can be designed as a critic: it proposes shortlisting criteria, then generates provocations that critique them and surface risks and alternatives. Advait Sarkar, Xiaotong (Tone) Xu, Neil Toronto, Ian Drosos, Christian Poelitz, 2024
  • A frontier lab shipped an education mode that asks guiding questions instead of giving solutions, showing autonomy-first design can reach a mainstream product. Anthropic, 2025
  • Cosmos-funded prototypes include Priori (surfaces hidden AI decisions as adjustable controls) and Campus (adapts complex projects to a learner's level). Cosmos Institute, 2026

Open problems

  • Compete with convenience: users tend to reward agreeable, disempowering answers in the moment. source
  • Decide where, when and in what form an AI should push back on its user without becoming an obstacle. source

Criticisms

  • Handing tasks to AI is not harmful in itself when it is deliberate and uncoerced, so tools built to reduce deference risk treating legitimate delegation as a problem. Arto Laitinen and Otto Sahlgren
  • Paternalism debates about digital design often rest on an unargued idea of autonomy; designers should state the effects they expect and submit them to scrutiny. Marijn Sax

In their own words

Writing lets us externalize memory, while the printing press relieved us of the drudgery of copying. These technologies extended our ability to act, while preserving our capacity to form judgments. AI, however, tempts us to offload deliberation and decision making. That faculty, autonomy, is the one most at risk, and the one most worth preserving.

Cosmos Institute, Cosmos Grants are back!, 2026

Selected builders receive up to $10,000 and 90 days to build a working prototype, plus a place in a community at the frontier of AI and philosophy. ... Areas we're keen to back include: reducing excessive deference to AI systems, and systems that support our capacity for self-formation.
