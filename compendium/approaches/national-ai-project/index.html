National AI project

Run frontier AI development as a government-led effort, for security against espionage and safety management.

Open in the compendium

A national AI project would put frontier AI development under government leadership, in the way the Manhattan Project put the atomic bomb under the US government. Supporters argue that only a state can protect the work from foreign espionage and manage the safety risks of superintelligence without commercial pressure to race. Critics argue it would intensify an arms race with China and concentrate enormous power in a few hands.

How it works

The best-known version is Leopold Aschenbrenner's 2024 essay series Situational Awareness. Its chapter 'The Project' predicts that by around 2027-28 the US government will set up some form of government AGI project, perhaps a joint venture with the major cloud providers and AI labs, making frontier AI 'functionally a project of the national security state'. In his words, "the leading labs will ('voluntarily') merge". Research would move to secure facilities with vetted staff and government-guarded data centres, to stop the theft of model weights. Abroad, the US would form an alliance with other democracies and offer to share benefits with other states that agree not to build their own superintelligence.

Other versions are more modest. In November 2024 the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, a body that advises Congress, recommended a programme modelled on the Manhattan Project and dedicated to racing for and acquiring artificial general intelligence (AGI), funded through multiyear government contracts with AI, cloud and data-centre companies. In November 2025 the US launched the Genesis Mission (Executive Order 14363), a Department of Energy platform that uses national labs, supercomputers and federal datasets to train scientific AI models. It is government-led, but it covers AI for science, not general frontier models.

Where research stands

This is a proposal and a forecast, not a policy in place. No government has taken control of frontier AI development, and the leading US labs remain private companies. The literature is mostly strategic essays and scenario analyses rather than empirical work. Supporters stress security against espionage and the need for a public chain of command over a technology with military uses.

Most analysts who study it are wary. MIRI's Technical Governance Team (Peter Barnett and Aaron Scher, May 2025) treats a US National Project as one of four scenarios and judges it to carry an unacceptable risk of catastrophe, preferring an internationally coordinated halt. Corin Katzke and Gideon Futerman (December 2024) argue that the premises behind a race to superintelligence also make it self-defeating. Forethought's analysts (December 2024) find it very unclear whether one centralised Western project would be better than several, and tentatively judge centralisation bad because of the risk of power concentration. Dan Hendrycks, Eric Schmidt and Alexandr Wang (March 2025) warn that an aggressive bid for unilateral AI dominance would invite preventive sabotage by rivals.

Key results

  • Aschenbrenner predicts that by 2027-28 the US government will lead frontier AI through some form of government project, perhaps a joint venture with cloud providers and labs, justified by espionage threats and the safety challenges of superintelligence. Leopold Aschenbrenner, 2024
  • The US-China Economic and Security Review Commission recommended that Congress fund a Manhattan Project-like programme to race to AGI, using broad multiyear contracting authority. Computerworld, 2024
  • Barnett and Scher judge every scenario except an international 'Off Switch' and halt, including a US National Project racing for unilateral control, to carry an unacceptable risk of catastrophic harm. Peter Barnett, Aaron Scher, 2025
  • Katzke and Futerman argue a race to artificial superintelligence would raise the risks of great-power conflict, loss of control and erosion of liberal democracy, and that cooperation is the better strategy. Corin Katzke, Gideon Futerman, 2024
  • Executive Order 14363 (24 November 2025) created the Genesis Mission, a Department of Energy platform to train scientific AI models on federal data and supercomputers, framed as a national effort comparable to the Manhattan Project. The White House, Federal Register, 2025

Criticisms

  • The assumptions that motivate a race to superintelligence also make the race extremely dangerous, raising the risks of war, loss of control and the erosion of democracy. Corin Katzke and Gideon Futerman
  • A US National Project racing for unilateral control carries an unacceptable risk of catastrophic harm; only an international halt avoids it. Peter Barnett and Aaron Scher, MIRI Technical Governance Team
  • Centralising Western AGI development would probably be bad overall because it would increase risks from power concentration. Rose Hadshar and Tom Davidson, Forethought
  • Under 'Mutual Assured AI Malfunction', a state's aggressive bid for unilateral AI dominance would be met with preventive sabotage by rivals. Dan Hendrycks, Eric Schmidt, Alexandr Wang

Status

Essay proposal, also recommended by a US congressional advisory body. In November 2024 the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission recommended that Congress "establish and fund a Manhattan Project-like program dedicated to racing to and acquiring an Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) capability". Barnett and Scher call this scenario extremely dangerous and do not endorse it.

  • Should There Be Just One Western AGI Project?, Rose Hadshar, Tom Davidson (2024). A balanced weighing of centralised versus multiple projects.

  • AI Governance to Avoid Extinction, Peter Barnett, Aaron Scher (2025). Places the national project among alternative strategic scenarios.

In their own words

Perhaps via defense contracting or similar, a joint venture between the major cloud compute providers, AI labs, and the government is established, making it functionally a project of the national security state. Much like the AI labs "voluntarily" made commitments to the White House in 2023, Western labs might more-or-less "voluntarily" agree to merge in the national effort.

Leopold Aschenbrenner, Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead, part IV: The Project, 2024

We will need government involvement to have even a hope of defending against the all-out espionage threat we will face; the private AI efforts might as well be directly delivering superintelligence to the CCP. ... We will need the government to manage the severe safety challenges of superintelligence, to manage the fog of war of the intelligence explosion.

Leopold Aschenbrenner, Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead, part IV: The Project, 2024
