Global coordination

This group needs two or more countries to act together, or is about how rival countries treat each other over AI. It runs from rivalry to joint institutions: how rivals compete and avoid war, then shared facts and agreed limits, then the bodies that would verify, enforce and share benefits, ending with a coordinated halt. Much of it is still at the proposal stage; an international scientific panel is now in place [International scientific assessment panel (IPCC-style)], and parts of the democratic-lead position are US policy [Keeping a democratic lead in AI].

Why it matters

Supporters argue that risks crossing borders need an international referee [International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style)]. Rival governments are unlikely to accept limits on AI unless they can detect cheating [Agreement verification technology]. AI can speed up war beyond human reaction time, and a misread incident could start a conflict [Oversight and arms control of military AI; Crisis channels and incident notification between rival states].

What is in it

Rivalry: keeping a democratic lead [Keeping a democratic lead in AI] and mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM) [Mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM)]. Avoiding escalation: crisis channels between rival states [Crisis channels and incident notification between rival states] and oversight and arms control of military AI [Oversight and arms control of military AI]. Shared facts and agreed limits: an international scientific panel [International scientific assessment panel (IPCC-style)], international AI red lines [International AI red lines] and space resource governance [Space resource governance]. Making deals hold: agreement verification technology [Agreement verification technology], benefit-sharing for joining [Benefit-sharing for joining governance (Atoms for Peace model)] and jurisdictional certification [Jurisdictional certification]. Joint institutions: an international inspection agency [International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style)] and a joint safety lab [International joint safety lab (CERN-style)]. The strongest step: halting frontier AI development by agreement [Halting frontier AI development].

How it connects

Global coordination extends Holding AI companies to account across borders: chip export controls are the main lever of a democratic lead, and an inspection agency would oversee national licensing [Keeping a democratic lead in AI; Compute governance; International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style); Regulation, standards and licensing]. Verification uses the same secure hardware that protects model weights in Releasing and running [Agreement verification technology; Securing model weights and lab secrets]. Crisis channels draw on domestic incident reporting and link to Society's preparation for warning shots [Crisis channels and incident notification between rival states; AI incident reporting systems and databases; Preparing for warning shots]. A halt is the opposite strategy to safety-motivated frontier labs [Safety-motivated frontier labs; Halting frontier AI development]. The scientific panel draws on capability evaluations and shared forecasts [International scientific assessment panel (IPCC-style); Dangerous-capability evaluations; Forecasting]. Space resource governance targets a route to lock-in that Society and Deciding what AI should value also study [Space resource governance; Concentration-of-power and lock-in research; Avoiding premature lock-in (the long reflection)].

Debates

Race or restraint: supporters of a democratic lead say it protects against authoritarian AI, while critics call AGI racing a 'suicide race' [Keeping a democratic lead in AI]. Deterrence or agreement: critics say sabotage deterrence lacks clear, monitorable red lines and could start a war; its authors reply that most objections apply to any race [Mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM)]. Halt or not: critics say a pause would push work to less regulated labs and countries, and supporters concede the political will does not yet exist [Halting frontier AI development]. The nuclear analogy: unlike nuclear weapons, weaponising AI promises to be neither expensive nor hard to hide [International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style)]. AI also has no clear weapons-grade equivalent, which limits IAEA- and Atoms-for-Peace-style designs [International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style); Benefit-sharing for joining governance (Atoms for Peace model)]. Political will: the US government has rejected global governance of AI [International AI red lines; Oversight and arms control of military AI].

The 13 approaches

  • Keeping a democratic lead in AIGive advanced AI to the intelligence and defence communities of the US and its democratic allies; restrict chips.
  • Mutual sabotage deterrence (MAIM)Deter any state from seeking AI dominance through the threat that rivals would sabotage its AI project.
  • Crisis channels and incident notification between rival statesSet up channels between rival states, with standard notices and regular talks, so AI incidents do not escalate.
  • Oversight and arms control of military AIKeep humans in control of AI used in military command, control and decision support, through agreements and national policy.
  • International scientific assessment panel (IPCC-style)An independent expert panel that builds shared scientific evidence on AI risk for governments.
  • International AI red linesSpecific AI behaviours and uses that countries agree to ban everywhere, such as AI that copies itself without human approval.
  • Space resource governanceAgree rules now against exclusive grabs of offworld resources, before advanced AI makes them reachable.
  • Agreement verification technologyBuild the technology and protocols that let governments, or AI developers, verify that an AI agreement is being kept.
  • Benefit-sharing for joining governance (Atoms for Peace model)Offer states access to AI benefits in return for joining safety governance, as Atoms for Peace did for nuclear technology.
  • Jurisdictional certificationAn international body certifies whole national AI regulatory regimes. States then block imports of AI-built goods from countries that are not certified.
  • International regulatory and inspection agency (IAEA-style)An international body that sets AI standards and checks that states and developers follow them.
  • International joint safety lab (CERN-style)Countries pool people and computing in one shared institution to do frontier AI or AI safety research.
  • Halting frontier AI developmentPrepare the legal, technical and institutional means for an internationally coordinated halt to the most dangerous AI development.

Its band: Societal resilience

Societal resilience makes the world outside the AI hold up. It protects people's own judgement, gets countries to act together, and strengthens society's defences and the balance of power.
