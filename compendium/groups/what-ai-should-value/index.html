Deciding what AI should value

This group asks what an AI should aim at, obey or refuse, and who decides. 'Do what humans want' hides very different targets, from literal instructions to what is morally right [Defining the alignment target]. The work runs from philosophy about the target to the written specs and constitutions that labs now train their models on [Model specs and constitutions].

Open in the compendium

Why it matters

An AI that perfectly hits the wrong target can still cause serious harm [Defining the alignment target]. Labs already publish documents that say what their models should value, including where to set corrigibility [Model specs and constitutions; Setting AI corrigibility]. Anthropic's constitution also sets a hard limit on helping power grabs [Hard constraints against concentration of power]. A small group of developers sets these values today, so the choices raise questions of legitimacy as well as safety [Public input into model values].

What is in it

Choosing the target: defining the alignment target [Defining the alignment target], including animals [Including animals in alignment targets], picking principles by a fair process [Alignment as fair treatment of claims], combining ethical theories under moral uncertainty [Moral uncertainty procedures], making AI better at philosophy [AI philosophical competence (metaphilosophy)] and avoiding premature lock-in [Avoiding premature lock-in (the long reflection)]. Borrowing from law: alignment as jurisprudence [Alignment as jurisprudence], rights as trumps [Rights as trumps] and law-following AI [Law-following AI]. The written document: model specs and constitutions [Model specs and constitutions], setting AI corrigibility [Setting AI corrigibility], hard constraints against concentration of power [Hard constraints against concentration of power] and a users' bill of rights [Users' bill of rights in model constitutions]. Who has a say, and what the data show: public input into model values [Public input into model values] and empirical values research [Empirical values research as input to normative debate].

How it connects

The target set here is what the rest of Making aligned models tries to build in: Constitutional AI turns a written spec into a training reward [Model specs and constitutions; Constitutional AI and rule-based rewards], and the idea of corrigibility that labs now set in their specs came from formal work in Theory [Setting AI corrigibility; Corrigibility as a formal target]. Evaluating checks the result, by testing models against their written spec and benchmarking the values they express [Model specs and constitutions; Model-character evaluation against a written spec; Empirical values research as input to normative debate; Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking]. The hard limit on power grabs links to Society's research on concentration of power [Hard constraints against concentration of power; Concentration-of-power and lock-in research]. Corrigibility connects to AI welfare, which asks what controlling an AI might cost if it can be wronged [Setting AI corrigibility; Otherness and control].

Debates

Narrow or broad target: one view keeps alignment to an AI trying to do what its operator wants and treats 'which values' as a separate question; another says preference-based targets are themselves inadequate [Defining the alignment target]. Obedience or independence: a fully obedient AI is only as safe as whoever controls it, a fully independent one only as safe as its values, and Anthropic says it may be approaching the question the wrong way [Setting AI corrigibility]. Legitimacy: critics say a company 'constitution' borrows the aura of public legitimacy without public accountability, since the company drafts, revises and interprets it [Model specs and constitutions; Hard constraints against concentration of power]; public input answers this in part, but the developer keeps the final say [Public input into model values]. Law as a target: supporters of legal alignment concede that law is at best a lower bound for safe and ethical AI [Law-following AI].

The 15 approaches

  • Defining the alignment targetSort the candidate things an AI could be aligned to — instructions, intentions, preferences, interests, values.
  • Including animals in alignment targetsMake animal interests something AI is aligned to weigh.
  • Alignment as fair treatment of claimsPick principles by a fair process that can be justified publicly to people who disagree.
  • Moral uncertainty proceduresCombine several ethical theories' verdicts into one decision rather than picking a theory.
  • AI philosophical competence (metaphilosophy)Make AI systems better at reasoning about hard philosophical questions, so they help rather than mislead on them.
  • Avoiding premature lock-in (the long reflection)Keep key choices reversible, and avoid irreversible steps until humanity has had time to work out what it wants.
  • Alignment as jurisprudenceTreat alignment like judging: principled decisions under constraints, applied to cases nobody foresaw.
  • Rights as trumpsRights override aim-maximising calculation; the side-constraint versus rights-as-goal debate.
  • Law-following AIGive AI agents actual legal duties, so they refuse illegal acts on a principal's behalf.
  • Model specs and constitutionsGive the model an explicit written statement of values and persona to train toward.
  • Setting AI corrigibilityWork out where to set the AI's disposition between obedience and autonomy, and why.
  • Hard constraints against concentration of powerForbid the model outright from helping any individual or group seize unprecedented and illegitimate degrees of absolute control.
  • Users' bill of rights in model constitutionsWork out a bill of rights for a model's users, such as notice, explanations and ways to contest harmful outputs. Write it into the model's governing document, backed by independent oversight.
  • Public input into model valuesRun deliberation and voting with members of the public to set the principles a model is trained to follow.
  • Empirical values research as input to normative debateStudy what values models actually express and what people actually want, to inform the "align to what" argument.

Its band: Making aligned models

Making aligned models builds the model so it wants and does the right thing. It covers deciding what the model should value, the theory of agents and goals, and the data and training that shape the model.
