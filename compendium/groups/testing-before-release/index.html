Testing before release

This group checks a model before release: what it can do, how it behaves, and whether it is safe enough to ship. It moves from capability tests to tests for misbehaviour and values, then asks whether the tests themselves can be trusted. Safety cases tie the results into one argument that a reviewer can check and challenge [Safety cases].

Open in the compendium

Why it matters

Test results decide whether a model ships, ships with extra safeguards, or is held back, and every major frontier developer now runs such tests [Dangerous-capability evaluations]. A test the model can see through or game gives false comfort [Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations]. A careless test can make a dangerous model look safe [Science of evaluations and capability elicitation].

What is in it

What it can do: dangerous-capability evaluations [Dangerous-capability evaluations], adversarial robustness and red-teaming [Adversarial robustness and red-teaming] and low-probability estimation of rare catastrophic outputs [Low-probability estimation]. How it misbehaves: model organisms of misalignment [Model organisms of misalignment], multi-agent evaluations and collusion detection [Multi-agent evaluations and collusion detection] and real-world trials of AI agents [Real-world trials of AI agents]. What it values: model-character evaluation against a written spec [Model-character evaluation against a written spec], multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking [Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarking] and autonomy-preserving benchmarks [Autonomy-preserving benchmarks]. Whether the tests can be trusted: sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations [Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations] and the science of evaluations [Science of evaluations and capability elicitation]. The release argument: safety cases [Safety cases].

How it connects

Testing checks the work of Making aligned models: spec evaluations test models against their constitutions, and red-teaming tests training-time defences [Model-character evaluation against a written spec; Model specs and constitutions; Adversarial robustness and red-teaming; Circuit breakers and representation rerouting]. It shares Evaluating with Looking inside: reasoning traces reveal test awareness, and model organisms are where interpretability tools are tried [Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations; Chain-of-thought faithfulness and monitoring; Model organisms of misalignment; Mechanistic interpretability]. Results flow to Holding AI companies to account: outside auditors run these tests, system cards publish them, and regulators could require safety cases [Dangerous-capability evaluations; Independent third-party evaluation and audit; Model cards, system cards and transparency reporting; Safety cases; Regulation, standards and licensing]. Autonomy benchmarks test a design goal from AI and people's own judgement [Autonomy-preserving benchmarks; Human empowerment as an explicit design goal].

Debates

Model or context: some argue misuse depends on the context of use, so model-level tests cannot settle it and defences belong outside the model [Dangerous-capability evaluations]. Access: testers who see only outputs, or work under non-disclosure, cannot give formal oversight [Dangerous-capability evaluations]. Lab or wild: trained sandbaggers and planted failures may not resemble failures that arise naturally [Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluations; Model organisms of misalignment]. Limits of testing: some argue evaluations cannot bound capabilities from above, and that many safety benchmarks track general capability [Science of evaluations and capability elicitation]. Who writes the case: a developer writing its own safety case faces confirmation bias and conflicted incentives [Safety cases].

The 12 approaches

  • Dangerous-capability evaluationsTest models for bio, cyber, persuasion, autonomy and scheming capabilities before release.
  • Adversarial robustness and red-teamingAttack the system deliberately to find and close jailbreaks.
  • Low-probability estimationEstimate how likely a model is to produce a rare catastrophic output, without having to find an input that triggers it.
  • Model organisms of misalignmentBuild deliberately misaligned models in the lab to study how deception arises and test whether safety methods catch it.
  • Multi-agent evaluations and collusion detectionTest groups of interacting AI agents for collusion, hidden signalling and miscoordination, and build monitors that catch it.
  • Real-world trials of AI agentsRun AI agents with real money, tools and people in open-ended settings, and record how they behave and fail.
  • Model-character evaluation against a written specMeasure how closely a model's behaviour matches its developer's own specification.
  • Multi-axis value and virtue benchmarkingScore models on many value dimensions at once and compare their profiles.
  • Autonomy-preserving benchmarksDirectly test whether a model respects the user's agency rather than fostering dependency.
  • Sandbagging and situational-awareness evaluationsTest whether a model hides capability or behaves differently when it knows it is being tested.
  • Science of evaluations and capability elicitationMake AI tests trustworthy: draw out a model's full ability, measure the right thing, and report statistical uncertainty.
  • Safety casesA structured, auditable argument that a system is acceptably safe, borrowed from nuclear and aviation.
