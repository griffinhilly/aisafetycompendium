AI welfare

This group asks whether an AI itself could be wronged, and what would be owed to it. It moves from the question of moral status, through research and public attitudes, to steps one lab already takes, and then to deals, legal status and rules for creating and copying digital minds.

Open in the compendium

Why it matters

Some safety practices, such as constraint, monitoring and retraining, would raise ethical questions if the systems turned out to be moral patients [Model welfare research]. Models that treat retirement as final may act to avoid it, so some welfare steps may serve safety too [Model deprecation and weight preservation commitments]. Public beliefs about AI minds will shape regulation, and how societies respond if AI systems claim rights [Moral-circle expansion to AI].

What is in it

The question: grounds of moral status [Grounds of moral status], model welfare research [Model welfare research], moral-circle expansion to AI [Moral-circle expansion to AI] and otherness and control [Otherness and control]. Practice in labs: pre-deployment welfare assessment [Pre-deployment welfare assessment], low-cost welfare interventions such as letting a model end abusive chats [Low-cost model welfare interventions], and model deprecation and weight preservation commitments [Model deprecation and weight preservation commitments]. Deals and law: deals and negotiation with AI systems [Deals and negotiation with AI systems] and legal personhood and standing [Legal personhood and standing for AI]. Many minds: governing creation and copying of digital minds [Governing creation and copying of digital minds].

How it connects

Welfare work sits in tension with Constraining: it asks when AI control is justified and what control costs if AIs are moral patients [Otherness and control; Model welfare research; AI control]. It relies on Looking inside, since the limits of introspection limit what self-reports can show [Pre-deployment welfare assessment; Model introspection]. Assessments appear in system cards beside capability tests [Pre-deployment welfare assessment; Model cards, system cards and transparency reporting; Dangerous-capability evaluations]. Weight preservation links to Theory's work on shutdown and corrigibility [Model deprecation and weight preservation commitments; Corrigibility as a formal target]. Deals offer an alternative to control for early misaligned models, and model organisms of alignment faking inform them [Deals and negotiation with AI systems; AI control; Model organisms of misalignment]. Legal personhood ties to liability and to law-following AI [Legal personhood and standing for AI; Liability and tort mechanisms; Law-following AI].

Debates

Could AI matter morally: some argue consciousness may require being alive, and that AIs are owned artefacts, not persons [Grounds of moral status; Otherness and control]. Distraction: critics say machine moral status diverts attention from people harmed by AI, such as data workers [Model welfare research; Moral-circle expansion to AI]. Manufactured evidence: developers co-design the model and the welfare metrics, so evidence could be manufactured or suppressed, and welfare claims could be used to resist oversight [Model welfare research; Pre-deployment welfare assessment; Low-cost model welfare interventions]. Self-reports: what models say about themselves is shaped by training, so interviews alone cannot settle welfare questions [Pre-deployment welfare assessment]. Law moving the other way: several US states have barred AI personhood, and critics fear it would let developers dodge responsibility [Legal personhood and standing for AI].

The 10 approaches

  • Grounds of moral statusWork out what capacity earns a being moral consideration: sentience, cognitive sophistication or something else.
  • Model welfare researchInvestigate whether and when an AI system's welfare deserves moral consideration, and how developers should act while the answer is unknown.
  • Moral-circle expansion to AIStudy empirically who extends moral concern to AI and why.
  • Otherness and controlWork out how agents with different values should relate, seeing some AIs as fellow creatures, not mere tools.
  • Pre-deployment welfare assessmentRun a structured welfare check on a model before release.
  • Low-cost model welfare interventionsMake cheap, reversible changes that would help a model if it has welfare, such as letting it end abusive conversations.
  • Model deprecation and weight preservation commitmentsKeep retired models' weights and record their preferences before retirement, to reduce shutdown-avoidant behaviour and respect possible model interests.
  • Deals and negotiation with AI systemsMake agreements with AI systems so that cooperating pays them better than defecting.
  • Legal personhood and standing for AIGive some AI systems a legal status, such as the right to make contracts, hold property or sue.
  • Governing creation and copying of digital mindsMake rules on how many digital minds are created and copied, so numbers cannot swamp society or produce bad lives.

Its band: AI welfare
