Releasing and running it safely

This group controls who gets a model, and watches and limits what the model does once it is in use. It follows the model out of the lab: who may hold the weights, then filters and monitors on each use, then control of the whole deployment, then tracing agents and studying real use. AI control starts from the assumption that training may have failed [AI control].

Open in the compendium

Why it matters

Nobody can yet check reliably whether a model's goals are safe, but people can test whether safeguards hold against a deliberately adversarial model [AI control]. Once weights are public or stolen, no one can recall them or add safeguards, so the other safeguards in this group depend on access and security [Limiting proliferation of high-risk models; Securing model weights and lab secrets]. Pre-release tests only check for harms someone thought of; watching real use can reveal patterns nobody predicted [Studying real-world AI use at scale].

What is in it

Who gets the model: limiting proliferation of high-risk models [Limiting proliferation of high-risk models] and securing model weights and lab secrets [Securing model weights and lab secrets]. Each use: input and output classifiers [Input and output classifiers (guardrails)], behavioural and activation monitoring [Behavioural and activation monitoring] and anomaly detection [Anomaly detection]. The whole deployment: AI control [AI control]. In the world: agent identity and accountability [Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems)] and studying real-world AI use at scale [Studying real-world AI use at scale].

How it connects

Monitors draw on Looking inside: interpretability supplies probes, and chain-of-thought monitoring feeds control protocols [Behavioural and activation monitoring; Mechanistic interpretability; AI control; Chain-of-thought faithfulness and monitoring]. Testing before release stress-tests these safeguards through red-teaming and control evaluations [Input and output classifiers (guardrails); Adversarial robustness and red-teaming; AI control]. Guardrails complement training-time defences, which they cannot replace for open weights [Input and output classifiers (guardrails); Tamper-resistant safeguards for open-weight models]. Agent IDs support Holding AI companies to account, since liability and incident investigation need a responsible party [Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems); Liability and tort mechanisms; Independent AI incident investigation (NTSB-style)]. Usage studies feed AI and people's own judgement by measuring disempowerment in real use [Studying real-world AI use at scale; Gradual disempowerment]. Limiting proliferation is the opposing strategy to Society's case for spreading capability widely [Limiting proliferation of high-risk models; Spreading AI capability widely (diffusion)].

Debates

Open or closed: supporters of open weights cite innovation, competition and transparency, and say keeping systems closed leaves control with a few companies [Limiting proliferation of high-risk models]. How far control reaches: its own proponents expect it to fail for wildly superhuman AI, and one critic says quietly caught misbehaviour could hide a warning shot [AI control]. Defences under attack: adaptive attackers break most published filters, and activations can be shaped to look harmless [Input and output classifiers (guardrails); Behavioural and activation monitoring; Anomaly detection]. Security gaps: critics say labs are not on track to stop top state attackers, and that algorithmic secrets are barely protected [Securing model weights and lab secrets]. Privacy: agent IDs could become surveillance infrastructure, and the privacy filters in usage analysis have been attacked [Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems); Studying real-world AI use at scale].

The 8 approaches

  • Limiting proliferation of high-risk modelsRestrict distribution, for example through monitored application interfaces.
  • Securing model weights and lab secretsProtect frontier model weights and key algorithmic ideas from theft by insiders and nation-states.
  • Input and output classifiers (guardrails)Run separate classifier models on users' prompts and the model's responses, and block harmful ones in real time.
  • Behavioural and activation monitoringWatch outputs or internal activations in live deployment for hazard signals.
  • Anomaly detectionFlag statistically unusual behaviour as a proxy for an unknown hazard.
  • AI controlDesign deployment so a misaligned model still cannot cause harm.
  • Agent identity and accountability (IDs for AI systems)Give AI systems unique IDs so their actions can be traced to the system and the party responsible.
  • Studying real-world AI use at scaleRead anonymised real conversations in aggregate to see what the system is actually doing to people.

Its band: Constraining

Constraining limits what a model, or the companies that build it, can do. It controls who gets a model and watches it in use, and it checks, insures and sets rules for AI companies.
